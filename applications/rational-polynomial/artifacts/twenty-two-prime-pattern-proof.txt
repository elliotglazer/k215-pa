Twenty-two prime forms and conjugate reduction orders
3 November 2026

Scope and result

This note gives the precise prime-pattern input used by the saved
six-contact multiplication certificate. It is a written proof using
the established Green--Tao--Ziegler theorem, Dirichlet's theorem,
Mertens estimates and standard elliptic-curve reduction facts.
It does not use the new pointwise 2-converse, the height estimate,
or any assertion that the candidate polynomial is universal.

Let C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60}, B=C union {1}. Fix
 F=Q(sqrt(2)), d=75-53sqrt(2), E:y^2=x^3-d^2*x.
Let P0 be the product of primes at most 324 and W=4*P0.
For every unit residue r modulo W, take the first 22 primes greater
than 324 in that residue. Let Hcat be their union and set
 Kcat={d0*h: d0 is a positive divisor of P0^4, h in Hcat}.

For every integer A and every bound B0, there are integers u1,u2,v,
with v nonzero and u3=A*v-u1-u2, such that all 22 values
 v, uj-b*v (j=1,2,3; b in B)
are a sign times a member of Kcat times a positive prime greater
than B0. Each resulting prime p splits in F, both reductions of E
are good, and their orders d1,d2 satisfy
 gcd(d1,d2)=4, p does not divide d1*d2.

The prime values need not be distinct. No assertion is made about
common factors of orders associated with two different primes.

1. The forms and the fixed catalogue

Write T=(u1,u2,v). The coefficient vectors, besides (0,0,1), are
 (1,0,-b), (0,1,-b), (-1,-1,A-b), for b in B.
Each is primitive. Within a family, a nonzero two-by-two minor is
a difference of two contacts, whose absolute value is at most 61.
Between families, one of the first two-coordinate minors is 1 or -1.
The vector of v is independent of the others over every field.
Thus all pairs are independent modulo p whenever p>324, uniformly
in A. They are also pairwise nonproportional over Q.

Choose a bounded open box of positive volume whose closure avoids
all 22 zero planes. On this box each form has a fixed sign epsilon.
There is no restriction that the original forms all be positive.
After changing each sign they are positive and comparable to X
on the box dilated by X.

For each p<=324, a primitive form is surjective modulo p^5. The
union of the events p^5 dividing one of the forms has proportion
at most 22/p^5<1. Choose a vector residue outside this union and
lift it modulo p^8. Each form now has fixed valuation e_p in
{0,1,2,3,4}, with its remaining unit known at least modulo p^4.
Consequently, after removing d0=product p^e_p, the signed unit is
known modulo 8 at p=2 and modulo p at each odd p<=324.

The desired residue of a positive prime is c2=1 modulo 8,
c7=3 modulo 7, and cp=1 modulo p at other small primes.
For each form, the needed unit of h is its signed unit divided
by cp. CRT specifies a unit residue modulo W. Since there are
only 22 forms, the 22 catalogue primes in each residue suffice
to select h values distinct for all occurrences. Different
residue classes cannot have a common prime. Thus the prescribed
Kcat contains every required multiplier k=d0*h.

At the selected catalogue prime h for one form, impose that form
zero modulo h. On this plane all other forms are nonconstant.
Excluding both zero and minus their signed multiplier removes
at most 42*h points from the h^2 points of the plane. Since h>324,
some point remains. Lift modulo h^2 so that the divided value of
the distinguished form is neither zero nor -1 modulo h. Its
primitive coefficients let this divided value run through all
residues, and h>2 suffices.

Combining these residues gives a fixed progression modulo
 W0=product_(p<=324) p^8 * product_(selected h) h^2.
For T on this progression, each signed divided form
 Psi_nu(T)=epsilon_nu*F_nu(T)/k_nu
is integral, nonzero at every prime dividing W0, and not -1
at the odd primes dividing W0. Its variable coefficients after
substitution T=T0+W0*n are divisible by those primes.
All choices so far precede the next cutoff L.

2. Lower count, retaining the dependence on L

Take L larger than the primes dividing W0, all catalogue primes,
B0 and any further fixed exception primes. At every new prime
p<=L, exclude F_nu(T)=0 and epsilon_nu*F_nu(T)=-k_nu
for all nu. At most 44*p^2 of the p^3 residues are excluded.
All these primes exceed 324.

The allowed residue density modulo
 WL=W0*product_(p<=L, p not dividing W0) p
is at least c*(log L)^(-C0). The constants are independent of L:
take logarithms of product(1-44/p), bound the quadratic remainder
by a convergent sum, and use the ordinary Mertens estimate.

Apply Green--Tao--Ziegler to each allowed progression after
T=T0+WL*n, with L fixed. These are integer affine forms with
pairwise nonproportional linear parts, hence finite complexity.
There is a positive real box on which all values are comparable
to X, and the local factors have a positive product.

In detail, at p dividing WL, every variable coefficient vanishes
and every constant is nonzero, giving beta_p=(1-1/p)^(-22)>=1.
At p not dividing WL, at most 22/p of the residues lie on a zero
hyperplane. For p>324,
 log beta_p >= log(1-22/p)-22*log(1-1/p) >= -22^2/p^2.
Pairwise independence also gives beta_p=1+O(1/p^2).
Thus the product is bounded below by one positive constant
independent of L and of the chosen allowed progression.

The weighted prime count has a main term proportional to X^3
times the residue density. Proper prime powers contribute at most
O_(A,L)(X^(5/2)*(log X)^23), because a fixed form value has
O_(A,L)(X^2) preimages. They are negligible. Dividing the weight
bound O_A((log X)^22) gives at least
 c_A*(log L)^(-C0)*X^3/(log X)^22
prime patterns for sufficiently large X depending on A,L.
There is no claim of a uniform threshold for X as L varies.

3. Why the reduction orders have the required form

Every selected p is 1 modulo 8 and 3 modulo 7. It splits in F,
and (7/p)=-1. At a chosen F-prime the two reductions d,d^sigma
multiply to 7, so they have opposite square classes. The two
curves are opposite quadratic twists of y^2=x^3-x. Their traces
are therefore opposite.

For p=1 modulo 4 the automorphism (x,y) -> (-x,i*y) is defined
over F_p. The centralizer of this automorphism in the geometric
endomorphism algebra is Q(i): on the rational 3-adic Tate module
its minimal polynomial X^2+1 is irreducible, so its centralizer
has dimension two, and Q(i) already supplies that dimension.
Frobenius commutes with the automorphism and is integral. It is
a+bi in Z[i], with a^2+b^2=p. In particular a is nonzero.
The two orders are p+1+2a and p+1-2a, in some order.

Both are divisible by four, since all the two-torsion is rational.
Their sum is 2(p+1), with valuation at 2 exactly two. Thus their
greatest common divisor has 2-part exactly four. Hasse's bound
gives 0<di<2p for these large primes; di cannot equal p since
four divides di. This proves p does not divide di.

If an odd prime l divides both orders, then l divides p+1 and a.
It follows that b^2=-1 modulo l. The progression conditions have
already excluded l<=L.

4. Upper bound for the remaining bad prime values

For p comparable to X, count its possible Gaussian pairs (a,b)
in a square of side O_A(sqrt(X)). Overcounting such pairs only
increases the bound for bad p. For L<l<=X^(1/8), there are at
most two residues b0 with b0^2=-1 modulo l. Put
 a=l*U, b=b0+l*V.
The resulting U,V lie in a square with n comparable to sqrt(X)/l,
so n is at least a constant times X^(3/8).

Sieve by primes q=1 modulo 4, q not equal to l, up to Z=X^(1/100),
above any fixed threshold. The equation a^2+b^2=0 modulo q cuts
out two lines with 2q-1 points, giving density 2/q-1/q^2.
The substitution in U,V is an affine bijection modulo q.
The elementary Selberg upper sieve gives
 O(n^2/log X+n*Z^6+Z^8)=O(n^2/log X)
uniformly in l,b0 and the square's location. For completeness,
weights supported on squarefree e<=Z have absolute value at
most e; summing the CRT residue errors at lcm(e,f) gives
O(n*Z^6+Z^8). The sieve denominator is bounded below by
a positive multiple of log X by Mertens in the progression
1 modulo 4. Omitting one prime changes that bound by a fixed
factor. The error is absorbed because n is at least X^(3/8)
up to a fixed factor.

Summing O_A(X/(l^2*log X)) over l>L gives O_A(X/(L*log X)).
For l>X^(1/8), use a lattice count. Since a is nonzero, there
are O_A(sqrt(X)/l) possible a, without an additive constant;
there are O_A(sqrt(X)/l+1) possible b. Summing
O_A(X/l^2+sqrt(X)/l), even over all integers up to O_A(sqrt(X)),
gives O_A(X^(7/8)+sqrt(X)*log X).
Thus the number of bad prime values of any fixed form is at most
 O_A(X/(L*log X)+X^(7/8)+sqrt(X)*log X),
with constants independent of L.

5. A uniform count on each nonzero fiber

Fix F_nu(T)=epsilon_nu*k_nu*p with p comparable to X.
Primitivity gives integral coordinates on this plane, contained
in a square of side O_A(X). Every other homogeneous form restricts
to a nonconstant affine function. Its zero line is distinct from
the other 20 zero lines modulo any sufficiently large sieving
prime q. Indeed, equality of two such lines would imply
 F_j-lambda*F_k=mu*(F_nu-c),
where c=epsilon_nu*k_nu*p is nonzero modulo q. Constant terms
force mu=0, contradicting pairwise independence. Here q<=Z<p,
and primes dividing the fixed multipliers are excluded.

The forbidden density is 21/q+O(1/q^2). The same upper sieve
therefore gives O_A(X^2/(log X)^21) prime patterns on each
fiber, uniformly in p and independently of L. Congruence
conditions used in the lower count may be discarded for this
upper bound.

Multiplying this fiber bound by the bad-value count and summing
over 22 forms leaves a total loss of at most
 C_A*X^3/(L*(log X)^22)+o_A(X^3/(log X)^22).
Choose L so large that C_A/L is smaller than half the lower
coefficient c_A*(log L)^(-C0), then choose X sufficiently large.
A prime pattern survives, and all its prime values exceed B0.
This proves the stated result.

Consequence for the candidate

The precise catalogue already encoded in fixed-certificate-constants.js
is sufficient. The number of forms, the signs for negative A, and the
extra contact at 1 introduce no new conjectural prime-distribution input.
For t=uj/v, the six contact primes used by the multiplication certificate
are among the selected primes in this theorem. The required two local
reduction orders at each such prime therefore exist.

This closes this particular source-level lemma using the named published
inputs. It does not close the height or pole-completeness lemmas, and
must not be used to mark the universal equation complete.

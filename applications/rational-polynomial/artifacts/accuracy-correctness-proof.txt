Accuracy polynomial: general correctness and artifact binding
3 November 2026

Statement and scope

Put F=Q(sqrt(2)), A=-11243+7950sqrt(2), and
 E: y^2=x^3+A*x,
 P=((1641-1160sqrt(2))/8,(-123720+87483sqrt(2))/32).
For a nonzero multiple Q of P put z(Q)=-x(Q)/y(Q), and put z(O)=0.
For h>=1 and j>=0 define rho(h,j)=Tr_F/Q(z(jP)/z(hP))/2.

The polynomial Aacc in accuracy-integer-polynomial.json, SHA256
6d1a4f92baa7f4931fa053875639d1f5c80a18498e5342fc1ac43b06bd6f52c6,
has the following property for k,h,j in N, with 0 included in N:

 exists w in N^1305804: Aacc(k,h,j,w)=0
 iff h>=1, j=k*h, and |rho(h,j)-k|<1/8.

The right side is false when h=0, without evaluating rho. This is a
natural-witness statement. It does not assert that the same polynomial
with arbitrary rational witnesses has the stated property.

This proof uses the natural halting-polynomial theorem proved in
integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt from the cited AFP results.
It supplies the additional algorithm, lowering and specialization
arguments for the accuracy polynomial. It uses no pointwise 2-converse
or multiplication-certificate theorem. The source/artifact comparison
is in accuracy-binding-checks.json. It is a written proof with finite
artifact checks, not a proof-assistant verification of the JavaScript.

1. The point and the nonvanishing needed by the algorithm

Write d=75-53sqrt(2); then A=-d^2 and d is nonzero. The projective
coordinates used in accuracy-program.js are
 X=13128-9280sqrt(2), Y=-1979520+1399728sqrt(2), Z=8.
They satisfy Y^2=X^3+A*X*Z^4 and give P under x=X/Z^2, y=Y/Z^3.
These integer coefficient identities are checked directly by
verify-accuracy-binding.js.

The primes (17,sqrt(2)-6) and (23,sqrt(2)-5) are primes of F with
residue fields F_17 and F_23. At them d reduces to 12 and 17,
respectively. Since the discriminant is 64*d^6, both are primes of
good reduction. Direct point counts, including O, are 20 and 24.
The verifier checks both counts by enumerating the residue fields.

Prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion injects into a good reduction.
Every torsion prime except 17 and 23 therefore divides gcd(20,24)=4;
17-primary torsion is excluded by reduction at 23, and 23-primary
torsion by reduction at 17. The entire torsion subgroup consequently
injects at both primes and has order dividing 4. The four points
O,(0,0),(d,0),(-d,0) already supply a subgroup of order 4. Thus the
torsion subgroup is exactly E[2]. Since P has nonzero y-coordinate,
P has infinite order.

It follows that, for every n>=1, nP is finite and not a 2-torsion
point. Its y-coordinate is nonzero. Its x-coordinate is also nonzero,
because x=0 on E implies y=0. Hence z(nP) is defined and nonzero.
For positive a,b, equal x-coordinates of aP,bP imply aP=+/-bP, so
a=b or a=-b. These elementary facts justify every denominator and
exceptional-case exclusion below. No generator or rank calculation
is required for this particular proof.

2. Natural, signed and quadratic-field arithmetic

The IR has natural registers with exact copy, addition, multiplication,
truncated subtraction, division by 2, comparisons and structured loops.
Represent an integer t by a canonical pair (p,n) of naturals with
t=p-n and p*n=0. Integer constants in IR.signed have this property.
The operations in accuracy-program.js preserve it:

 sadd((p,n),(q,m)) = ((p+q) dotminus (n+m),
                      (n+m) dotminus (p+q));
 sneg(p,n)=(n,p);
 smul((p,n),(q,m))=(p*q+n*m,p*m+n*q);
 ssquare(p,n)=((p+n)^2,0).

Here dotminus is truncated subtraction. In smul, canonical inputs
ensure that at most one output component is nonzero. Thus ssquare,
which would not be correct for arbitrary redundant signed pairs,
is correct at every use in this program. Scaling is by nonnegative
integer constants and preserves the same invariant.

A pair of these integer representations stores a+b*sqrt(2). Field
addition is componentwise; multiplication is
(a,b)*(c,d)=(ac+2bd,ad+bc). The emitted fsquare is the specialization
(a^2+2b^2,2ab). All of these are exact operations in Z[sqrt(2)].
No real-number approximation occurs in the acceptance decision.

3. Pair decoding, guards and termination at the IR level

Define pair(x,y)=(x+y)(x+y+1)/2+y. In unpair(a), the loop begins
with r=a,w=0 and maintains a=w(w+1)/2+r. Its guard is w<r.
Under that guard, r>=w+1, so the update r:=r-(w+1), w:=w+1
uses ordinary subtraction, not a truncated approximation. It preserves
the invariant and strictly decreases r. At termination 0<=r<=w,
and the returned pair (w-r,r) is the unique nonnegative pair mapped
to a. This also proves termination for every natural a.

Two calls decode pair(k,pair(h,j)). The second call copies its input
before modifying the remainder. The program rejects h=0, then rejects
j!=k*h by testing (j dotminus kh)+(kh dotminus j). If j=0 after
these guards, h>=1 implies k=0, and rho(h,0)=0, so acceptance is
correct. Otherwise both scalar multiplications are called with a
positive integer, as required in the next section.

All arithmetic operations are total on natural registers. Each
unpair loop terminates as just shown; each scalar loop halves a
positive count. There are no other unbounded loops at the IR level.
Every natural input therefore reaches accept or reject.

4. Jacobian formulas and the scalar-loop invariant

Coordinates are Jacobian: (X,Y,Z) means (X/Z^2,Y/Z^3). The formulas
can be verified directly from the affine chord and tangent law; the
same coordinate convention and formulas are documented in the EFD:
https://www.hyperelliptic.org/EFD/g1p/auto-shortw-jacobian.html

For doubling, put D=4XY^2, L=3X^2+A*Z^4 and
 X'=L^2-2D, Y'=L(D-X')-8Y^4, Z'=2YZ.
When YZ!=0 the affine tangent slope is L/Z'. The identities
D/Z'^2=X/Z^2 and 8Y^4/Z'^3=Y/Z^3 give exactly
 x'=slope^2-2x, y'=slope*(x-x')-y.
Thus IR.double is correct whenever the doubled point is finite and
not 2-torsion; its output has Z'!=0.

For addition put
 U1=X1*Z2^2, U2=X2*Z1^2,
 S1=Y1*Z2^3, S2=Y2*Z1^3, H=U2-U1, R=S2-S1,
 X'=R^2-H^3-2U1*H^2,
 Y'=R*(U1*H^2-X')-S1*H^3, Z'=H*Z1*Z2.
When both Z coordinates and H are nonzero, the affine slope is R/Z'.
Since H^3+2U1*H^2=H^2(U1+U2), the displayed X' gives
x'=slope^2-x1-x2. Dividing the Y' identity by Z'^3 gives
y'=slope*(x1-x')-y1. Thus IR.pointAdd is correct for distinct
affine x-coordinates, and its output has Z'!=0.

At the start of scalar-loop iteration i, base=[2^i]P, count=floor(n/2^i),
and the bits already processed sum to r=n mod 2^i. If the flag is
set, acc=[r]P with 0<r<2^i; if it is unset, r=0 and acc is not read.
For an odd count, an unset flag causes a copy of base. A set flag
causes pointAdd(acc,base). Their x-coordinates are distinct: equality
would imply r=+/-2^i by the infinite order of P, contrary to 0<r<2^i.
Thus this call never reaches a missing inverse or doubling case.

After halving count, a further iteration doubles base. The base point
is a nonzero multiple of P, so its Y is nonzero by section 1. This
proves the doubling precondition. The invariant follows at iteration
i+1. Since n>0, at least one bit is set, and the returned acc is nP.
All temporary arithmetic outputs are fresh registers. A pointCopy
mutates base or acc only after all new coordinates have been computed,
so the algebraic formulas are not affected by an in-place overwrite.

5. Exact acceptance comparison

Let H=hP and J=jP, with their computed Jacobian coordinates, and put
 Bnum=X_J*Z_J*Y_H=a+b*sqrt(2),
 Bden=X_H*Z_H*Y_J=c+d*sqrt(2).
The signs in z=-x/y cancel, so Bnum/Bden=z(J)/z(H).
Section 1 and the scalar invariant prove Bden!=0. Consequently
D=c^2-2d^2 is a nonzero integer, and
 rho(h,j)=(ac-2bd)/(c^2-2d^2)=N/D.
The program computes exactly N,D and accepts precisely when
 64*(N-kD)^2<D^2.
For either sign of D this is equivalent to |rho(h,j)-k|<1/8.
The signed-pair invariant in section 2 applies to each square here.
Together with the early guards, this proves the IR's stated predicate
for every natural input, not just the finite test cases.

6. Lowering the IR to the primitive register program

The Assembler uses increment, decrement-with-zero-branch and halt.
Its macro proof is recorded in integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt.
Here are the additional aliasing and termination checks for lowerIR.

IR register 0 maps to primitive register 0. All other IR registers
receive distinct fresh primitive registers, disjoint from the
Assembler's zero register and from all subsequently allocated scratch
registers. Every binary IR operation has a fresh output. Explicit
assignment is implemented by the alias-safe copy macro. Constants
are collected during construction and initialized before the body.

setBig clears its output and follows the binary digits, doubling and
optionally incrementing; the alias-safe addInto(out,out) implements
doubling. Zero is handled by the single digit 0. Addition copies the
first operand to the fresh output and adds the preserved second.
Multiplication copies its first operand into a private count, repeatedly
adds the preserved second to a cleared accumulator, and copies the
result out. Subtraction copies its second operand into a private count
before copying the first operand to the output, then applies that many
saturating decrements. These loops are finite for all natural inputs.

divmod2 copies its input before clearing outputs; its two outputs are
fresh and distinct. It removes pairs until zero or one remains and
returns floor(x/2) and x mod 2. ifNZ restores the tested register
before its selected branch. ifLt compares private copies and preserves
both operands. whileNZ repeats the preserving zero test; whileLT
repeats the preserving comparison at its loop label. Structural
induction on an IR block now gives exactly its intended control flow.

accept jumps to a finite cleanup that clears every primitive register
and then halts. reject jumps to a decrement instruction whose zero
and nonzero successors are itself; hence it never halts. There is no
other halt in the generated program. All arithmetic macro invocations
terminate, so the primitive program halts exactly on the IR's accepting
inputs, and it halts with all registers zero as required by H.

7. Specialization and its exact binding to the saved polynomial

Let eAcc be the integer in accuracy-program-code.hex. The verifier
freshly constructs the IR, lowers it, and checks exact equality with
accuracy-program-ir.json and accuracy-register-program.json. It then
encodes the entire fresh program and compares its code with eAcc.
Thus section 6 concerns the actual specialized program, not merely a
similar algorithm. The hashes of all six relevant artifacts are in
accuracy-binding-checks.json.

By the natural halting theorem, H(eAcc,u,v)=0 has natural witnesses
iff that program halts on u. The accuracy polynomial uses variables
k,h,j,q,u,v and is exactly

 H(eAcc,u,v)^2
 +(2q-(h+j)(h+j+1)-2j)^2
 +(2u-(k+q)(k+q+1)-2q)^2.

A sum of integer squares is zero iff each is zero. The second and
third terms therefore force q=pair(h,j) and u=pair(k,pair(h,j));
conversely those natural choices exist for every k,h,j. Applying
sections 3-6 and the theorem for H proves the statement at the top.

verify-accuracy-binding.js checks every one of H's 293568 parent nodes
after specializing e and shifting variable indices. It checks the
unchanged power templates and every private witness-block shift. It
then expands the small outer expression symbolically, treating H's
root as an independent symbol, and verifies the displayed sum of
squares. The variable audit gives 1305807 variables, of which 3 are
inputs and 1305804 are natural witnesses. This adds precisely q,u to
the 1305802 witnesses of H; the program parameter e is specialized.

Finite execution tests in accuracy-program-checks.json supplement
the proof. They are not its induction step and do not justify replacing
natural witnesses by rational witnesses. That replacement still needs
the exact elliptic integer model and its separate translation proof.

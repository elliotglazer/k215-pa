The restricted converse and completeness of the actual pole predicate
3 November 2026

Statements and scope

For a positive squarefree integer l with l=11 modulo 12, put

  E_l: y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l).

The restricted converse used here is

  corank_Z2 Sel_(2^infinity)(E_l/Q)=1
      implies ord_(s=1) L(E_l,s)=1.                       (RC)

The forward Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin theorem then gives rank one and
finite Sha. In particular, if dim_F2 Sel_2(E_l/Q)=3, the argument below
gives rank one and Sha(E_l/Q)[2]=0.

The pole-completeness conclusion concerns the exact eleven-witness
polynomial of explicit-pole-predicate.txt. If B is rational with square
reduced denominator and

  0<B<1/2,       v_p(B)>=4 for p in {2,3,5,7,13},

then that polynomial has a rational zero over B. Call this assertion PC.

This file combines the component mathematical proofs and checks their
interfaces. It uses the ordinary split-witness branch only for the
displayed l. It does not claim to prove every case of the general
pointwise 2-converse preprint, and it is not a Lean formalization.

There is a scope correction to older notes: the elementary split-witness
contradiction using (l/p)=-1 requires l to be a nonsquare. It would not
apply to l=1. Squarefree l=11 modulo 12 guarantees l>1 and the required
nonsquareness. Both twists constructed for PC satisfy this restriction.

1. Component results and their order

All references to the OpenAI source in the component proofs use commit
4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae. The following are the component
results used here, in dependency order.

(a) Nonvanishing with local conditions. For a fixed elliptic curve and
any nonempty fixed local filter of fundamental discriminants, the
appropriate central value or derivative is nonzero at arbitrarily large
indices. In the positive-sign case there is an absolute bound A_0 on
the number of prime factors of such an index, and all its primes can
avoid a prescribed finite set. The bound on size can depend on the
curve and filter; A_0 cannot. The progression moment, nonzero-frequency
estimate and lower linear sieve proving this are in nonvanishing-audit.txt.

(b) Even lower bound and even transfer. For a fixed non-CM E and its
allowed twist parameters h, write w(h)=omega(h), and, where finite,
s(h)=length_Z2 Sha(E^(h)/Q)[2^infinity]. At nonzero central values,

  v_2(L_*(h)) >= 2w(h)+s(h)-C.                            (1)

For a binary family with a corank-zero base, a uniform upper bound on
v_2(L_*)-2w-s at its nonzero vertices implies nonvanishing and the same
kind of upper bound at its base. These statements are proved in
cyclotomic-noncm-audit.txt. The construction uses one fixed-form Kato
class before character evaluation, integral smoothing and Euler-factor
corrections, and Fourier intersection. It does not use a pointwise
rank-one converse. Kato's rational theorem at coefficient prime two,
including the local H^2 correction, was checked separately in
restricted-converse-primary-input-checks.txt.

(c) Coefficients and symbols. The even coefficient system detects L_*(h)
and its exact nonzero coefficient ratios satisfy

  2v_2(a(D)/a(D_0))=v_2(L_*(sigma D)/L_*(sigma D_0)).      (2)

The weighted odd coefficient systems detect the genus Heegner points
P_h to arbitrarily high finite precision. At each stage their vanishing
modulo that precision includes all parameters with zero derivative.
Their constants are independent of the moving detecting prime.
These are the constructions in coefficient-trace-audit.txt and
weighted-theta-audit.txt. The even comparison uses the nonzero Shimura
eigenspace and the same-local-class Waldspurger comparison. The (H1)
condition is supplied by that nonzero eigenspace; an (H2) condition
absent from this comparison is not silently assumed.

(d) Ring-class lower bound and height ratio. If h is already known to
have analytic rank one and hk has nonzero central value, with K=Q(sqrt(k))
an allowed imaginary companion and omega(k)<=A, then

  2j_h(k) >= 4w(h)+s(h)+s(hk)-C(E,A).                     (3)

The corresponding unnormalized Heegner-height comparison is

  2j_h(k)-2j(P_h)=v_2(L_*(hk))+O_(E,A)(1).                (4)

The finite classes, exact local conditions, limits, switches and central
comparison proving (3) are in finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt,
limit-interface-audit.txt, determinant-switch-audit.txt and
central-specialization-audit.txt. The actual paired functional and both
ranges of uniformity are checked in paired-functional-and-uniformity-proof.txt.
Equation (4), including the conductor factor in the unaveraged sums,
is derived from Cai--Shu--Tian in restricted-pointwise-assembly.txt,
section 5. These arguments apply at already known simple zeros; they
do not presume a nonzero point at the base of (RC).

(e) Graph realization and addressing. Once a coefficient system has
its arithmetic lower bound, its least normalized depth defines a binary
symbol. The trace calculation gives product-label contraction, omission
of a whole rational prime, and isolation of a primary vertex. From these
identities, arithmetic-graph-audit.txt proves the finite address theorem.
Two separately nonzero terminal polynomials can be made simultaneously
one at every nonzero binary address by one finite graph of primary
auxiliaries. All those auxiliaries are absent at zero. The graph is
realizable by distinct primes with the prescribed support and splitting.

In the present full-two-torsion branch all vertices are split vertices
and all weights are one. The general simple-vertex and donor branches
are not used. No claim about simple vertices is needed for (RC) or PC.

2. Checking the coefficient-to-graph interface

The squarefree coefficient lower bounds give an attained least depth in
a discrete translate of Z. For the odd finite-precision systems, use
one fixed ultrafilter and a bounded-weight sequence attaining the least
depth. For every fixed weight bound, divisibility at that depth holds
on a large set of stages. For an all-coefficient statement at a selected
precision, large squarefree weights are handled by the coarser arithmetic
lower bound, and the remaining bounded weights by this minimum. Thus
normalization gives integral coefficient operators at one sufficiently
late stage for any fixed finite graph problem.

Unary subtraction divides by the integer 2. The square-shell recurrence
proves this divisibility coefficient by coefficient, before reduction
modulo the maximal ideal of the character coefficient ring. Good Hecke
operators and diamond operators preserve the resulting integral lattice;
the diamond corrections are undivided unary terms. After a fresh trace
those corrections vanish. The reduced trace identities are therefore

  d(u)t(v)=t(v),
  t(u)t(v)=t(uv)+t(uv^{-1}),       t(1)=0.                (5)

The conductor-one local inertia calculation, using the same Frobenius
lift on both sides, gives

  U_p(t(g tau_p phi_p)-t(g phi_p))
       =d(phi_p)(t(g tau_p)-t(g)).                        (6)

At an omitted prime p the lower-source coefficient system was formed
without p, with the same original character and minimum. It is unramified
at the entire rational prime. Its two product Frobenius tests therefore
agree, and (6) leaves exactly their common contraction symbol. This
checks the whole-prime omission required in the forest argument.

In that argument every cyclic set of edge differences vanishes; each
forest contracts to its product labels. The grounded Laplacian minors
give the constant and pair coefficients of an auxiliary network. The
singular network has zero constant at its target address and first term
equal to the desired edge derivative. The two nonsingular networks have
constant one; their first-degree difference is that derivative only at
the target. The ordinary polynomial product of the two terminal
polynomials has a nonzero top monomial, with exponents at most two.
Assign singular networks to exponent-two variables and the paired
nonsingular networks to exponent-one variables. Summing over the binary
network choices kills all constant choices, and the top-degree budget
leaves coefficient one. Some one network choice makes both symbols one
at all nonzero addresses. No common evaluation of the original two
terminal polynomials is assumed.

These checks identify the precise symbol rules consumed by the graph
proof. Neither the normalization nor the graph step creates its own
arithmetic lower bound.

3. Obtaining the odd lower bound without circularity

At a least positive-size unit seed, changing an edge contracts to a
smaller nonempty configuration and hence gives zero. Thus its value
depends only on the individual labels. A sufficient supply of those
labels, with the same total label, contracts to that seed by retaining
all but one of its vertices and merging the rest. Duplicated full
labels give packets of total zero, with arbitrary reserves.

Use this fact and the one-symbol address theorem with the even symbols.
For a partitionable parameter h of corank zero, every nonzero address
has an even unit and hence
v_2(L_*)<=2w+C. Apply the even transfer in (b) to obtain

  L_*(h)!=0,       v_2(L_*(h))<=2w(h)+s(h)+C'.             (7)

Now fix an h already known to have analytic rank one. For each possible
total at the finite support prepare the appropriate even negative-filter
seed. There are finitely many. Adjoin a bounded dummy supply of their
labels. Absorb h and this dummy supply into the curve before applying
the bounded-factor nonvanishing result (a), with a finishing progression
that makes the final k' negative, fundamental, coprime to 2Nh and locally
square at 2Nh. The final twist has even sign and nonzero central value.
The dummy labels make hk' partitionable. The number of dummy and finishing
primes is bounded independently of h. Thus omega(k')<=A for a fixed A,
and (7) applies to hk'.

Subtract (4) from (3) and use (7) and w(hk')=w(h)+w(k') to get

  2j(P_h) >= 2w(h)+s(h)-C_o.                              (8)

The constant in (8) is independent of h. This establishes the arithmetic
lower bound needed for the odd minimum in (c). The steps preceding (8)
used only even normalized symbols and independently known simple-zero
parameters, not an odd normalized symbol.

4. A fixed companion and a nonempty split witness

Since j(E_l)=35152/9 is not an algebraic integer, E_l and all its twists
are non-CM. Apply the preceding construction to E=E_l. Parity turns the
corank-one hypothesis in (RC) into odd functional sign. Normalize the
odd coefficient system using (8). If its empty configuration is a unit,
an actual late stage already detects the base derivative and proves (RC).
Otherwise retain a nonempty minimum odd seed.

Choose a separate negative even unit k, with a fresh ramified prime
outside the old support, and fix K=Q(sqrt(k)). Its central value is
nonzero. This choice precedes every later binary dimension.

The ordinary even coefficient system for E_l^(k), normalized at 1, has
v_2(a(D))>=omega(D). If no nonempty K-split parameter attains equality,
discreteness improves this to omega(D)+1 at every such parameter. The
integral trace calculation in ordinary-split-witness-audit.txt then
forces

  a_p(E_l^(k))=p+1 modulo 8                               (9)

at every good K-split prime outside the support. In deriving (9), a
fresh prime p' matches the support data of p, so that pp', rather than
p alone, belongs to the square filter. Thus (9) does not require p to
be individually trivial on all support characters.

Because l>1 is squarefree, Q(i,sqrt(l)) is biquadratic. The fresh
ramification of K makes it independent of that field. Chebotarev
therefore supplies good primes p with

  p=1 modulo 4,        (l/p)=-1,        (k/p)=1.

For the three roots 0,l,-3l, respectively the differences -l,l,-4l
are nonsquares. No nonzero rational two-torsion point is twice a point
over F_p. Therefore E_l(F_p)[2^infinity]=(Z/2Z)^2 and
#E_l(F_p)=4 modulo 8. As k is a square at p, this contradicts (9).
The ordinary even system has a nonempty K-split unit seed.

This step is why the nonsquareness of l was recorded explicitly. It
does not establish the same ordinary-seed claim at l=1.

5. Two tests and the missing vertex

Enrich the finite rational label alphabet to contain the fixed K
character and the support of the even system. Every old odd label has
a K-split lift, because K has fresh ramification outside its intrinsic
label extension. Every even seed label already occurs at a K-split
prime. Take the union of sufficient compatible lifts for the two seeds
separately, then duplicate every full label. No arbitrary pair of
projected labels is asserted to have a simultaneous lift.

Each packet has total zero and sufficient reserves. Under each of the
two projections, a positive union of packets contracts to that test's
nonempty minimum seed. Hence the two terminal polynomials are separately
nonzero. Apply the two-symbol address theorem of section 2. For each
chosen binary dimension it supplies a finite common network on which
both tests are units at every nonzero address. All active primes split
in K, and the zero address has h=1.

At each nonzero address, the odd and even detections give analytic
orders one for E_l^(h) and zero for E_l^(hk). Their unit bounds and (4)
give

  2j_h(k)<=4w(h)+C.

The nonnegative finite Sha lengths may be subtracted, so the corrected
ring-class determinant has a uniform upper valuation bound there.
At zero, the companion has corank zero by the forward theorem, and
restriction and the two quadratic projections give

  s_2(E_l/K)=s_2(E_l/Q)+s_2(E_l^(k)/Q)=1.

This is a rational corank identity; no integral direct sum or finiteness
of the unknown base Sha is assumed.

The proof in paired-functional-and-uniformity-proof.txt now applies.
It constructs the universal class and its dual functional over the
group ring before character evaluation, removes unused-prime factors
integrally, and supplies a bounded clearing factor. Its bound depends
on the fixed E_l,K, not the binary dimension. Choose the desired bit
precision and dimension, then the finite networks, then an actual
coefficient stage meeting their finitely many requirements. The odd
depth and the companion remain fixed throughout.

If the base derivative vanished, the central Heegner class and its
determinant coordinate would vanish. Binary coefficient transfer would
then force a nonzero-address coordinate to have valuation above the
uniform upper bound. This contradiction proves (RC).

6. The finite Selmer hypothesis actually supplied by descent

For full rational two-torsion, the finite Kummer sequence gives

  dim Sel_2(E_l/Q)=rank E_l(Q)+2+dim Sha(E_l/Q)[2].

If its left side is 3, write the 2-primary Sha group as its maximal
divisible subgroup (Q_2/Z_2)^t plus a finite group F. Its finite quotient
has a nondegenerate alternating Cassels--Tate pairing, since the
principal polarization comes from the rational divisor (O). Hence
dim F[2] is even. It follows that F=0 and rank+t=1. Thus the full
2-primary Selmer corank is one, exactly the hypothesis of (RC).

Apply (RC) and the forward theorem. The rank is one; substituting back
in the finite Kummer equation gives Sha[2]=0. Every finite 2-Selmer
class is now represented by a rational point. No inference from finite
Selmer dimension directly to a positive Mordell--Weil rank was made.

7. Applying the converse to the actual eleven-witness predicate

Let S0={2,3,5,7,13} and M={+d,-d: d divides 2730}. This list represents
all real, 2-adic and 3-adic squareclasses, as shown by the explicit
invertible six-bit signature calculation in explicit-pole-predicate.txt.
For B in the statement of PC, choose m in M so that A=mB is positive,
a square at 2, and a nonsquare unit squareclass at 3. Then m>0. Put

  H=A(A-B^2)=A^2(1-B/m).

H is positive and a local square at every prime of S0. Indeed m>=1,
B<1/2, and v_p(B/m)>=3. Let beta,theta be the positive squarefree
representatives of A,H. They avoid 2 and 3; beta is a square at 2
and nonsquare at 3, while theta is a square at both.

Their supports are disjoint, and each is a local square at every prime
of the other's support. To verify this, at a prime q outside S0 with
v_q(A) odd, the square reduced denominator of B forces v_q(B)>0,
and 1-B/m is a square unit. Thus H is locally square. Conversely, if
v_q(H) is odd, then v_q(A) is even. Positive or negative v_q(B) would
make v_q(H) even, so v_q(B)=0 and cancellation requires B/m=1 modulo q.
Then A=m^2(B/m) is a square locally. At S0 the conclusion follows from
the already verified local square property of H.

Use the simultaneous matrix construction in
pole-descent-completeness-audit.txt, sections 1-5. Here are its exact
interfaces. If B_set is the nonempty support of beta and J is the
support of theta, adjoin one prime of type ([-1/q],[3/q])=(1,1) and
|B_set|-1 primes of type (0,0). Prescribe the latter cross-symbol
matrix to span the even-sum hyperplane of F_2^(B_set). The smaller
deleted matrix has the block form

  [ A C; C^t 0 ],      1^t A 1=1,

and is nonsingular. The full-product coefficient in the enlarged
matrix's free diagonal bits is this same determinant, so those bits
can be chosen to make the enlarged matrix nonsingular as well.
Quadratic reciprocity supplies the reverse symbols; CRT and Dirichlet
realize the auxiliary primes without changing old symbols.

The resulting squarefree parameters n and n theta are both positive,
coprime to 6, and 11 modulo 12. For both, the exact local Kummer
calculation gives dim Sel_2=3 and includes the class (beta,1), which
is distinct from all two-torsion classes. Sections 4 and 6 therefore
apply to both parameters. They supply rational points with nonzero
x-coordinate in the class beta=[A]. This is the precise location at
which (RC) enters PC, and it uses no l outside its stated range.

Write H=theta c^2. Scaling the second point by (x,y)->(c^2x,c^3y)
gives a point on E_(nH). If the first x-coordinate is A q^2, divide
both curve parameters by q^2 and all point coordinates by (q^2,q^3).
Put e=n/q^2. The two points now have x-coordinates A and A z^2 for
some nonzero rational z, on E_e and E_(eH). Their ordinates give

  r1^2=A(A-e)(A+3e),
  r2^2=A(A z^2-eH)(A z^2+3eH),

where the second ordinate was divided by z. Set w=1/(eHz), and encode
m by the six Boolean multiplier bits. These are exactly the eleven
witnesses of the saved pole polynomial. This proves PC.

Status of this conclusion

The restricted converse and PC now have a combined written proof using
the component source-level arguments named above and their cited
classical theorems. The scope correction l=11 modulo 12 is part of the
statement, not an extra assumption on the rational input B. The
simultaneous descent constructs both needed parameters in that range.

This closes the pointwise/pole-completeness portion of the construction
at the level of the written mathematical argument. It is not a
machine-checked proof of (RC) or PC. The separate end-to-end completion
audit still has to combine PC with the actual multiplication template,
the six-contact height and prime-pattern inputs, and the two translated
integer circuits before declaring the saved U universally correct.

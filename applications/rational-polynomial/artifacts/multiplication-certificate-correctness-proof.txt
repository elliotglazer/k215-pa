The actual multiplication template: soundness and conditional completeness
3 November 2026

Scope and inputs

This proof concerns the template named multiply in the library of
universal-rational-equation.json, SHA256
be7347c439459759121cda9656eda501afd75ca48897aa0f0acf5f85f6a43b1f.
Fresh source generation is bound to that exact file by
rational-translation-binding-checks.json. The parameters are three
five-coordinate point codes, and all 35134 witnesses are rational.

Use the following previously proved inputs, with their exact scope:

 G. On E/F, F=Q(sqrt(2)), E:y^2=x^3-(75-53sqrt(2))^2*x,
    the stated point P generates D=2E(F), and has infinite order.
    See elliptic-generator-proof.txt and explicit-elliptic-data.txt.

 H. For C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60} and S containing all primes <=324,
    h(t)<=Aheight*M_S(t)^24, where M_S(t) is the product of primes
    outside S with v_p(t-c) positive and odd for some c in C.
    The actual Aheight is an admissible integer for the actual S.
    See six-contact-height-proof.txt and height-constant-binding-checks.json.

 L. For every integer A, the three slopes u_j/v with sum A can be
    chosen with all 22 integral forms v,u_j-cv (c in C union {1})
    equal to signed fixed-catalogue multipliers times arbitrarily
    large selected primes. At each such prime E and its conjugate
    split with good reduction, their orders d1,d2 have gcd(d1,d2)=4,
    and p does not divide d1*d2. See twenty-two-prime-pattern-proof.txt.

The only further assertion used for completeness below is:

 PC. If B is rational with square reduced denominator, 0<B<1/2,
     and v_p(B)>=4 for p in {2,3,5,7,13}, then the eleven-witness
     polynomial Q_pole(B,...) has a rational zero.

PC is the exact pole-completeness assertion in
pole-descent-completeness-audit.txt. Its positive-rank input is the
restricted pointwise-converse argument; this proof does not validate
that new arithmetic argument by assuming PC.

Result. Using G and H, every rational zero of multiply(A,B,C) has
A=iota(a), B=iota(b), C=iota(ab), with a,b in Z. Using also L and PC,
every such triple has a rational zero. The elementary direction of the
pole predicate, used in soundness, does not depend on PC.

1. Interpreting the actual library constraints

The member template is the exact doubling image 2E(F). Its finite
branch gives a finite preimage with nonzero y, its tangent slope,
and the doubled output coordinates. The other branch gives O.
Every point in 2E(F) is obtained this way: a double that is not O
has a preimage that is neither O nor a two-torsion point.

The height_certificate conjunction imposes member on all three root
codes. By G they are iota(n1),iota(n2),iota(n3), for integers ni.
The outer scalar equations then force C0=[cstar]P, Ci=[cstar]Pi.
The scalar graphs are applied only to valid nontorsion cyclic points
or O, so their division-polynomial guards lose no intended solution.

Each test_group chooses four points H,U1,U2,U3 in D and defines
V0=H-C0, Vi=Ui-Ci using exact group addition. Thus write
 H=[h]P, Ui=[zi]P, V0=[h-cstar]P, Vi=[zi-cstar*ni]P.
It requires H,V0 finite and their L values nonzero. Here
 L(z)=16575z-6630d^2 z^5+11050d^4 z^9-25500d^6 z^13
       +68250d^8 z^17-198900d^10 z^21+612612d^12 z^25,
 d=75-53sqrt(2), z(Q)=-x(Q)/y(Q), z(O)=0.
The inverse-x convention at O is zero.

The twelve outputs are, in order: the four inverse-x values at H,Ui;
the conjugates of the four inverse-x values at V0,Vi; the three
differences
 L(z(Ui))/L(z(H)) - conjugate(L(z(Vi))/L(z(V0)));
and the product of the first two unprimed ratios minus the third.
This is precisely the output order in multiplication-certificate.js.

The slope template excludes t in C union {1}, sets eta^2=1,
 R0=eta*product_(c in C)(t-c)/(t-1)^8,
 a=1-R0*q^2, R=ell^2*a^2*R0,
and requires a!=0. For six independent test groups and precisions
k_j=1 for j<=8 and k_j=25 for j>=9 it sets
 C_gj=D_gj/[R0^k_j*(t-1)^(k_j+1)],
 W_g=product_j(1+R*C_gj^2),
 Theta=Tr_F/Q(sum_g 1/W_g), B=R/Theta^2,
with explicit nonzero guards on W_g and Theta, and requires Phi0(B).
The letter B in this local calculation denotes this scalar, not a
root-code parameter. All quotients are guarded or have denominators
already proved nonzero by the same conjunction.

2. The elementary pole obstruction

The Q_pole formula is displayed in explicit-pole-predicate.txt.
For a nonzero B it supplies a signed divisor m of 2730, sets
A=mB, H0=A(A-B^2), and gives two points with nonzero x-squareclass A
on the curves y^2=x(x-e)(x+3e) and y^2=x(x-eH0)(x+3eH0).
Its inverse constraint ensures e,H0 and the second x-coordinate
are nonzero. Zero ordinates cause no difficulty.

If p does not divide 2730 and v_p(B)<0 is odd, then
v_p(A)=v_p(B) and v_p(H0)=3v_p(B) are odd. One of e,eH0 has even
valuation. Square scaling makes that parameter a p-adic unit without
changing the x-squareclass. On y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l), with l a unit and
p!=2,3, a nonzero x of positive valuation gives RHS valuation v_p(x),
and one of negative valuation gives RHS valuation 3v_p(x). Either
must be even. A unit x also has even valuation. This contradicts the
odd valuation of its squareclass A. Consequently

 Phi0(B) implies no negative odd valuation outside {2,3,5,7,13}.

This argument is elementary and uses neither PC nor a Selmer converse.

3. One slope forces divisibility at each height-contact prime

Let p outside the prescribed S satisfy v_p(t-c)=delta>0 odd for
some c in C. All finite contacts stay distinct modulo p, since their
nonzero differences are at most 61 and p>324. Thus t-1 and the other
five t-c' are units, and v_p(R0)=delta. Also ell and cstar are units.

If q!=0, delta+2v_p(q) is odd and nonzero. It follows that
b=v_p(a)=min(0,delta+2v_p(q))<=0. For q=0 take b=0. Put
alpha=v_p(R)=delta+2b; alpha is odd. Define, only for this argument,
D'_gj=(ell*a)^(2k_j)*D_gj/(t-1)^(k_j+1), so C_gj=D'_gj/R^k_j.

At every place v above p, the valuation of each factor of W_g is
 min(0, alpha+2(v(D'_gj)-k_j*alpha)).
The expression after the comma is odd, so no equal-valuation
cancellation is possible. In particular every v(W_g)<=0.
Let lambda be the maximum v(W_g) over the six groups and all places
above p. Then v_p(Theta)>=-lambda>=0, including in the inert case,
since conjugation preserves the valuation there. By section 2 the
odd valuation v_p(B)=alpha-2v_p(Theta) is nonnegative. Thus
 alpha>0 and lambda>=-alpha/2.

Choose one common group and place attaining lambda. All its factor
valuations are nonpositive and sum to lambda, so each is at least
lambda. Rearranging gives, for every j in this same group and place,
 v(D'_gj)>=(k_j-3/4)*alpha,
 v(D_gj)>=(k_j-3/4)*delta-(3/2)*b>=k_j-3/4.
The extension is unramified at p, so these valuations are integers.
Consequently all eight inverse-x tests have valuation >=1 and all
four other tests have valuation >=25, simultaneously.

4. From the twelve local tests to p^25 | n1*n2-n3

At good unramified p>324, a point with v(1/x)>=1 is in the formal
group (or is O under the convention above). Thus the eight tests put
H,Ui and the conjugate V0,Vi in their required formal groups. In the
formal parameter z, v(1/x)=2v(z), so nonzero such points have v(z)>=1.

The formal logarithm is normalized by dz at O. The displayed L is
16575 times its truncation through degree 25; the next possible
nonzero degree is 29. For integral Weierstrass coefficients the
logarithm coefficient in degree r has valuation at least -v_p(r).
For p>324 and v(z)>=1, the omitted terms therefore have valuation
at least 29v(z); the leading scalar 16575 is a unit. Both the full
logarithm and L preserve the valuation of z.

The full local logarithm extends additively from the formal group to
E(F_v), by multiplying into that group and dividing the logarithm.
Its kernel is torsion. Since P is nontorsion, its local logarithm is
nonzero. Hence the exact full-log ratios of the above points are
 r_i=zi/h and r'_i=(zi-cstar*ni)/(h-cstar).
The guards imply h and h-cstar are nonzero.

At least one of h,h-cstar is a p-adic unit. Its corresponding r_i
or r'_i is integral for every i, since all indices are integers.
The equality of valuations between full and truncated logarithms,
and the valuation-25 difference tests, make both ratios integral.
For an integral ratio, truncation changes it by valuation at least
28, so, writing L_i,L'_i for the two truncated ratios,
 zi/h=L_i mod p^25,
 (zi-cstar*ni)/(h-cstar)=L'_i mod p^25,
 L_i=L'_i mod p^25.
Zero numerator points obey the same congruences with value zero.

Multiply the first two congruences by the integer denominators and
subtract. Since cstar is a p-adic unit, ni=L_i mod p^25. The twelfth
test now gives n1*n2-n3=0 mod p^25. For a rational integer this local
divisibility at any unramified place is exactly ordinary p^25
divisibility. This proves the soundness consequence for each slope.

5. Height bound, explicit finite box, and soundness of multiply

Let f=n1*n2-n3!=0. Section 4 shows M_S(t)^25 divides f for each
of the 27 slopes. Input H gives h(t)<=Aheight*|f|^(24/25).
The height_certificate constraints express the two x-coordinates
of each root as a0/b,a1/b with b!=0, each of a0,a1,b being a sum
of three slopes. No integrality of these coordinates is assumed.
The rational height inequalities for sums and quotients give
 h(x_j)<=6Aheight*|f|^(24/25)+2log(3).

The calculation in finite-guard-bound.txt applies unchanged to six
contacts: it uses only this slope height inequality. For completeness,
the isomorphism x_C=(1+sqrt(2))^4*x_E to
C:y^2=x^3-(3-sqrt(2))^2*x and the duplication bounds there give
 2*hat_h(P)>1,
 2*ni^2*hat_h(P)<=12Aheight*|f|^(24/25)+B0, B0<10.
The additive constant includes the field-component height inequality,
the fixed isomorphism, and log(676)/3. All are independent of ni.

For N=max|ni|>=1 and |f|<=2N^2 this implies
 N^2<24Aheight*N^(48/25)+10.
If N>=5, then N^(2/25)<48Aheight and hence
 N<(48Aheight)^(25/2)<=(48Aheight)^13=T.
The same final bound holds for N<=4, since Aheight>=1.

Every false triple admitting the unguarded certificate is therefore
inside [-T,T]^3. The actual finite_guard_product includes its exact
denominator-cleared squared-distance factor. That factor vanishes
at its three canonical codes, making the guard product zero. The
outer guard demands a multiplicative inverse, a contradiction.
Thus every zero of multiply is a true multiplication triple. PC and
the new pointwise converse were not used in this direction.

6. Slopes for true triples and the exceptional primes

Now let n1*n2=n3. Choose integral homogeneous coordinates for the
three actual root x-coordinates, using (0,0,1) for O. Apply L to
each of their nine integer quantities. The resulting three slopes
sum to that quantity and avoid all seven finite excluded contacts.

Put Sb={primes<=324} union Hcat. At p outside Sb, each of the 22
forms has valuation zero or one. If v_p(v)=0, at most one finite
contact form for a given slope is divisible by p. A contact in C
gives v_p(R0)=1 and the contact 1 gives -8. If v_p(v)=1 while
v_p(u_j)=0, the slope has negative valuation and v_p(R0)=2. If both
have valuation one, all contact forms must also have valuation one;
after cancellation all normalized contacts are units and v_p(R0)=0.
Thus outside Sb the odd valuations of R0 are exactly +1 at at most
six selected primes. The selected primes were chosen beyond max(S),
where S=Sb union {prime divisors of cstar}.

7. Constructing the six test groups

Assign a distinct group to each of those at most six primes p.
Let d1,d2 be the orders of the two good split reductions. Choose h
by the compatible congruences
 h=0 mod lcm(M,d1), h=cstar mod d2.
Their common divisor divides M*gcd(d1,d2)=4M=cstar. A solution
exists, and can be chosen h>cstar. Set H=[h]P and Ui=[ni]H.
Then H,Ui reduce to O at the first place, and V0,Vi do so at the
conjugate place. Their exact full-log ratios are all ni. The formal
truncation estimate in section 4 makes every D_gj meet its required
precision at the assigned group and place, since f(ni)=0.
Unused groups can use any h>cstar divisible by M and the same Ui.

At every prime of Sb, H,Ui,V0,Vi lie in the small formal subgroup
specified by M, since h and h-cstar are multiples of M. Every such
nonzero point has normalized v(z)>=2. The coefficient bound
v(c_r)>=-v_p(r) for the normalized logarithm makes its full series
and degree-25 truncation equal to z times a unit there. L differs
by the same fixed scalar in numerator and denominator. Thus the
two truncated ratios for Ui=[ni]H and Vi=[ni]V0 are integral at all
these places. All twelve D_gj are integral there. H,V0 and their
L values are globally nonzero, so all group guards are satisfied.

8. Normalization produces exactly the inputs covered by PC

For p in Sb, let Bp=4 for p<=324 and Bp=1 for catalogue primes.
The form valuations imply |v_p(t-c)|<=Bp and the conservative bounds
 |v_p(R0)|, |v_p(t-1)|<=Cp=14Bp.
Hence v(C_gj)>=-Dp with Dp=51Cp for all j and places above p.
The actual ell has exponent Ep=52Cp+v_p(12)+4: this is
ell=12*P0^2916*Hproduct^732, as saved in the fixed constants.

Choose eta so R0>0. Weak approximation supplies rational q integral
at all selected primes, sufficiently small p-adically at Sb, and
arbitrarily close in R to a root of 1-R0*q^2, without equality.
Thus a is a unit at Sb and the selected primes, but is nonzero and
arbitrarily small in R. At p in Sb,
 v(R*C_gj^2)>=2Ep-Cp-2Dp=Cp+2v_p(12)+8>v_p(12).
Therefore v_p(Theta-12)>v_p(12), and
 v_p(B)>=2Ep-Cp-2v_p(12)=103Cp+8>=4.

At a selected odd-contact prime, v_p(R)=1. Every inverse W_g has
residue zero or one at either split place: the factor valuation
1+2v(C_gj) is odd, so a factor is either 1 modulo p or has negative
valuation. The assigned group and place has every C_gj integral,
so its inverse W_g has residue one. Theta is the sum of twelve such
residues, at least one of them one. Since p>324, Theta is a unit
and v_p(B)=1. At every other p outside Sb, v_p(R0) is even, so
v_p(B)=v_p(R0)+2v_p(ell*a/Theta) is even. These statements allow
negative EVEN valuations and exclude negative odd ones everywhere.
The reduced denominator of B is consequently a square.

Finally R>0 and every W_g is positive in both real embeddings.
The C_gj are independent of q. As a tends to zero, all W_g tend to
one, Theta tends to twelve, and B tends to zero through positive
values. All W_g and Theta are nonzero. Choose q close enough to
obtain 0<B<1/2, simultaneously with the stated local conditions.
PC supplies the pole witnesses. This proves completeness of each
slope and hence of the height certificate for every true triple.

9. Adding the guard and locating the unresolved arithmetic input

At a true triple, none of the false-triple squared-distance factors
vanishes, since canonical codes are injective. Their finite product
is a nonzero rational, so its inverse supplies the guard witness.
The exact fixed scalar and membership witnesses already exist.
Thus, conditional on PC and using G,H,L, every true triple satisfies
the ACTUAL outer multiply polynomial.

Combining this with section 5 gives hypothesis M of
rational-universality-conditional-proof.txt. Its soundness direction
alone already proves that a rational zero of U(e,n,...) implies that
program e halts on input n: the extraction part of that proof uses
only soundness of multiplication. Completeness of U still requires PC.

The dedicated pole-descent audit reduces PC to a positive-rank
assertion for a precisely specified full-rational-two-torsion family
with full 2-primary Selmer corank one. That new restricted pointwise
argument remains the arithmetic dependency to finish validating.
The present certificate proof neither removes it nor infers it from
finite tests, matrix identities, or the candidate's assembly audit.

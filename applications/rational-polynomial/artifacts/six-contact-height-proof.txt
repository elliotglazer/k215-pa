A source-level proof of the six-contact height estimate
3 November 2026

This proves the height input used by the current candidate polynomial
from the published arithmetic-geometric inputs listed below. It does
not prove the restricted pointwise 2-converse, certificate completeness,
or the correctness of the universal compiler. It is not a Lean proof.

Statement and constants

Let
 C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60},
 F(u)=u(u+15)(u+20)(u+24)(u+30)(u+60),
 G(u)=7u^2+300u+3600,
 J(u)=-57600 F(u)^2/G(u)^6.
Let S be any finite set of rational primes containing every prime at
most 324. For u in Q outside C, define
 M_S(u)=product of primes p outside S for which
        v_p(u-c) is positive and odd for some c in C.
The product is squarefree; an empty product is one. Negative valuations
of u do not enter this definition.

Put
 n=324, d0=n!,
 d=d0*3^(256*d0^4),
 G0=116064000000,
 E0=10*(d*G0)^2,
 R=max(d,max(S),10^12),
 Q0=R^(R*E0),
 B(d)=d^2*3^(21000*d^2)+1,
 C_CM(d)=2*B(d)+4096*(1028+5*d+84000*d^2),
 H6(S)=2*10^13*max(1,C_CM(d),Q0^17).
Then, for the absolute logarithmic rational height,
                   h(u)<=H6(S)*M_S(u)^24.                 (1)

The same conclusion holds with any larger R in the definition of Q0.
This is the version needed when the certificate enlarges S.

1. The fine quaternionic cover, including all components

Use the indefinite division algebra B=(2,-3), with i^2=2, j^2=-3 and
ij=-ji, and the maximal order
 O=Z[i,(1+j)/2].
Set mu=2j+ij, so mu^2=-6. On O put
 psi(x,y)=Trd(mu^(-1)*x*bar(y)).
Choose the half-plane and the sign of psi to give the positive
polarization. In the basis 1,i,(1+j)/2,(i+ij)/2 its matrix is
 [0,0,-1,1; 0,0,1,-2; 1,-1,0,0; -1,2,0,0].
It is alternating, integral and has determinant one. Right
multiplication is a symplectic similitude with multiplier Nrd.
This is the principally polarized quaternionic moduli problem.

The published discriminant-six description identifies the normalizer
quotient with P^1_j. Its orbifold points are j=0,infinity,-16/27, of
orders 4,6,2. The norm-one quotient V has signature (0;2,2,3,3);
its Atkin--Lehner quotient has degree four. The forgetful map to
principally polarized abelian surfaces factors through this quotient.

These facts are in Baba--Granath, Section 3, the table in Section 3.1,
and Proposition 10. Lin--Yang, introduction, equation (2), identifies
the moduli coordinate by
 [J2,J4,J6,J10]=[12(j+1),6(j^2+j+1),4(j^3-2j^2+1),j^3].
In particular equal j-values give the same geometric polarized
moduli point. At the rational u-values in (1), j is finite and nonzero.

Now let K0=O_hat^* and
 K3=ker(K0 -> (O/3O)^*).
The associated Shimura curve Y3 carries full level three for its
symplectic lattice, hence a universal abelian scheme. All components
of Y3 are retained. It is important not to discard the component
permutation when computing a degree over Q.

Let N be the finite-adelic normalizer of O_hat. Conjugation by N
preserves 3*O_hat, so N normalizes K3. Its quotient by central ideles
and K0 has order four: at split primes the normalizer consists of
central scalars times order units, while each of the two ramified
primes contributes one parity of a uniformizer.

Central finite ideles act trivially at level K3. To see this, multiply
such an idele by a rational scalar to make it a unit at every finite
prime. Its component at three is 1 or -1 modulo three. Changing the
rational scalar's sign makes it 1 modulo three. The remaining central
idele belongs to K3, and a rational central scalar acts trivially on
the symmetric domain. Consequently
 H=N/(A_f^* K3)
acts on Y3 and
 |H|<=4*|(O/3O)^*|<=4*81=324.                           (2)
After removing any ineffective action the bound still holds.
Its quotient is P^1_j. This argument counts the entire cover, including
the permutations of its geometric components.

The exact finite calculation in verify-six-contact.js gives 72 units
in O/3O and 36 of norm one. Quotienting the units by the two central
residues gives the stronger bound |H|<=144. We retain 324 in every
constant of the candidate; no coefficient change is required.

There are no elliptic stabilizers at level K3. Indeed a stabilizer
lifts to a finite-order integral symplectic automorphism congruent to
the identity modulo three, and that congruence kernel is torsion-free.
The usual proof expands a prime-order power I+3^a X: reduction modulo
3^(a+1), or 3^(a+2) for order three, contradicts X nonzero modulo three.
This works over Z_3 as well as over Z.

Thus each geometric component is a torsion-free orbifold cover of V.
Since V has orbifold Euler characteristic -1/3, every such compact
component has negative Euler characteristic, hence genus at least two.
The ramification of Y3 -> P^1_j is exactly 4,6,2 at the three points
above and nowhere else.

2. Good integral models without increasing the cover degree

Fix a prime p>324. Here B_p=M2(Q_p), O_p=M2(Z_p), psi is self-dual,
and the p-component of K3 is hyperspecial. Right multiplication gives
a closed reductive embedding into GSp: the matrix determines the
multiplier on 1, and the equations for right multiplication are
linear. Its group of units is GL2 over Z_p.

Kisin's Theorem 2.3.8, with its setup in 2.3.1--2.3.5, supplies the
smooth integral canonical models at sufficiently small prime-to-p
levels, with finite etale transition maps and a map to integral
Siegel moduli. Only p>2 is used here.

One may descend from a sufficiently small auxiliary level to K3.
This does not enlarge the final degree in (2). The K3 moduli problem
already has no automorphisms: a polarized automorphism is of finite
order; its action on the 3-adic Tate module is faithful; and full
level three puts it in the torsion-free congruence kernel. The
finite changes of auxiliary prime-to-p marking therefore act freely.
Their quotients and the universal abelian schemes descend etale
locally. This gives a smooth K3 model and its abelian scheme at p.
An auxiliary level is only a construction of this model, not an
additional level in the cover or in the constants.

Properness requires a separate argument. An abelian surface with this
B-action has potentially good reduction at a valuation. After finite
extension it is semistable and the B-action is defined. If its toric
part had positive dimension t, then 1<=t<=2 and its rational character
space would be a nonzero unital B-module of dimension t. A division
quaternion algebra has no nonzero module over Q of dimension below
four. Thus t=0. This reasoning also applies at CM fibers; it uses
only the specified B-action.

Full level three now makes the finite inertia action trivial: a
finite subgroup of I+3*M4(Z_3) is trivial. Neron--Ogg--Shafarevich
gives good reduction over the trait itself, since p is not three.
The polarization, the O-action and the prime-to-p level extend.
The point stays in the closed moduli image, and its lift extends
through the finite normalization defining the canonical model.
This valuative argument proves properness. Equivalently it applies
at an auxiliary fine level and properness descends to K3. Hence Y3
has a smooth proper model at p with its abelian scheme.

Every element of H extends to this model. One can use functoriality
of the integral canonical model, or uniqueness of the smooth proper
model on the genus-at-least-two components. The finite etale scheme
of components also extends their permutation action.

Since p>|H|, this action is tame. For a nonidentity stabilizer element,
the derivative at a fixed point is a nontrivial root of unity.
A finite-order power series of order prime to p with derivative one
is the identity. The fixed-point equation therefore has invertible
linear term, so each fixed point lifts uniquely and its fixed locus
is finite etale over the trait. Reduction is faithful for this tame
action; an automorphism trivial modulo p would be trivial modulo
every power of p by expanding its prime-to-p order.

These observations also prevent collisions of branch orbits.
A stabilizer embeds faithfully in the multiplicative group of the
tangent line and is cyclic. A generator at a special point has its
unique fixed lift. All powers fix that lift, so the same stabilizer
occurs generically. Two generic branch orbits cannot merge without
creating a larger special stabilizer or violating uniqueness of a
fixed lift. Thus the quotient is smooth and the three branch
sections remain disjoint, with the same inertia orders 4,6,2.

The quotient has genus zero and these rational sections, so it is
P^1 over Z_p. The stated coordinate j gives this integral model:
0,infinity,-16/27 are disjoint over Z_p, since 2 and 3 are units.
The coordinate transformation fixed by these three sections is an
integral projective linear transformation. There is therefore no
unaccounted denominator prime greater than 324.

3. Pullback to the six contacts

Put
 H(u)=(u^2-60u-1800)(13u^2+480u+3600)
                         (11u^2+600u+7200).
Exact polynomial arithmetic gives
                G(u)^6-97200*F(u)^2=H(u)^2.              (3)
The degree-twelve map J has six simple roots of F over 0, of
ramification index two; two roots of G over infinity, of index six;
and six roots of H over -16/27, of index two. Their ramification
contribution is 6+10+6=22=2*12-2, so there are no others.

The discriminants and pairwise resultants of G and the three
quadratics in H have prime support in {2,3,5}. The same is true
of G(c) for c in C and of differences of contacts. Their leading
coefficients are units for p>324. Equation (3) and these tests show
that the three fibers stay disjoint and separable in those residue
characteristics, including their projective points.

Let Z be the normalization of Y3 pulled back by J. Base change and
normalization do not increase the degree over the new base, so
                  deg(Z -> P^1_u)<=324.                  (4)
This is not multiplied by twelve.

For a tame cover of local index e and a base change of index a, the
normalized local index is e/gcd(e,a). More explicitly, after an
unramified extension and extracting roots of units, the equation
z^e=v^a splits into gcd(e,a) branches with
 v=w^(e/gcd(e,a)), z=zeta*w^(a/gcd(e,a)).
Their local rings are smooth power-series rings over the base DVR.
Here the remaining indices are 2,1,1 over the three fibers in (3).
Consequently Z is smooth and proper at every p>324, and its only
branch divisor over u consists of the six sections C, all of index
two. It carries the pulled-back abelian scheme.

Locally at a contact the map has equation u-c=z^2 after unramified
extension. A rational specialization with positive even valuation
of u-c therefore lifts over an unramified extension; units acquire
square roots unramifiedly. Away from the contacts the map is etale.
If v_p(u)<0, its reduction is infinity, which is not a contact, so
this case also causes no ramification. This proves precisely the
odd-contact rule defining M_S in (1).

4. Bounded fiber and Hom fields

Write M=M_S(u) and T=S union {prime divisors of M}. The splitting
field K0 of the finite etale fiber of (4) has degree at most d0=324!.
It is Galois and unramified outside T by the local calculation.
Choose a fiber A/K0. Properness and the abelian scheme show that A
has good reduction at every place outside S, even when K0 ramifies
at a prime dividing M. Its Galois conjugates have the same j-value,
hence are geometrically isomorphic as polarized abelian surfaces.

There are at most d0 conjugates. The direct sum of the geometric
Hom groups between every ordered pair has rank at most 16*d0^2:
each Hom embeds in the homomorphisms of integral rank-four homology.
The Galois image on this lattice is finite. Its kernel inside G_K0
is normal in G_Q because G_Q permutes the ordered pairs.

Every finite subgroup of GL_r(Z) injects modulo three, by the
torsion-free congruence-kernel argument above. The resulting Galois
Hom field L therefore has
 [L:Q]<=d0*3^((16*d0^2)^2)=d.
At a prime outside T, inertia already acts trivially on the Tate
modules of every conjugate, so it acts trivially on this Hom lattice.
Thus L is unramified outside T. All geometric homomorphisms and the
B-action are defined over L, and A remains good outside S.

5. The arithmetic height bound for these fibers

The arguments in effective-cm-height-bound.txt and
explicit-noncm-height-bound.txt apply with this d and S. The following
checks identify their hypotheses and close their use for this family.

On rational homology the prescribed B acts by left multiplication.
Its commuting Hodge endomorphisms are the elements of right B that
commute with the complex structure. They form either Q or an
imaginary quadratic field k. Indeed the real centralizer of the
complex structure is C, and a nonrational element of the rational
division algebra in that centralizer generates an imaginary
quadratic field. The full geometric endomorphism algebra is
B tensor this centralizer.

In the quadratic case it is M2(k), since a maximal quadratic
subfield splits B. Matrix units are defined over L. Their images
give a CM elliptic curve E/L and an L-isogeny A~E^2. The bound in
effective-cm-height-bound.txt therefore gives
                        h_F(A)<=C_CM(d).                 (5)
Its required input, Fowler's Proposition 2.7, applies to all
imaginary quadratic orders. The exact constant and the original
stable height convention were checked against the published
sources in height-primary-input-review.txt.

In the other case End^0(A)=B and A is absolutely simple. The
conjugacy isogenies can be chosen B-linear by Skolem--Noether.
Their composition discrepancy is rational. Dividing each isogeny
by the positive fourth root of its degree reduces this discrepancy
to a sign cocycle epsilon on Gal(L/Q).

Here is the control needed in the descent argument. View epsilon
as a two-torsion Brauer class. It is locally zero outside T because
there it factors through the unramified quotient. The square-root
obstruction for a finite local character is nonzero exactly when
the character takes value -1 on -1 in local reciprocity. At each
odd prime with nonzero invariant choose an odd character modulo
that prime; at two use the character modulo four if needed.
Their global product chi has the required obstruction at the
finite primes and hence also at infinity by Brauer reciprocity.
Its order m satisfies
             m<=2*product_(q in T, q odd)(q-1)
               <=2*R^R*M.

A continuous square-root cochain can consequently be corrected
by signs to beta with beta^2=chi and delta(beta)=epsilon.
On G_L it is a conjugacy-invariant character. Outside T its
nontrivial inertia restrictions, if any, are quadratic at finitely
many odd primes. The quadratic character of the product of those
primes cancels them, introducing possible ramification only at two,
which is already in S. It changes neither chi nor the value group
mu_(2m). Set L' to be the kernel field of beta restricted to G_L.
Then L'/Q is Galois and
                    [L':Q]<=2*m*d<=Q0*M.                 (6)

For coefficient primes ell=5 and ell=3, the corrected conjugacy
action on V_ell(A) commutes with B and splits over a coefficient
extension as r_ell plus r_ell, with r_ell two-dimensional.
On G_L' it is the actual Tate action. Faltings' semisimplicity and
endomorphism theorem make r_ell absolutely irreducible: the
commutant of the four-dimensional action is M2 and the multiplicity
representation has scalar commutant. The representation is de Rham,
with cyclotomic exponents 0,1, by finite descent of the de Rham
property. Complex conjugation followed by a holomorphic isogeny
exchanges the two Hodge subspaces, so it cannot be scalar; its
eigenvalues on r_ell are 1,-1.

Pan's Theorem 1.0.4 at p=5 now gives a weight-two newform f.
There is no extra residual-image condition at this coefficient
prime. Comparing the traces of reduction of the same conjugacy
isogeny followed by relative Frobenius identifies r_3 with the
three-adic representation of this same f. Endomorphism traces
are rational and independent of ell; Chebotarev then gives the
identification. Thus r_3 can measure its conductor at five.

For completeness the uniform conductor calculation is as follows.
At a rational prime q, put F_local=L_v and adjoin full three-torsion,
or full five-torsion if q=3. The extension has degree at most d*G0
over Q_q. It gives semistable reduction and trivial wild Tate action.
For a local extension of degree a, its normal closure has no upper
ramification above 4*a^2. This follows from the different bound
d_F/e<=1-1/e+v_q(e), the integral different formula, and e<=a!
in the normal closure. Hence
 U0=4*(d*G0)^2
kills the wild Tate action in absolute upper numbering.

At odd q the square of beta on principal units is trivial and a
pro-q group has no nontrivial sign character. At q=2, beta on units
has order at most four; the local logarithm gives
 (U_F^(e+1))^4=U_F^(3*e+1).
Thus its relative break is at most 3e<=3d, also below U0 after
upper-numbering comparison. The conductor of r_ell is therefore
at most 2+2U0<=E0 at every q. At q>R, the bounded-degree extension
L has trivial wild inertia and A is good; the representation is
tame, with conductor at most two. It is unramified outside T.
The primitive level of f consequently satisfies
                         N<=Q0*M^2.                      (7)

The shared Tate constituent and Faltings' Hom theorem give a
nonzero L'-homomorphism A -> J_1(N). Absolute simplicity gives
finite kernel, so Poincare reducibility supplies
 J_1(N)~A*D0 over L'.
Invariants in the finite-dimensional Hom space commute with
coefficient extension; thus the shared constituent really does
give a rational Hom, not only a coefficient-field morphism.

The remaining numerical transfer is explicit. Write J=J_1(N).
The genus and the degree of its standard Belyi map are at most N^2.
Javanpeykar's Theorem 1.1.1 gives h_F(J)<=13000000*N^12.
Zarhin's trick on the complementary factor gives
 C1=J^8 ~ C2=A^8*DZ over L',
with DZ principally polarized. If g=dim C1 and d'=[L':Q], then
 g<=Q0^3*M^4, d'<=Q0*M, h_F(C1)<=Q0^13*M^24.

Gaudron--Remond's Theorem 1.4 uses the original stable Faltings
height and gives an isogeny C1 -> C2 with
 log(deg phi)<=1024*g^3*(64*g^2*log(14g)+log(d')
                    +2*log max(h_F(C1),log(d'),1)).
Using log(M)<=M^4/4 and log(Q0)<=Q0, the three logarithms are at
most 5*Q0*M^4, 2*Q0*M^4 and 19*Q0*M^4 respectively. Consequently
 log(deg phi)<=368640*Q0^16*M^24<=Q0^17*M^24.
The exact published constant and normalization were checked in
height-primary-input-review.txt.

The lower bound h_F(DZ)>=-2g, additivity, and height variation under
isogeny give
 8*h_F(A)<=Q0^13*M^24+(1/2)*Q0^17*M^24+2*Q0^3*M^4
          <=Q0^17*M^24.
In particular
                    max(1,h_F(A))<=Q0^17*M^24             (8)
in the non-CM case. No prime-two Fontaine--Mazur theorem or
pointwise 2-converse was used in (5)--(8).

6. The explicit comparison with the base coordinate

The full coordinate calculation is in six-contact-height-model.txt,
Sections 5--7. Its inputs have been checked with their normalizations.
Writing z_m for the ten even theta constants, they give
          j=3779136*(product z_m)^4/(sum z_m^8)^5.
Both homogeneous polynomials have degree forty, with coefficient
norms 3779136 and 10^5. Therefore
                 h(j)<=40*h([z_m])+22.                   (9)

These are Pazuki's r=2 coordinates at the zero section: the sixteen
half-characteristic theta values consist of these ten and six zeros.
The quarter-period translation lifts differ from the analytic
characteristic sections only by roots of unity, and meet the
composition and half-period compatibility in his good-choice
definition. There is no uncontrolled change-of-basis matrix.
Ordinary projective height is at most the Fubini--Study height.

For surfaces, Pazuki's Deligne-normalized height is the original
stable height plus log(pi). His Corollary 1.3 consequently gives
 h([z_m])<=10000*2^24*max(1,h_F(A)).
Here the corollary's constant is at most 1000*2^24; the elementary
bound log(3x+2)<=2x for x>=1 absorbs the normalization and logarithm.

Set P=-57600*F^2 and Q=G^6. Their coefficient l1 norms are
 14533295809536000000 and 3556808369970021551449,
both less than 4000^6. The nonzero polynomial P(X)-jQ(X) has u as
a root, so the elementary root-height estimate gives
 h(u)<=h(j)+log(4*4000^6)<h(j)+74.
There is no exceptional rational indeterminacy: G has negative
discriminant, and F(u) is nonzero by the statement's hypothesis.
Combining with (9),
                     h(u)<10^13*max(1,h_F(A)).           (10)

Equations (5), (8), and (10), with M>=1, prove (1). The extra factor
two in H6 is conservative and agrees with the already generated
candidate expression.

7. Evidence and scope

The finite verifier checks the rational-map identity, discriminants,
resultants, contact separation, coefficient norms, pairing, and
level-three ring calculation. It does not test the theorems about
Shimura varieties, modularity or heights. Those enter the written
proof as the named published results with the hypotheses checked
above. The height argument is independent of the new restricted
pointwise 2-converse.

This establishes the six-contact replacement used by the candidate.
It does not compute the missing theta comparison for the original
discriminant-210 five-contact family. The current universal
polynomial still requires the pointwise-converse and compiler
reviews before its universality is established.

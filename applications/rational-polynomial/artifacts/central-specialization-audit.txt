CENTRAL SPECIALIZATION AND QUADRATIC COMPARISON AUDIT
2 November 2026

Scope
-----
This note checks rd:derived-specialization, rd:active-local, and
rd:central-formula in the pinned pointwise 2-converse source. It builds
on finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt, limit-interface-audit.txt, and
determinant-switch-audit.txt. It is a written mathematical audit, not
a Lean proof.

Fix E/Q, its conductor N and modular parametrization. Let K=Q(sqrt(k))
be imaginary quadratic, split at 2N, excluding the fields specified in
the source. Let h be a product of signed, distinct, good odd primes,
all split in K, with (h,k)=1. Write
  t_p = dim_F2 E[2]^{Fr_p},       w(h) = (1/2) sum_{p|h} t_p.
For this note assume the product
  L(E^(h),s) L(E^(hk),s)
has a simple zero. No such assumption is made at the unknown base in
the later missing-vertex argument.

The classical forward theorems of Gross--Zagier and Kolyvagin are inputs:
the primitive weighted Heegner point P_h is nontorsion, E^(h)(K) has
rank one, and the relevant Tate--Shafarevich groups are finite. These
inputs do not invoke the converse being audited. Let j_h be the
2-adic index of P_h in E^(h)(K)^hat_2 modulo torsion, and put
  s(h)=length_Z2 Sha(E^(h)/Q)[2^infinity].

1. Derived specialization
-------------------------
Put Lambda=Z_2[[t]] and A=Lambda_(t). This is a DVR with residue field
Q_2. A bounded free complex over A splits into zero-differential free
cohomology terms and elementary disks
  A --t^a--> A
in adjacent degrees, up to unit factors. A disk with a=0 contracts.
Each disk with a>0 contributes one extra central cohomology dimension
in each of its two degrees and no generic cohomology.

Consequently, if generic and central rational cohomology both have
dimensions one in degrees 1 and 2 and zero elsewhere, no disk with
a>0 can occur. There is an A-linear contraction onto two free lines.
A cycle z and a closed functional lambda defined over A project onto
these lines without division by t. Their determinant tensor is regular
at t=0 and specializes to [z_0] tensor [lambda_0].

This proves regularity for representatives defined over A. Integrality
of an arbitrarily chosen representative over the different DVR
completion of Lambda_(2) would not prove it.

The integral central complex C_0=C tensor^L_Lambda Z_2 must still be
retained. Termwise reduction of a free model computes it. In particular,
  0 -> H^i(C)/t H^i(C) -> H^i(C_0) -> H^{i+1}(C)[t] -> 0.
Ignoring the last term could change the central determinant lattice.

2. The active local quotient
---------------------------
Let F/Q_p be unramified, p odd, let E/F have good reduction, and twist
its Tate module by a ramified quadratic character. Prime-to-2 inertia
has no higher cohomology on this module. The relevant pro-2 tame
inertia generator acts as -1. Its two-term complex has differential
-2, so its cohomology is zero in degree 0 and E[2] in degree 1.

The unramified quotient is procyclic. The residue-cardinality correction
on degree-one inertia cohomology is 1 modulo 2. Hochschild--Serre thus
gives
  H^0(F,T')=0,
  H^1(F,T')=ker(Fr-1 on E[2]),
  H^2(F,T')=coker(Fr-1 on E[2]).
Both finite groups have length t_p. This calculation also allows an
unramified quadratic factor in the twist, because its reduction
modulo 2 is trivial.

For any elliptic curve over F with p odd, its pro-2 point completion is
finite. Local Tate duality identifies the 2-primary Weil--Chatelet
group with the dual of that completion. It is finite as well, and its
inverse Tate module is zero. The inverse-limit Kummer sequence therefore
identifies E^chi(F)^hat_2 with the entire H^1(F,T') above.

The integral Kummer condition U=E^chi(F)^hat_2[-1] includes its derived
degree-zero torsion after finite reduction. Its cone in R Gamma(F,T')
has only H^2, of length t_p. Thus changing the Kummer condition to the
full local condition has an exact, not merely bounded, contribution.

There are two K-primes above each active p|h. Their total local length
is 2t_p. No error proportional to omega(h) may be discarded here.

3. The central Heegner class and its paired functional
----------------------------------------------------
At a finite stage the split auxiliary-prime distribution relation is
  Tr_{H_{|h|r}/H_{|h|}} y_{|h|r}
    = (a_r - sigma_r - sigma_bar_r) y_{|h|}.
Specializing the scalar deformation to 1 and taking the quadratic
weighted sum gives
  z_r(0) = Kummer(L m_r P_h),
  m_r = a_r - 2 chi_h(r),
where L is the single fixed multiplier from the finite descent.

This is a point identity before passing to any limit. On contracted
target complexes the difference is an actual boundary. Retain a
degree-zero boundary witness together with the comparison map from
the fixed central Kummer complex. Their ranks are bounded for this
fixed h and K even though r and the ring-class degree vary. The same
ultrafilter and cofinal precisions preserve this identity.

The character construction gives v_2(m_r)<=c_E and r->1 in Z_2.
Compactness yields a limiting m with v_2(m)<=c_E. Indeed the finite union
of valuation strata 0,...,c_E is closed, so the limit cannot be zero.
The limiting central class is the image of Lm P_h and is nonzero.

At an active local prime, -2 becomes a unit over A. At r, the central
Frobenius determinants are nonzero. The corresponding full local
complexes contract over A. Composing these contractions with the
retained global duality defines the paired functional over A. Its
central value is the functional of the central Kummer point under
rational Kummer duality. It is the same generic functional as the one
used over the completion of Lambda_(2), because both arise from the
same derived comparison.

The central relaxed complex has rational cohomology dimensions 1 in
degrees 1 and 2 and zero elsewhere: the local quotients just described
are finite, and the ordinary Kummer complex has rank one and finite Sha.
Its nonzero degree-one class lifts to an actual A-cycle. This rules out
a generically acyclic disk supplying its central line. More explicitly,
H^0(C_0 tensor Q_2)=0 implies H^1(C tensor A)[t]=0 by base change.
The lifted nonzero class therefore lies in a nonzero free H^1 over A.
Semicontinuity bounds its rank by one; duality gives the other rank.
The hypotheses of section 1 are consequently satisfied. This justifies
the generic rank-one assertion without presupposing it.

4. The integral Kummer determinant
---------------------------------
Let C_Kum be the ordinary integral Kummer Selmer complex of E^(h)/K.
Inverse-limit Kummer theory and finite Sha give
  H^1(C_Kum) = E^(h)(K)^hat_2.
For D=Q_2/Z_2, derived base change gives
  0 -> H^1(C_Kum) tensor D
    -> H^1(C_Kum tensor^L D)
    -> H^2(C_Kum)[2^infinity] -> 0.
The middle term is the discrete 2-primary Selmer group, because the
exact finite Kummer conditions, including degree-zero invariants, were
retained. Its quotient by E^(h)(K) tensor D is Sha[2^infinity].
Hence
  length H^2(C_Kum)_tors = length Sha(E^(h)/K)[2^infinity].
Integral global duality pairs the free parts of H^1 and H^2
unimodularly. Torsion in degrees 1 and 3 has uniformly bounded length.
For example, the fixed scalar from the torsion audit kills the global
2-primary torsion, which is a subgroup of E[2^e] of size at most 2^(2e).

Use the inverse determinant
  D(C)=tensor_i (det C^i)^{(-1)^(i+1)}.
For a disk whose cokernel in degree i is Z_2/2^d, its canonical
cohomology tensor has coordinate of valuation (-1)^(i+1)d.
Thus degree-two torsion subtracts its length.

In a primitive free basis, the point Lm P_h has coefficient of
valuation j_h+v_2(Lm). Unimodular duality gives the same valuation for
its paired functional. Their tensor therefore contributes
2j_h+2v_2(Lm). Consequently the Kummer determinant coordinate has
valuation
  2j_h - length Sha(E^(h)/K)[2^infinity] + O_E(1).       (1)

This computation uses the entire complex, not just the point index
and a guessed lattice for its dual.

5. Relaxation and the auxiliary prime
------------------------------------
There is a distinguished triangle from the Kummer complex to the
relaxed complex with third term the sum of the local Kummer quotients.
The determinant functor is multiplicative for this triangle. Section 2
therefore subtracts exactly
  2 sum_{p|h} t_p = 4w(h).
The argument does not require every local H^2 quotient to be the
cokernel of an injection on global H^2; the triangle accounts for the
neighboring cohomology as well.

At each split place above r, the good unramified central local quotient
has degree-two length
  v_2(r+1-a_r chi_h(r)).
The trace construction imposes
  v_2(2-a_r), v_2(2+a_r) <= c_E,
  v_2(r-1) > c_E.
Writing the local expression as
  (2-a_r chi_h(r))+(r-1)
shows that its valuation is that of the first term, at most c_E.
This also bounds the trace multiplier m_r. These statements persist
in the limit. The two auxiliary contributions are therefore bounded
independently of h and K.

At 2N the original integral Kummer conditions are retained. The local
twisting classes range over a fixed finite set and K is split. All
remaining differential and lattice comparisons there have a bound
depending only on E and the fixed parametrization data.

Combining these observations with (1) gives
  v_2(u_h(0))
    = 2j_h - length Sha(E^(h)/K)[2^infinity]
      - 2 sum_{p|h} t_p + O_E(1).                       (2)

6. Quadratic comparison without a BSD assumption
-----------------------------------------------
Set
  A_h=Res_{K/Q}(E^(h)_K),       B_h=E^(h) x E^(hk).
After base change to K the sum-and-difference maps give isogenies
phi:A_h->B_h and psi:B_h->A_h with both composites [2].
They are defined over Q. Shapiro identifies Sha(A_h/Q) with
Sha(E^(h)/K). Sha(B_h/Q) is the product of the two rational Sha groups.
All these groups are finite here by the forward theorem and isogeny
invariance of finiteness.

Use the exact Cassels--Tate isogeny identity for the arithmetic
BSD quotient, with compatible dual regulators and Neron measures:
  #Sha(A) Reg(A) Per(A) prod c_p(A)
      / (#A(Q)_tors #A^dual(Q)_tors)
is unchanged by isogeny. This is an unconditional identity when these
Sha groups are finite. It is not an assertion that either quotient
equals an analytic leading coefficient. Milne's proof of I.7.3,
especially identity (7.3.1), supplies this distinction.

Here all noncomponent ratios have bounded 2-adic valuation:
* On each free rank-one rational-point lattice, the isogeny indices
  divide 2 because the compositions are [2]. The same holds for dual
  lattices. Functoriality of the height pairing bounds the regulator
  ratio by fixed powers of 2.
* For rational torsion the kernels lie in the fixed-size geometric
  2-torsion. The cokernels are quotients of torsion modulo 2 and have
  size at most 2^4 on these surfaces. Apply the same argument to duals.
* On Neron Lie lattices, both maps extend by the Neron property and
  their composite is multiplication by 2. At every odd prime they are
  isomorphisms. At 2 their elementary divisors are bounded by those of
  [2] on a rank-two lattice. Archimedean kernel and component indices
  are also bounded in dimension two. Thus the period and differential
  ratios are bounded, uniformly in the number of primes in h or k.

The component ratios must be computed separately:
(a) At a split prime of K, A_h and B_h have the same two local elliptic
    factors. Their component numbers agree. This includes every p|h.
(b) At an odd p|k outside 2N, the curve E^(h) is good. The special fiber
    of its Weil restriction from the ramified quadratic extension is an
    extension of its connected good fiber by a connected additive group.
    Thus c_p(A_h)=1.
    On B_h one factor is good and the other is a ramified quadratic
    twist of a good curve. The latter has unipotent connected reduction.
    Its connected point group is uniquely 2-divisible, so its component
    group's 2-primary part equals its pro-2 point completion.
    Section 2 identifies that completion with E[2]^{Fr_p}, of order
    2^{t_p}. Hence v_2(c_p(B_h))=t_p.
(c) Other good nonsplit primes have both component numbers 1. At 2N
    only the fixed finite local comparisons remain.

Taking valuations in the exact isogeny identity therefore gives
  length Sha(E^(h)/K)[2^infinity]
    = s(h)+s(hk)+sum_{p|k}t_p+O_E(1).                  (3)
The sign of the sum is positive on the left-hand comparison: the
extra ramified-twist component factors occur on B_h. A claim that
Sha kernels and cokernels are killed by 2 alone would not establish
(3); their dimensions need not be bounded.

For omega(k)<=A, the displayed sum is at most 2A. Substitution into
(2) proves
  v_2(u_h(0)) = 2j_h-s(h)-s(hk)-4w(h)+O_{E,A}(1).       (4)
In particular no unrecorded error grows with omega(h).

7. Consequence and remaining work
--------------------------------
The determinant-switch audit supplies nonnegative height-one
valuations away from (2), and a uniform lower bound at (2).
Normality of Lambda then gives
  2^{C(E,A)} u_h in Lambda.
Evaluating at t=0 and using (4) yields
  2j_h >= 4w(h)+s(h)+s(hk)-C'(E,A)
for the known simple-zero instances.

This closes the central comparison needed for the ring-class lower
bound, relative to the explicitly stated classical inputs and the
previous finite/limit audits. It does not establish the unknown
base's rank. That still requires the coefficient and weighted-theta
identities, binary-family construction, and the missing-vertex
argument with all its arithmetic hypotheses. The final ordinary-input
universal equation also still requires the height constants and
polynomial instantiation.

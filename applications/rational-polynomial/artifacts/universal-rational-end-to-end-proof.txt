A fixed rational Diophantine representation of the halting relation
3 November 2026

Statement

Let U be the polynomial defined by the finite expression in
universal-rational-equation.json, SHA256
be7347c439459759121cda9656eda501afd75ca48897aa0f0acf5f85f6a43b1f.
Let N=878664928321. For the effective universal program numbering in
universal-interpreter-spec.txt and all ordinary e,n in {0,1,2,...},

  program e halts on input n
       if and only if
  there exists w in Q^N such that U(e,n,w)=0.                 (U)

The expression defines one member of Z[e,n,w_1,...,w_N]. Its
coefficients and number of variables do not depend on the supplied
program or input. In particular, the inputs in (U) are the ordinary
integers e,n, not externally calculated elliptic-curve coordinates.

This file gives the final composition argument and the precise
dependency audit for the saved expression. It is a written mathematical
proof using the component proofs listed below and their classical
inputs. It is not a Lean proof or a claim that the finite execution
checks prove the arithmetic theorems. The construction uses the
six-contact height estimate, not the original five-contact family.

1. What polynomial the file defines

Only the expression definitions, library node lists and roots, fixed
integer definitions, and recipe constraints define U. Source hashes,
metadata, and the historical status strings do not add conditions.

The elementary scalar nodes are integer constants, variables, sums,
and products. A library call substitutes its scalar parameter
expressions and a private block of witness variables into an earlier
library polynomial. A recipe adds the squares of its listed constraint
polynomials. The remaining three finite constructors are:

* fixed_integer: an integer defined by the fixed prescriptions below;
* psi_component: one component of a fixed division polynomial, obtained
  by the complete normalized recurrences in the expression;
* finite_guard_product: the finite product explicitly specified there.

No constructor tests the halting relation, invokes an integrality
oracle, or has a bound depending on a quantified variable. Library
references go backwards. Recipe dependencies are

  universal -> halting, bridge;
  bridge -> accuracy, raw_trace_ratio, strict_close;
  halting, accuracy -> power_value and library templates;
  power_value -> library templates.

Repeated finite substitution consequently yields an ordinary
polynomial. A rational constant p/q is implemented by a witness r
and qr-p=0, with fixed nonzero integer q. Its defining equation is
conjoined outside branch choices. All scalar polynomial coefficients
are therefore integers. Over Q, a sum of squares is zero exactly
when each summand is zero, and a product is zero exactly when a
factor is zero. These facts justify the Boolean encodings.

2. Termination and order of the fixed prescriptions

The relevant exact code is in fixed-certificate-constants.js,
rational-field-arithmetic.js, finite-polynomial-expressions.js,
elliptic-division-polynomials.js, and rational-model-assembly.js.

Put F=Q(s), s^2=2, d=75-53s, and

  E: y^2=x^3-d^2 x,
  P=((1641-1160s)/8,(-123720+87483s)/32).

The proof in elliptic-generator-proof.txt, with the elementary descent
in explicit-elliptic-data.txt, gives

  E(F)_tors=E[2](F),       2E(F)=Z P,

and P has infinite order. The exact finite search in that proof covers
every candidate in its stated height bound; its search domain is
derived there, not inferred from a list of sampled multiples.

Let P0 be the product of primes at most 324 and W=4P0. For every
unit r modulo W take the first 22 primes greater than 324 in that
progression. There are finitely many such progressions and Dirichlet's
theorem gives infinitely many primes in each. Trial division is a
terminating primality test. Thus the prescribed catalogue, its product
Hproduct, and its maximum Hmax are fixed computable integers. Its primes
are distinct: classes are disjoint, and each class lists distinct primes.

Set Ns=P0*Hproduct. The next prescription is the least m>=1 such that

  1/x([m]P)=(a+b*s)/c,  c>0, gcd(a,b,c)=1,
  Ns^4 divides a and b, and gcd(c,Ns)=1.                (1)

Here is a termination argument with the dyadic place included. The
Weierstrass equation is integral at every finite place. On a sufficiently
small formal subgroup, with z=-x/y, one has 1/x=z^2 times a unit power
series. For an odd p dividing Ns, require v_p(z)>=2 at every place of
F above p. The extension is unramified there, and extraction of the
two rational coefficients uses only the units 2 and s. Both coefficients
of 1/x are then in p^4 Z_p.

At p=2, instead take v_2(z)>=3, using the normalization v_2(2)=1
on F_2. Then 1/x has valuation at least six. Its rational coefficients
are obtained by adding the conjugates and dividing by 2, or subtracting
them and dividing by 2s. Their valuations are at least five and
six-3/2 respectively, and hence at least four. Thus this smaller
formal subgroup also ensures (1) at 2. Each chosen subgroup is open
of finite index in the corresponding compact local elliptic group.
The simultaneous subgroup has finite index in the finite product.
Some positive multiple of P belongs to it.

If both rational coefficients belong to p^4 Z_p, their normalized
common denominator is prime to p and both common numerators are
divisible by p^4. Thus this multiple satisfies the literal test (1).
Every step in the search computes [m]P and its reciprocal coordinate
by exact field arithmetic. Nonzero multiples are neither O nor
two-torsion; in particular x([m]P)!=0. No test is undefined. The
least-m search therefore terminates. Denote its output by M.

For the later local argument it is enough that this selected [M]P
has v_p(z)>=2 at every place over p|Ns. That follows directly from
(1) and 1/x=z^2 times a unit. All multiples of [M]P remain in that
formal subgroup, including at bad and ramified places. The set in
(1) itself need not be asserted to be a subgroup.

Now set

  cstar=4M,  ell=12*P0^2916*Hproduct^732,
  S={p<=324} union {p|Hproduct} union {p|cstar}.

The remaining fixed definitions are, in their dependency order,

  F0=324!, D=F0*3^(256*F0^4), G0=116064000000,
  E0=10*(D*G0)^2, R=max(D,Hmax,cstar,10^12),
  Q=R^(R*E0), Bcm=D^2*3^(21000*D^2)+1,
  Ccm=2*Bcm+4096*(1028+5*D+84000*D^2),
  Aheight=2*10^13*max(1,Ccm,Q^17),
  T=(48*Aheight)^13.

All powers and factorials have fixed nonnegative integer arguments.
R bounds max(S). These expressions have no cyclic dependency.
The selected primes used to witness a particular multiplication
certificate are chosen only afterwards and do not enter the constants.

For each fixed division index, the normalized recurrence refers to
smaller nonnegative indices once the cases 0,...,4 have been supplied.
It uses polynomial operations only. The actual indices are positive
constants and their indicated neighbors; no undefined negative-index
recursion is needed. Division occurs only in computing fixed point
coordinates, where nontorsion of P proves all denominators nonzero.

Finally, [-T,T]^3 is finite. Compute the fifteen rational coordinates
of (iota(a),iota(b),iota(c)) for each triple ab!=c in that box, where
iota(k) is the canonical code of [k]P. If those coordinates are A_j/B_j
in lowest terms with B_j>0 and D0=product B_j, its guard factor is

  sum_j (D0*X_j-(D0/B_j)*A_j)^2.

Every factor has integer coefficients. Their finite product is the
exact guard in the expression. Thus every coefficient prescription
terminates as an algorithm with unbounded integer storage. This does
not assert that a physical JavaScript runtime can store the resulting
astronomical integers, or that the expanded monomial list is practical.

3. The arithmetic inputs supplied to multiplication

The inputs are distinct and have the following exact interfaces.

G: The cyclic-carrier theorem in section 2. Its proof is elementary
descent, torsion reduction, a height bound, and the exhaustive finite
calculation in elliptic-generator-proof.txt.

H: six-contact-height-proof.txt proves, for C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60},

  h(t)<=Aheight*M_S(t)^24,

where M_S(t) is the squarefree product of p outside S with positive
odd v_p(t-c) for some c in C. The proof covers negative valuations
of t and every allowed rational t. Its bounded fine cover, Hom field,
CM and non-CM cases, and theta-height comparison yield exactly the
fixed constants above. height-constant-binding-checks.json binds
the symbolic definitions to the candidate. Pan's theorem is used
at coefficient prime five, not two. The original discriminant-210
five-contact theta comparison is not an input.

L: twenty-two-prime-pattern-proof.txt proves the required simultaneous
linear prime patterns for every integer homogeneous coordinate,
including negative ones. The fixed catalogue is the one in section 2.
At each selected prime the two conjugate reductions of E have orders
d1,d2 with gcd(d1,d2)=4 and p not dividing d1*d2. The theorem permits
arbitrarily large selected primes. It asserts no unneeded gcd relation
between orders belonging to different primes.

RC: restricted-converse-and-pole-completeness-proof.txt proves the
pointwise implication only needed here: for positive squarefree
l=11 modulo 12, full 2-primary Selmer corank one for

  E_l: y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l)

implies analytic rank one. This non-CM family is different from E/F.
The proof first obtains the even lower bound and the varying-companion
ring-class bound, then the odd lower bound, then normalizes the odd
symbol. It fixes the final companion before choosing the binary
dimension. Its elementary split-witness contradiction uses l nonsquare,
which is automatic in the stated range. It does not apply that argument
to l=1 or assume a rank conclusion from finite 2-Selmer dimension alone.

The RC dependency proofs are nonvanishing-audit.txt,
cyclotomic-noncm-audit.txt, coefficient-trace-audit.txt,
weighted-theta-audit.txt, finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt,
limit-interface-audit.txt, determinant-switch-audit.txt,
central-specialization-audit.txt, determinant-transfer-audit.txt,
arithmetic-graph-audit.txt, ordinary-split-witness-audit.txt,
restricted-pointwise-assembly.txt, and
paired-functional-and-uniformity-proof.txt. The primary-source range
checks for Kato and Purkait are recorded separately. None of these
arguments uses the universal polynomial or H, L, or its multiplication
certificate to prove its own premise.

The final audit corrected an intermediate claim in section 6 of the
paired-functional supplement. An exact inverse of an augmented block
does not have a precision-independent Laurent pole bound. Instead use
C_i=adj(A_i)/(t^{d_i}v_i) when det A_i=t^{d_i}v_i+2w_i, d_i<=4. Then
BC=1+2F, and both C and F have poles at most four. Modulo 2^m,

  B^{-1}=C*sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(-2F)^j

has poles at most 4m. This is the argument already proved in
determinant-transfer-audit.txt. It leaves the required clearing bound
L(m)=16r*m unchanged, with 2r+2 the old graded rank. The correction
does not change any coefficient or witness count in U.

PC: The same combined RC proof and pole-descent-completeness-audit.txt
give the literal eleven-witness pole polynomial used by the library:

  B has square reduced denominator,
  0<B<1/2, and v_p(B)>=4 for p in {2,3,5,7,13}
     imply Phi0(B).

The two descent parameters n and n*theta are both squarefree and
11 modulo 12. Thus RC applies to both. The finite Selmer dimension
three gives full Selmer corank one using the alternating Cassels--Tate
pairing modulo the maximal divisible subgroup. RC and the forward
theorem then give Sha[2]=0 and the required rational points. The
non-torsion squareclasses yield the literal inverse guard in Phi0.

Separately, the elementary valuation argument gives

  Phi0(B), B!=0 => no negative odd v_p(B) outside {2,3,5,7,13}.

This direction uses neither RC nor PC. These two different directions
of the pole predicate are used separately in the certificate proof.

4. The actual multiplication template

multiplication-certificate-correctness-proof.txt proves the following
statement for the exact template multiply in the saved expression:

  for A,B,C in Q^5, there are rational multiply witnesses
  if and only if A=iota(a), B=iota(b), C=iota(ab) for some a,b in Z. (M)

The template uses 35134 witnesses. Its height_certificate calls member
on all three root codes. The outer four fixed-scalar calls impose
C0=[cstar]P and Ci=[cstar]Pi. Consequently the test_group preconditions
are consequences of the same conjunction, not assumptions on arbitrary
rational inputs.

The code has eight reciprocal-x tests of precision one and four tests
of precision 25, six independent test groups per slope, and 27 slopes.
Their normalization is exactly

  R0=eta*product_(c in C)(t-c)/(t-1)^8, eta^2=1,
  a=1-R0*q^2, R=ell^2*a^2*R0,
  C_gj=D_gj/[R0^k_j*(t-1)^(k_j+1)],
  W_g=product_j(1+R*C_gj^2),
  Theta=Tr(sum_g 1/W_g), Bpole=R/Theta^2.

All denominators are explicitly guarded or nonzero by the contact
guards. Bpole is nonzero. For any rational zero, the elementary pole
obstruction selects a common group and place giving all twelve local
inequalities at each positive odd contact prime. The full-log versus
truncated-log comparison then gives p^25 | a*b-c for the root indices.
Thus M_S(t)^25 divides a*b-c at every slope. H and the elementary
canonical-height comparison force every false triple inside [-T,T]^3.
Its own factor in the guard vanishes, contradicting the guard inverse.
This proves the forward implication of (M) using only G and H.

For a true triple, L gives all 27 slopes and at most six odd-contact
primes per slope. At such a prime the congruences

  h=0 modulo lcm(M,d1), h=cstar modulo d2

are compatible since gcd(lcm(M,d1),d2) divides 4M=cstar. Choose h>cstar
and use Ui=[a_i]([h]P). All exact log ratios are the required integer
indices. The small formal subgroup from section 2 controls the fixed
exceptional primes. Weak approximation chooses q with the required
local units and with a nonzero but arbitrarily small real a.

The explicit exponents in ell make v_p(Bpole)>=4 at the five primes
of PC. At selected odd-contact primes the twelve trace residues are
zeros and ones, at least one is one, and p>324, so their sum is a unit.
At other primes outside the fixed set the valuation is even. The
denominator of Bpole is therefore a square. Positivity of every real
W_g and the choice of a give 0<Bpole<1/2. These are exactly the premises
of PC, which supplies the pole witnesses. No guard factor vanishes at
a true triple. This proves the reverse implication of (M).

5. The two natural-witness theorems being interpreted

integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt proves the general compiler and
universal-interpreter theorem for the saved natural polynomial H:

  exists v in N^1305802: H(e,n,v)=0
       iff program e halts on n.

The proof includes zero-length runs, arbitrary input and register
sizes, the base-B no-carry invariants, and the complete alpha and
exponentiation formulas from the cited AFP development. It does not
replace any natural witness by a rational witness.

accuracy-correctness-proof.txt proves for the saved polynomial Aacc:

  exists v in N^1305804: Aacc(k,h,j,v)=0
       iff h>=1, j=kh, and |rho(h,j)-k|<1/8,

where rho(h,j)=Tr(z([j]P)/z([h]P))/2. Its exact arithmetic algorithm,
Jacobian formulas, scalar-loop preconditions, primitive lowering, and
specialization into H are included in that proof. The integer code is
fixed, and the two Cantor-pairing equations are literal polynomials.
The theorem is false for h=0 without evaluating an undefined ratio.

These two proofs are independent of (M). Their use inside the
elliptic model is the next, separate step.

6. Interpreting both polynomials with rational witnesses

Under (M), the natural template is exact on arbitrary rational
five-tuples. Its four multiply calls give four integer squares, and
its three additions give their sum. The four-square theorem proves
the converse. Hence it defines precisely iota(N).

The raw_add formulas are exact on canonical input codes. The origin,
inverse, distinct-x and nonzero tangent cases are exhaustive. Its
preconditions are supplied by the preceding natural, constant or
arithmetic nodes. Fixed-scalar graphs are exact on ZP: nonzero
multiples are nontorsion, so the division-polynomial denominator
cannot vanish. Constant nodes are the required multiples of P.

Induction through each source circuit now gives the exact integer
value at every output code. Intermediate values can be negative;
only source natural variables receive natural constraints. A
zero-mode recipe requires its root code to be O. A value-mode recipe
identifies its root with the output parameter instead.

In particular power_value evaluates its 474-node polynomial with
79 private natural witnesses. It does not force every power subcall
to vanish, which would incorrectly constrain unselected Boolean
branches. Every private block is used exactly once and has no parent
variable reference. The rationally extracted natural assignments
therefore combine to one assignment to the original natural circuit;
conversely every natural assignment splits into the prescribed blocks.

It follows that V(A,B), the translated halting recipe, holds precisely
when A=iota(a), B=iota(b) for a,b in N and program a halts on b. The
translated accuracy recipe has precisely the natural-index meaning
in section 5 on arbitrary rational input codes.

The complete induction, including all variable scopes and the output
mode distinction, is in rational-universality-conditional-proof.txt.
Section 4 supplies its sole remaining hypothesis (M).

7. Ordinary integer input parameters

The bridge Gamma(a,A) chooses codes H,J and a rational r and imposes

  accuracy(A,H,J), raw_trace_ratio(H,J,r), |a-r|<1/8.

The last condition is the explicit four-square equation

  (1-64(a-r)^2)*(q1^2+q2^2+q3^2+q4^2)=1.

The accuracy theorem forces A=iota(k), H=iota(h), J=iota(kh), with
k>=0, h>=1 and |r-k|<1/8. H is a nonzero multiple of P, and all
trace-ratio denominators are nonzero. For an ordinary integer a,
the two strict inequalities give |a-k|<1/4, hence a=k.

Conversely fix a=k>=0. In the compact group E(R) times E^sigma(R),
positive multiples of (P,P^sigma) approach the origin. They can have
arbitrarily large indices, since no positive multiple is the origin.
At either embedding, z([k]Q)=k*z(Q)+O(z(Q)^2). The ratio therefore
approaches k at both embeddings, and its half trace does too.
Some h>=1 satisfies |rho(h,kh)-k|<1/8. For k=0 the ratio is zero.
The accuracy and strict-proximity formulas then have witnesses.
Positive rational numbers are sums of four rational squares by
clearing denominators and applying the integer four-square theorem.

Thus for a in N and every A in Q^5,

  Gamma(a,A) holds if and only if A=iota(a).             (2)

No statement that Gamma defines the integer subset of Q is used.
Its behavior at nonintegral a is irrelevant to (U). The recurrence
argument supplies existential witnesses; it is not a limiting
operation inside the polynomial.

8. Both directions of (U) and the witness count

The universal recipe is exactly the sum of the squares of

  V(A,B,v), Gamma(e,A,g_e), Gamma(n,B,g_n),

with ten freely chosen coordinates A,B and disjoint witness blocks.
If it has a rational zero, all three summands vanish. Equation (2)
forces A=iota(e), B=iota(n), and V says that program e halts on n.
Conversely, if the program halts, choose those two codes. V has
witnesses by its translated theorem, and both bridges have witnesses
by section 7. Disjointness permits all choices simultaneously.

The actual allocation is

  power_value: 17786698 witnesses beyond 20 parameters;
  halting:     292887770443 witnesses beyond 10 parameters;
  accuracy:    292888578910 witnesses beyond 15 parameters;
  bridge:      292888578934 witnesses beyond 6 parameters.

Therefore the outer count is

  10+292887770443+2*292888578934=878664928321.

There are N+2=878664928323 total polynomial variables including e,n.
This is an upper bound from this construction, not a minimality claim.

10. Consequences and scope

Equation (U) is universality for ordinary integer program/input
parameters with rational existential witnesses. Any computably
enumerable predicate can be encoded by a fixed program in this
numbering. In particular, the code of a search for a finite ZFC proof
of contradiction can be substituted for e and input zero for n.
The resulting integer-coefficient polynomial has a rational zero
exactly when that proof search halts. This observation does not decide
whether ZFC is consistent. The numerical code of that separate proof
checker has not been produced here.

The construction and written proof are supplied; a proof-assistant
formalization is not. The original five-contact theta calculation is
also not supplied and is not needed by this six-contact construction.
No optimality of the number of variables or degree is claimed.

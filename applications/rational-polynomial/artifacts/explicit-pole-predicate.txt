An explicit polynomial for the pole predicate
2 November 2026

This file fixes the finite multipliers and the smallness constants in
the pole predicate used by linear-prime-certificate.txt. Its elementary
soundness and its polynomial compilation are proved below. Completeness
is now proved at the source-audit level in
pole-descent-completeness-audit.txt, using the restricted positive-rank
dependency chain in restricted-pointwise-assembly.txt. None of these
notes is a Lean formalization.

1. Explicit multipliers

Take

  M = {+d,-d : d is a positive divisor of 2730},
  2730 = 2*3*5*7*13,
  S0 = {2,3,5,7,13},
  epsilon = 1/2,
  b_p = 4 for every p in S0.

There are 64 multipliers. They represent every class in

  R^*/R^{*2} x Q_2^*/Q_2^{*2} x Q_3^*/Q_3^{*2}.

Here is a direct proof. The classes of -1,2,3 control respectively
real sign and the valuation parities at 2 and 3. After those have been
set, 5,7,13 are positive units at both local primes. In coordinates
given by the unit generators -1,5 at 2 and the nonsquare unit at 3,
their bit vectors are

  5  : (0,1,1),
  7  : (1,0,0),
  13 : (0,1,0).

This matrix is invertible over F_2. The last three exponents therefore
set both 2-adic unit bits and the 3-adic unit bit without changing
sign or valuation parities. All six exponents can be chosen in {0,1}.

Consequently, for every nonzero B in Q, one of these multipliers makes
mu B positive, a square in Q_2, and a nonsquare unit square class in Q_3.
"Unit square class" permits a nonzero even 3-adic valuation.

A direct enumeration of all 64 signatures was also performed, with
no duplicates. The signatures are saved in explicit-pole-data.json.

2. One integer-coefficient polynomial

Use eleven rational witness variables

  b0,b1,b2,b3,b4,b5, e,z,r1,r2,w.

The six b variables will be constrained to 0 or 1. Define polynomial
expressions, not additional quantified variables,

  m = (1-2b0)(1+b1)(1+2b2)(1+4b3)(1+6b4)(1+12b5),
  A = m B,
  H = A(A-B^2),
  T1 = A(A-e)(A+3e),
  T2 = A(A z^2-e H)(A z^2+3e H).

Define the single polynomial

  Q_pole(B,b0,...,b5,e,z,r1,r2,w)
    = B * [
        sum_{i=0}^5 (bi^2-bi)^2
        + (e H z w-1)^2
        + (r1^2-T1)^2
        + (r2^2-T2)^2
      ].                                                (1)

All coefficients in (1) are integers. Its total degree is at most 75.
There are eleven existential rational witnesses, independently of B.

Let Phi0(B) mean that (1) has such witnesses. If B=0 it holds. If B is
nonzero, the sum of rational squares must vanish term by term. Thus
m belongs to M and e,H,z are all nonzero. The two square equations give

  (A,r1) on y^2=x(x-e)(x+3e),

and

  (A z^2,z r2) on y^2=x(x-eH)(x+3eH).

Conversely, whenever the two curves have points with nonzero
x-coordinates in the class A Q^{*2}, simultaneous square scaling makes
the first x-coordinate equal to A. Explicitly, if the original first
x is A q^2, replace both curve parameters by division by q^2 and all
point coordinates by (x/q^2,y/q^3). The second parameter remains the
first parameter times H. The second x becomes A z^2 for a nonzero
rational z. Its ordinate divided by z supplies r2. Taking
w=1/(eHz) gives (1). Zero ordinates are allowed.

Thus (1) is exactly the pole predicate with preparation parameter zero
for the chosen explicit multiplier list.

3. Unconditional elementary soundness

Suppose B is nonzero, Phi0(B) holds, and p is outside S0. If
k=v_p(B)<0 were odd, then m would be a p-adic unit, and

  v_p(A)=k,
  v_p(A-B^2)=2k,
  v_p(H)=3k.

The valuations of e and eH have opposite parities. Choose the parameter
l among them with even valuation, and scale by a square power of p so
that the curve parameter is a p-adic unit. This scaling does not change
the x-coordinate square class.

On y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l) with l a unit and p not 2 or 3, every nonzero
x-coordinate has even valuation. For v_p(x)<0, the right-hand side has
valuation 3v_p(x). For v_p(x)>0, it has valuation v_p(x). The latter two
factors are units in that case. Either valuation must be even because
the right-hand side is a square. If v_p(x)=0 the assertion already holds.

But both points furnished by (1) have x square class mB, whose valuation
parity is odd. This is a contradiction. Therefore

  Phi0(B) implies v_p(B) is not negative odd, for every p outside S0.

No pointwise converse or finiteness statement about Sha enters this
soundness proof.

4. Completeness and its arithmetic dependency

The normalized pole construction in Sun, Proposition 2.3, uses a
multiplier list with the local property proved in section 1. Its
smallness choices are

  epsilon = (1/2) min_{mu>0} mu,
  b_p = 3 + max(0,max_mu v_p(mu)).

Our list has positive minimum 1 and maximal valuation 1 at every
p in S0, giving exactly epsilon=1/2 and b_p=4.

Therefore its asserted completeness specializes to

  B has square reduced denominator,
  0 < B < 1/2,
  v_p(B) >= 4 for every p in {2,3,5,7,13}
       implies Phi0(B).                                 (2)

The local preparation for (2) is explicit: the selected m is positive,
and 1-B/m is positive and a square in Q_p for every p in S0. Indeed
v_p(B/m)>=3, which suffices also at p=2. What remains is the rational
point construction on the two elliptic curves, not any choice of these
constants.

Pole-descent-completeness-audit.txt now proves (2) directly for this
polynomial. It checks the local Kummer images, both simultaneous
residue matrices and the preparation H=A^2(1-B/m). The restricted
source-level pointwise argument excludes the divisible-Sha alternative
and supplies the required rational points. This direct proof does not
need the original preparation parameter or a second multiplier list.

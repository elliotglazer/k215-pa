A rational-zero polynomial for ZFC inconsistency, using K215
3 November 2026

Definition

Let e_K be the nonnegative integer printed in k215-program-code.txt.
Its hexadecimal representation is k215-program-code.hex. The integer
has 403,898 binary digits and 121,586 decimal digits. It is not the
integer 215: it encodes the 7,768-instruction register simulation of
the 215-state Turing machine, in the numbering understood by U.

Let U be universal-rational-equation.json, SHA256
 be7347c439459759121cda9656eda501afd75ca48897aa0f0acf5f85f6a43b1f.
Define the single polynomial

             P(w) = U(e_K,0,w),                 (1)

with N=878664928321 rationally quantified coordinates in w. Its
integer coefficients and entire finite expression are specified in
zfc-inconsistency-polynomial.json, SHA256
 2d6b97e7496ab8eb99f8b6e8b2f2e1792bbc8b5142ef73a6270ca6a1982e5a5e.

The latter file contains the complete source expression for U and
the exact decimal values of both substitutions. Thus it is usable
without looking up another polynomial file. Literal substitution
introduces no extra variables and preserves integer coefficients.
As with U, the coefficient prescriptions are finite and terminating;
their expanded decimal values and monomial list are not printed.
The free variables of P are w alone. This is a multivariable polynomial.

Claim and dependencies

Using the written universality theorem for U and the K215 theorem
specified below,

  exists w in Q^N with P(w)=0
      if and only if
  ZFC has a finite formal proof of contradiction.          (2)

The new work here is the explicit register simulation, its integer
encoding, and the literal specialization (1). The arithmetic proof
of U remains the earlier written proof, including its six-contact
height construction and restricted pointwise-converse dependency.
The repository's complete MM0 proof binary was not rerun locally.
Its theorem is an external mathematical input with a checked
specification/receipt match, not a newly kernel-checked result here.

1. Pinned K215 specification and theorem

Repository:
 https://github.com/elliotglazer/k215-pa
Revision:

The machine is the literal table in pa-mm0/k215_literal_tm.mm0,
whose SHA256 is
 175a704301df3887f75e4d7ecbd3b7a15b0423dae39758797d578e751732d4c5.
It has working states 0,...,214 and halt state 215. The tape alphabet
is {0,1}, the blank symbol is zero, and it starts in state 0 on a blank
two-sided tape. Direction 0 means left; direction 1 means right.

The definition encodes a configuration by a triple (q,l,r). The
lowest bit of r is the head symbol; higher bits are symbols further
right. The lowest bit of l is the symbol immediately left of the
head. Beyond the finite binary expansions, all symbols are zero.
For transition (w,d,t), its literal move equations are

 d=0: (q,l,r) -> (t, floor(l/2),
                        l mod 2 + 2w + 4 floor(r/2));
 d=1: (q,l,r) -> (t, 2l+w, floor(r/2)).              (3)

The source's k215LiteralHalts says that some finite iterate from
(0,0,0) reaches state 215. The step function fixes halt configurations.

The endpoint K215_halts_iff_ZFC_inconsistent_final in
pa-mm0/k215_final_thm1.mm0 has no hypotheses and states
the equivalence of k215LiteralHalts and zfcInconsistent. It agrees
with the frozen target theorem in pa-mm0/targets/final.mm0.
The final .mm1 proof source applies the previously proved full
theorem to k215FinalFull_fixed_closed, discharging its old premise.

zfcInconsistent is zfcProvable sfBot. zfcProvable explicitly quantifies
a finite list of ZFC axioms and a finite full natural-deduction proof
of the target sequent. The axiom specification includes the fixed
set-theoretic axioms, Choice, and the Separation and Replacement
schemata with their bound-variable checks. This concerns syntactic
inconsistency, not the truth of a semantic assertion about all models.

The 23 files in the frozen statement import closure were downloaded
at the pinned revision and checked against its SHA256 manifest. The
actual import closure agrees with that manifest. The definition files

The endpoint's binder and formula agree with the frozen target. The

2. Register-machine simulation

The compiled program uses four natural-number registers:
 R0=L, R1=R, R2=T, R3=Z.
At each simulated-state boundary q_i, L and R have the meaning in
(3), and T=Z=0. Z is never incremented. A decrement/test of Z with
both successors equal implements an unconditional jump.

There are two finite macros, emitted literally as increment and
decrement/test instructions by TapeCompiler.

scale(x,k,c) assumes T=0. Its first loop consumes x, incrementing
T exactly k times for each unit consumed. After j iterations,
 x=x_initial-j and T=kj.
It terminates when x=0. Its second loop transfers T back to x one
unit at a time, preserving x+T=k*x_initial, and terminates with
T=0. It then increments x exactly c times. Its result is
x=k*x_initial+c, with T=0 and all other registers unchanged.

pop(x,even,odd) also assumes T=0. It consumes pairs from x, adding
one unit to T per pair. After j pairs, x=x_initial-2j and T=j.
It eventually sees either zero remaining or exactly one remaining.
In the second case it consumes that last unit. On either branch,
x=0 and T=floor(x_initial/2). The selected branch transfers T back
to x one unit at a time and jumps to the specified even or odd
successor. It leaves x=floor(x_initial/2), T=0, and chooses the
successor according to the original x modulo 2.

Both macros terminate for every finite natural input. Their loops
have strictly decreasing natural counters. Their code contains no
halt instruction, and their private control labels are resolved
before encoding. They preserve the boundary invariant.

At q_i, the compiled block first jumps to a private entry and then
applies pop(R,a_i0,a_i1). Keeping q_i distinct from the pop loop
means that no internal loop iteration is mistaken for another
simulated-state entry. The pop selects the source transition on
the head bit and leaves
R=floor(r/2). For a right move it applies scale(L,2,w) and jumps to
q_t, giving exactly the second line of (3). For a left move it
applies scale(R,4,2w), then pop(L,even,odd); the odd branch adds one
to R. It jumps to q_t, giving exactly the first line of (3).

The unique halt instruction is q_215. All other branch targets
are within the emitted program, and every source target is between
0 and 215. Starting with input zero puts L=R=T=Z=0 at q_0.
Induction on source steps therefore proves exact simulation.
Each source step takes finitely many register steps, so a source
halt gives a register halt. Conversely a register halt can only
occur at q_215, reached after a finite number of simulated source
steps; no macro can produce a spurious halt or run forever during
one source step. Thus

 register simulation halts on input zero
       iff K215 halts on its blank tape.                  (4)

The generated register program is k215-register-program.json.
The table used by the compiler is k215-transition-table.json.
A parser consumes every expression in all 22 source table blocks
and evaluates each of the 430 state/symbol cases. An independent
extraction of the explicit branches agrees with that evaluation.

3. Exact numerical encoding

The existing universal-interpreter numbering uses four base-B
digits per instruction, with B=2^w. Here w=13 is large enough for
all register numbers, opcodes and jump targets. An increment has
fields (1,register,next,0), a decrement/test has fields
(2,register,nonzero,zero), and halt has fields (0,0,0,0).

If these fields at instruction i are (o_i,j_i,a_i,b_i), define

 d_i=o_i+B*j_i+B^2*a_i+B^3*b_i,
 D=sum_(i=0)^7767 d_i B^(4i),
 e_K=2^13(2D+1)-1.                                    (5)

Then e_K+1 has 2-adic valuation exactly 13. The universal decoder
recovers D, and its four-digit extraction recovers each instruction
because every field lies in [0,B). Missing high digits are zero,
consistent with the final all-zero halt record. Every actual jump
stays within the explicit list. The verifier decodes all 7,768
records from the printed integer and compares every field.

The universal interpreter's private cleanup on a source halt does
not change its halting behavior. It merely satisfies the all-zero
acceptance convention of the natural polynomial compiler. Combining
its previously proved semantics with (4)-(5),

 program numbered e_K halts on input 0
       iff K215 halts on its blank tape.                  (6)

4. Polynomial specialization and both implications

The existing written universality theorem says, for all ordinary
nonnegative integers e,n,

 exists w in Q^N: U(e,n,w)=0
       iff program numbered e halts on n.

The literal constants in (5) and zero satisfy its parameter domain.
If P has a rational zero, substitution (1), universality, (6) and
the upstream K215 theorem give a finite formal ZFC contradiction.
If a finite formal ZFC contradiction exists, the K215 theorem gives
a finite K215 halt, (6) gives a halt for program e_K on zero, and
universality supplies rational witnesses for U(e_K,0,w)=0. These
are exactly witnesses for P(w)=0. This proves (2) from its stated
mathematical inputs.

5. Verification scope

verify-k215-specialization.js checks source hashes and the statement
import closure, receipt binding, the literal table, fresh register
compilation, every encoded instruction, 21,500 complete transition
executions with both bit values and boundary cases, and 32 consecutive
blank-tape steps against an independent tape-cell simulation.
Those bounded executions do not decide whether K215 eventually halts.
The all-input simulation proof is the decreasing-loop argument above.

The verifier also compares the whole embedded source polynomial to U,
checks its hash, the exact two integer substitutions and the number of
remaining variables. The original U package has a separate integrity
and assembly checker. These checks cannot certify the arithmetic
proof of U or replace kernel verification of the upstream MM0 proof.

This construction is not evidence that ZFC is inconsistent. It gives
one fixed polynomial whose rational solvability has that meaning.
No stronger independence claim is made here.

1. Exact local descent for one parameter
----------------------------------------
Let l>0 be squarefree, coprime to 6, with l=3 modulo 4 and l=2 modulo 3.
Write Q for its prime support and use Kummer coordinates (x+3l,x-l)
on E_l:y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l). The two independent rational torsion classes are

                      tau1=(l,1), tau2=(3,-l).

At an odd local prime the Kummer image has order four. Indeed an open
formal subgroup is uniquely 2-divisible; passing to the finite quotient
preserves both the quotient modulo 2 and the full rational 2-torsion.
For a finite abelian group, the quotient modulo 2 and its 2-torsion
have the same size.

At q not dividing 3l, the roots are distinct modulo q. A nonintegral
x has even valuation by the curve equation; for integral x, at most
one root factor has positive valuation, again even. The local Kummer
image is therefore exactly the four unit squareclass pairs.

At q_i|l, the valuation pairs of tau1,tau2 are (1,0),(0,1), so these
classes generate the full local image. At 3, l is a nonsquare unit
and -l a square. The local image is all first-coordinate squareclasses
with square second coordinate.

At 2 only a necessary condition is used: both coordinate valuations
are even. Negative v_2(x) is even by the product equation. For integral
even x, the two selected coordinates are units. If x-l=2u with u odd,
the equation would give

             (y/2)^2=(l+2u)u(u+2l)=l modulo 8,

which is impossible for l=3 or 7 modulo 8. Otherwise both x-l and x+3l
have valuation at least two. Their difference is 4l, so the minimum
valuation is two, and their sum of valuations is even. Both are even.
The torsion replacement values have the same property.

At infinity the first Kummer coordinate is positive. The sign of
the second coordinate is a surjective character, because tau2 has
negative second coordinate. Its kernel has index two. Every class
in this positive kernel has the unique squareclass representative

                   (3^delta product q_i^u_i, product q_i^v_i),

with delta in F_2 and u,v in F_2^Q.

2. The matrix criterion and the exact dimension
-----------------------------------------------
Put s_i=[-1/q_i], lambda_i=[3/q_i], with square=0 and nonsquare=1, and

       (Ku)_i=sum_(j!=i)[q_j/q_i](u_j+u_i),   N=K+D_s.

Quadratic reciprocity gives K+K^t=ss^t+D_s. The congruences on l imply
s^t*1=1 and lambda^t*1=0. Hence

                    K1=K^t1=0,  N=K^t+ss^t.

Matching the local torsion class tau1^u_i tau2^v_i at q_i gives

                 Ku+D_lambda v=delta*lambda,   Nv=0.

The second coordinate condition at 3 is (lambda+s)^t v=0.

Assume a nonempty proper B_set subset Q satisfies

         ker K=span{1,1_B_set},   lambda not in image K.        (1)

Then ker K^t has dimension two and contains 1. Multiplying Nv=0
by 1^t first shows s^t v=0. Thus

                     ker N=ker K^t intersect s-perp

is a line. Since lambda not in image K, lambda is nonzero on ker K^t.
It vanishes on 1, whereas s does not. Therefore lambda is nonzero
on the complementary line ker N. The condition at 3 forces v=0.
Now Ku=delta*lambda and (1) force delta=0 and u in ker K.

There are at most four positive-kernel classes, hence at most eight
Selmer classes in total.

Let beta=product_(q in B_set) q. Assume beta is positive and a square
at 2, a nonsquare unit class at 3, and K1_B_set=0. Then (beta,1) is
an actual local class:
* identity at 2 and infinity;
* tau1 at 3;
* tau1 to the indicator exponent at q_i|l, by K1_B_set=0;
* a unit pair at every remaining odd prime.
It is neither 1 nor tau1, because B_set is nonempty and proper.
It is neither tau2 nor tau1*tau2, whose second coordinates are negative.
It supplies the third independent Selmer direction. Consequently

                     dim_F2 Sel_2(E_l/Q)=3.                    (2)

3. From finite Selmer dimension to a rational point
---------------------------------------------------
Equation (2) and full rational 2-torsion give

                 rank E_l(Q)+dim_F2 Sha(E_l/Q)[2]=1.

Write the 2-primary Sha group as (Q_2/Z_2)^t plus a finite group F.
The Cassels--Tate pairing modulo the maximal divisible subgroup is
nondegenerate and alternating; for an elliptic curve the polarization
is represented by the rational divisor (O). Therefore dim F[2] is even.
It follows that F=0 and rank+t=1. In particular the FULL 2-primary
Selmer corank is exactly one.

The restricted pointwise argument applies and gives rank E_l(Q)=1.
The preceding Kummer identity then implies Sha[2]=0. The class
(beta,1) is therefore represented by a rational point, and its
distinction from all rational two-torsion classes excludes the origin
and all three roots. The curve equation gives

                 [x]=[x+3l][x-l]=beta.

Thus the point has nonzero x in beta*Q^{*2}. Neither the finite
Selmer calculation alone nor a Mordell--Weil rank hypothesis was
substituted for the full corank argument.

4. One prime support works for two twists
----------------------------------------
Suppose beta,theta are positive squarefree odd integers, their supports
B_set,J are disjoint and avoid 2,3, and

 theta is a local square at every q in B_set,
 beta is a local square at every q in J.

Suppose both are squares at 2, beta is a nonsquare unit class at 3,
and theta is a square there. Then

 sum_B s=sum_J s=0,  sum_B lambda=1,  sum_J lambda=0,

and each row sum of the fixed cross-symbol blocks B_set--J is zero.

Add a prime q0 with (s0,lambda0)=(1,1), and a set I of |B_set|-1 primes
with type (0,0). These types are realizable by 7 and 1 modulo 12.
Let Q0=B_set union I union {q0}, and Q1=Q0 union J. Both have total
s=1 and total lambda=0, so their prime products are 11 modulo 12.

Choose b_* in B_set and let a be the vector supported at q0 and b_*.
Then a^t1=0 and a^t1_B_set=1. If K1_B_set=0, put

                 Ksharp=K+lambda*a^t,  T=Id-1_B_set*a^t.

The map T projects onto a-perp, kills 1_B_set, and fixes 1. Moreover
Ksharp1=0 on both sides, Ksharp1_B_set=lambda, and K=Ksharp*T.

If ker Ksharp=span{1}, then (1) follows:
Kx=0 forces Tx=c1 and x in span{1,1_B_set};
Kx=lambda would force Tx-1_B_set in span{1}, contradicting a^tTx=0.
It suffices to make the matrix obtained by deleting q0 from Ksharp
nonsingular. Subtracting the q0 coordinate times 1 then proves that
its full kernel is span{1}.

5. Constructing both nonsingular deleted matrices
------------------------------------------------
Let C be a B_set-by-I matrix whose columns form a basis of the
even-sum hyperplane of F_2^B_set. For |B_set|=1, C is empty.
Prescribe the B_set--I symbols to be C in both directions and every
other off-diagonal symbol incident with I to be zero. Prescribe

                 [q0/q_b]=sum_(i in I) C_bi,

and take the reverse symbols by reciprocity. Leave q0--J free.

These choices give K1_B_set=0 on both Q0 and Q1:
the B_set row sums from I and q0 cancel; C has even column sums;
sum_B s=0 handles q0; and the fixed cross-row sums handle J.

The smaller deleted matrix is

                    M0 = [ A   C  ]
                         [ C^t 0  ],

where 1_B_set^t A1_B_set=1. No symmetry of A is needed.
If M0(x,y)=0, then C^t x=0, so x=c1_B_set. Multiply the first block
equation by 1_B_set^t to obtain c=0, and then injectivity of C gives
y=0. This includes even |B_set| and the case |B_set|=1, where M0=(1).

On adjoining J, the B_set,I block remains M0 because the old-to-J
row sums vanish. Changing one bit [q0/q_j] changes its reverse by
the same bit; after q0 is deleted, only the jth diagonal is toggled.
Thus the free q0--J bits independently vary the J diagonal.

The enlarged determinant is a multilinear polynomial in these bits.
Its full-product coefficient is det M0=1. The squarefree monomials
are linearly independent as Boolean functions, so this polynomial
is nonzero at some bit assignment. If J is empty there is nothing
further to choose. Both deleted matrices are now nonsingular.

Finally choose the auxiliary primes successively by CRT and Dirichlet.
A nonzero residue at each old prime imposes its required symbol; the
reverse symbols were already made consistent by reciprocity. The
modulo-12 type imposes no conflict, and finitely many used primes can
be avoided. The resulting positive squarefree

                     n=product_(q in Q0) q

is coprime to 6theta. Both l=n and l=n*theta satisfy (1), contain the
nonempty B_set, and contain the additional q0. Sections 1-3 provide
the required rational points on BOTH curves.

6. Applying this directly to Phi0
---------------------------------
The explicit predicate uses M={+d,-d: d divides 2730}, S0={2,3,5,7,13}.
Its 64 elements represent all simultaneous real, 2-adic and 3-adic
squareclasses, as proved in explicit-pole-predicate.txt.

Take rational B with square reduced denominator, 0<B<1/2, and
v_p(B)>=4 for p in S0. Choose m in M so that A=mB is positive,
a square at 2, and a nonsquare unit squareclass at 3.
Since B>0, this m is positive and m>=1. Put

                         H=A(A-B^2)=A^2(1-B/m).

At infinity H>0. At p in S0, v_p(B/m)>=3, since v_p(m)<=1.
Thus 1-B/m is a local square (including p=2), and H is a local square.

Let beta and theta be the positive squarefree representatives of A
and H. Their supports avoid 2,3. Beta is a square at 2 and a nonsquare
unit class at 3; theta is a square at both.

If q divides beta and q is in S0, H is already a local square.
If q is outside S0, v_q(A)=v_q(B) is odd. A square reduced denominator
excludes negative odd valuations, so v_q(B)>0. Then
1-B/m is in 1+qZ_q and is a square. Again H is a local square.
Consequently the supports of beta and theta are disjoint.

Conversely let q divide theta. It is outside S0, and v_q(A) is even.
Since m is a unit there, write alpha=v_q(B)=v_q(A).
If alpha>0, 1-B/m is a square unit and v_q(H)=2alpha is even.
If alpha<0, v_q(1-B/m)=alpha and v_q(H)=3alpha is even.
Both contradict q|theta, so alpha=0. For v_q(H) to be odd, cancellation
forces B/m to lie in 1+qZ_q. Hence A=m^2(B/m) is a local square.
Thus beta is a square at q.

All hypotheses of sections 4-5 now hold. Obtain n and points on
E_n and E_(n*theta) with x-coordinate class beta=[A].
Write H=theta*c^2. The square-scaling isomorphism
(x,y)->(c^2x,c^3y) gives the second point on E_(nH) with the same class.

Let the first point have x=A*q^2, q!=0. Divide both curve parameters
by q^2 and their point coordinates by (q^2,q^3).
The first new parameter is e=n/q^2, its point has x=A, and the
second parameter is eH, with point x=A*z^2 for some nonzero z.
Their ordinates provide the two square equations

       r1^2=A(A-e)(A+3e),
       r2^2=A(A*z^2-eH)(A*z^2+3eH).

Here r2 is the second ordinate divided by z. Since e,H,z are nonzero,
take w=1/(eHz). Together with the six Boolean bits representing m,
these are exactly the eleven witnesses of Q_pole(B,...)=0.

This proves the required Phi0 completeness implication. It uses the
direct H=A(A-B^2) preparation and does not import the original paper's
extra rational parameter or a second multiplier list.

7. Consequence for the universal-equation construction
-----------------------------------------------------
The explicit eleven-witness predicate now has both:
* elementary exclusion of negative odd valuations outside S0;
* completeness for the small, sufficiently S0-divisible rational inputs
  with square reduced denominator used in the linear-prime certificate.

The first property is unconditional elementary algebra. The second is
supported by the source-level restricted pointwise dependency audit and
the classical theorems listed there. No additional unproved arithmetic
conjecture has been introduced in passing from that implication to
Phi0. There is still no Lean proof or instantiated universal polynomial.

The remaining tasks are the explicit five-contact height constant and
exceptional-set bookkeeping, the resulting numerical finite guard,
and compilation of the fixed model and ordinary-input bridge into U.

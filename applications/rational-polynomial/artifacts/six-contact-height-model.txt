An explicit six-contact height model
2 November 2026

Result

There is an alternative to the five-contact discriminant-210 family
which gives an explicit base-height constant. Put
 F(u)=u(u+15)(u+20)(u+24)(u+30)(u+60),
 G(u)=7u^2+300u+3600,
 J(u)=-57600 F(u)^2/G(u)^6.                              (1)

The discriminant-six quaternionic family pulled back by J has a
fine cover of degree at most 324. Its only ramification over the
u-line has index two, at the six rational contacts
                     0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60.                (2)
For a rational u outside (2) and a principally polarized fiber A
of this cover, the base-height comparison is
                  h(u) <= 10^13 max(1,h_F(A)).              (3)

Here h_F is the original stable Faltings height. The geometric and
height assertions are consolidated and reviewed in
six-contact-height-proof.txt. The candidate universal equation now
uses this six-contact construction, but its remaining arithmetic
dependencies and general compiler proof are not yet complete.

1. The published discriminant-six family

Baba--Granath construct the quaternionic curve for discriminant six.
Its maximal normalizer quotient is a rational curve with coordinate j.
The three orbifold points are
 j=0, infinity, -16/27,
of orders 4,6,2 respectively. The underlying norm-one curve has
two points of order two and two points of order three; its
Atkin--Lehner group has order four. See Section 3, including the
table in Section 3.1 and Proposition 10.

Primary source:
 https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/serien/e/mpi_mathematik/2005/18.pdf

Their moduli parametrization, also recorded in Lin--Yang, is
 [J2,J4,J6,J10]
 =[12(j+1),6(j^2+j+1),4(j^3-2j^2+1),j^3].                (4)
These are weighted projective Igusa invariants, not the
Igusa--Clebsch invariants under another normalization.

Lin--Yang, introduction, equation (2), and equation (14):
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00466

The j=0 and infinity fibers are the decomposable principally
polarized surfaces. The abelian surfaces exist there even though
a smooth genus-two curve with its canonical polarization does not.

2. A degree-twelve pullback with rational order-two contacts

For a preliminary coordinate t, put
 lambda=(2t+1)/(1-t^2),
 y=(t^2+t+1)/(1-t^2).
Then y^2=lambda^2-lambda+1, and
 j=-4 lambda^2(1-lambda)^2/(lambda^2-lambda+1)^3
  =-4 t^2(t+2)^2(2t+1)^2(1-t^2)^2/(t^2+t+1)^6.

Set u=60/(t-2). Substitution gives exactly (1).
This is a degree-twelve map of projective lines.

For a direct ramification check define
 H(u)=(u^2-60u-1800)(13u^2+480u+3600)
                    (11u^2+600u+7200).
Integer polynomial arithmetic gives
                    G(u)^6-97200 F(u)^2=H(u)^2.            (5)
Consequently
                    J(u)+16/27=16 H(u)^2/(27G(u)^6).

The six simple roots of F lie above j=0 with ramification index two.
The two simple roots of G lie above infinity with index six.
The six simple roots of H lie above -16/27 with index two.
The three fibers are disjoint. The discriminants of the three
quadratic factors of H are 10800,43200,43200; that of G is -10800.
Their pairwise coprimality can also be checked directly from the
displayed polynomials. The ramification contributions are
 6*(2-1)+2*(6-1)+6*(2-1)=22=2*12-2.
Thus there is no further ramification.

Pulling back a tame orbifold cover changes a local index e to
e/gcd(e,a), where a is the ramification index of the base change.
Here the residual indices are
 4/gcd(4,2)=2,
 6/gcd(6,6)=1,
 2/gcd(2,2)=1.
This proves precisely the six residual contacts in (2).

3. An explicit fine level and degree bound

Use B=(2,-3), with i^2=2, j^2=-3, ij=-ji. Baba--Granath give the
maximal order
 O=Z[i,(1+j)/2]
and the pure quaternion mu=2j+ij, with mu^2=-6.

On the lattice O use psi(x,y)=Trd(mu^(-1)*x*bar(y)), changing its
sign if necessary for positivity on the chosen half-plane.
In the basis
 1, i, (1+j)/2, (i+ij)/2,
the Gram matrix is
 [ 0, 0,-1, 1]
 [ 0, 0, 1,-2]
 [ 1,-1, 0, 0]
 [-1, 2, 0, 0].
Its Pfaffian is -1 and its determinant is one. The lattice is
therefore integral and self-dual.

Right multiplication preserves this lattice, and has similitude
Nrd(g). The kernel K(3) of O_hat^* -> (O/3O)^* maps into the
principal Siegel level-three group. It supplies a fine family of
principally polarized abelian surfaces. The rational B-action and
the polarized moduli class are those of (4); there is only one
component of the discriminant-six quaternionic locus.

Since |O/3O|=3^4=81, the total level degree, including geometric
components, is at most 81. The normalizer quotient has degree four,
so the fine cover of the j-line has degree at most
                           n=324.
Normalization after the degree-twelve base change does not multiply
this degree: the cover's degree over the new base is still at most
324. The family pulls back to its normalization.

The fine cover has no orbifold stabilizers; its ramification over j
is exactly the orbifold indices used in Section 2. All fibers above
a fixed u have the same j-moduli point and are geometrically
isomorphic as principally polarized surfaces. In particular they
are geometrically isogenous, as required for the representation
and height argument.

4. Geometric exceptional primes

A conservative sufficient geometric exceptional set is
                    Sgeom={primes p<=324}.

At p>324, the order and pairing are unramified and self-dual,
K(3)_p is hyperspecial, and the Hodge-type integral canonical
model is smooth. The family is proper: the division-quaternion
action rules out a positive toric rank after semistable reduction,
and full prime-to-p level three kills the remaining finite inertia
action. This is the same valuative argument written in
explicit-geometric-exceptions.txt.

The level and Atkin--Lehner quotient actions have order at most 324
and are tame at p>324. Their branch sections over j stay separated.
For the degree-twelve rational map, the explicit factorizations
(1) and (5) give the same three separable, disjoint fibers in every
such residue characteristic. One can check disjointness directly:
G at the six roots of F is respectively
 3600,675,400,432,900,10800,
all supported only on 2,3,5. The discriminants and pairwise
resultants of the displayed quadratic factors are also supported
on 2,3,5. Homogeneous coordinates include possible roots at infinity.

The tame local normalization of z^e=v^a times a unit is smooth
after unramified base extension and has residual index
e/gcd(e,a). Thus the normalized pullback has good reduction and
exactly the six separated order-two contacts outside Sgeom.
Its local equation at each contact is u-u_i=z^2 after unramified
extension. Even contact gives unramified specialization, and the
pulled-back abelian scheme gives good reduction over that field.

This conservative set is sufficient; no claim of minimality is made.

5. A degree-forty theta formula for the base moduli coordinate

With the invariant normalization of (4), Lin--Yang equation (14)
gives
 J4=(J2^2-psi4)/24,   J10=-4 chi10.
Hence on the family
 psi4=144j,  chi10=-j^3/4,
and away from j=0,infinity,
                 j=16*144^5*chi10^2/psi4^5.                (6)

Let z_m be the ten even theta constants. Igusa's normalized formulas are
 psi4=(1/4) sum_m z_m^8,
 chi10=-2^(-14) product_m z_m^2.
A primary source reproducing these formulas is Lauter--Naehrig--Yang,
Section 2.2:
 https://people.math.wisc.edu/~tonghaiyang/2015-LNYFinal-JNT.pdf

Substitution into (6) gives
 j=3779136 * (product_m z_m)^4 / (sum_m z_m^8)^5.           (7)
Both polynomials are homogeneous of degree forty. Their coefficient
l1 norms are respectively 3779136 and 10^5. Thus ordinary
projective-height functoriality gives
                    h(j)<=40 h([z_m])+22,                  (8)
using 3779136<2^22 and log 2<1.

There is no indeterminacy for the rational base points considered:
G has negative discriminant, so J(u) is finite for rational u.
Excluding the roots of F makes J(u) nonzero. Formula (4) then
makes chi10 and psi4 nonzero.

6. Matching the theta height without an unknown basis constant

Use r=2 in Pazuki's construction. Start analytically with the
standard section theta(tau,z), and pull it back by multiplication
by two. For a quarter-period x=(m+tau*l)/4, its translated section
with the theta-group lift is
 exp(pi*i*l^t*tau*l/4+2*pi*i*l^t*z)
             theta(tau,2z+(m+tau*l)/2).
It equals theta[l/2,m/2](tau,2z) up to a fourth root of unity.

The compositions of these lifts have fourth-root-of-unity
multipliers; on half-periods they agree with the canonical
pullback lifts. They are therefore good choices in the sense of
Pazuki Section 2.3.1. Other good choices change individual
coordinates only by bounded-order roots of unity, a permutation,
and a common scalar. These operations preserve projective height.

At the zero section, the sixteen half-characteristic coordinates
include the ten even constants and six zero odd constants. The
ordinary projective height of this vector is at most its
Fubini--Study height. Therefore
                       h([z_m]) <= h_Theta(A).              (9)
No unknown Fourier or model-comparison constant is used in (9).

Pazuki Sections 2.3.1--2.3.4 and Corollary 1.3:
 https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.2623.pdf

As detailed in base-height-certificate.txt, the corollary at r=2,
including the normalization h_F^D=h_F+log pi, gives
 h_Theta(A) <= 10000*2^24*max(1,h_F(A)).                    (10)

7. Recovering u and completing the numerical comparison

Write J=P/Q with P=-57600F^2 and Q=G^6. Exact integer arithmetic gives
 ||P||_1=14533295809536000000,
 ||Q||_1=3556808369970021551449.
Both are less than 4000^6=4096000000000000000000.

For a fixed finite value j, the polynomial P(X)-jQ(X) is not the
zero polynomial and has u as a root. Its coefficient-vector
height is at most h(j)+log(2*4000^6). The elementary root-height
bound gives
 h(u)<=h(j)+log(4*4000^6)<h(j)+74,
since 4000<2^12 and log 2<1.

Together with (8)--(10), this gives
 h(u) <= 40*10000*2^24*max(1,h_F(A))+96
      <= 6710886400096*max(1,h_F(A))
      < 10^13*max(1,h_F(A)).
This proves (3).

8. Consequence for a fully quantitative six-contact estimate

The representation, conductor and modular-isogeny arguments apply
to any fixed indefinite division-quaternion family with these
properties. The discriminant need not be 210. Use n=324 in the
Hom-field bound:
 D=324!*3^(256*(324!)^4).
For a finite S containing Sgeom, put
 G0=116064000000,
 E0=10*(D*G0)^2,
 R=max(D,max(S),10^12),
 Q0=R^(R*E0).

Let C_CM(D) be the explicit surface bound in
effective-cm-height-bound.txt. The calculations in
explicit-noncm-height-bound.txt then give the valid choice
 H6(S)=2*10^13*max(1,C_CM(D),Q0^17)
for
                  h(u)<=H6(S)*M_S(u)^24,                   (11)
where M_S counts primes of positive odd contact with the six
points (2). The harmless factor two covers either recorded
base-height normalization.

This supplies the explicit alternative height input now used in the
candidate universal equation. The six-contact certificate, fixed
catalogue, finite guard and ordinary-integer-input polynomial have
been assembled. The restricted pointwise-converse dependency,
certificate completeness and general compiler proof still require
their final mathematical review.

The independent validation of the original five-contact argument
remains recorded in the earlier files. Equation (11) is a different
height model, not a claimed computation of its missing theta map.

Scope
-----
This note checks co:even and co:minimum through co:symbol-interface in

1. Even coefficient comparison and rational phases
--------------------------------------------------
Primary-source scope check (3 November 2026): see
restricted-converse-primary-input-checks.txt. Purkait Corollary 5.2
has no (H2) requirement, but retains (H1); the nonzero eigenspace
construction discharges (H1) through Theorem 5. That construction
and its character and level therefore remain necessary dependencies.

Fix an even-sign unit-squareclass filter, a nonvanishing squarefree
index D_0, and the associated Shimura packet for E^(-sigma). A positive
Fourier index D tests E^(sigma D). The packet member with nonzero
D_0 functional exists by the classical Shimura--Waldspurger theory.
At unramified primes use its spherical vector; the unramified
squarefree local factor is nonzero at valuations zero and one.

At a support prime start with a vector whose D_0 functional is nonzero.
Project under a sufficiently large compact upper-unipotent subgroup
to additive frequency D_0. For a unit translate u, the resulting
functional vanishes unless u^2 is sufficiently close to 1. These
units lie in small neighborhoods of 1 and -1. After removing the
fixed metaplectic Weil factor, choose a finite unit character that
agrees with the forced action at -1 and is trivial sufficiently
deeply. It can have 2-power order, because the 2-primary unit quotient
detects -1. The two neighborhoods then contribute with the same
sign to the unit-character projection, so nonvanishing is preserved.
Smoothness supplies deep lower-unipotent invariance. This constructs
a scalar-character vector at a level supported on the fixed support,
including arbitrary required depth at 2.

The squareclass and coprimality projections are finite coefficient
sieves. They commute with good T(p^2), since multiplying an index by
p^2 preserves their conditions. The resulting good recurrence is
  a(p^2 n)=[eta(p)a_p-psi(p)(-n/p)]a(n)
                    -p eta(p)^2 a(n/p^2),                         (1)
with psi/eta=chi_(-sigma) and both characters of 2-power order.

Use the full good-Hecke eigenspace V at this level, orthogonal to
unary theta series, not only one preferred newvector. For D/D_0 a
square at every support prime, Waldspurger's same-local-class
comparison says for every f in V that
  a_f(D)^2 = c_D a_f(D_0)^2,
  c_D=psi(D/D_0) L_*(sigma D)/L_*(sigma D_0).                       (2)
This statement is also printed as Corollary 5.2 of Purkait, with no
(H2) hypothesis and with an explicit note allowing arbitrary f in the
eigenspace. Thus an (H2) restriction in a different version of the
global coefficient formula must not be imported here.

Over C, the polynomial identity
  (ell_D-sqrt(c_D)ell_0)(ell_D+sqrt(c_D)ell_0)=0
in the two linear functionals on V implies that one factor is zero,
because the polynomial ring is a domain. Hence ell_D is a single
scalar multiple of ell_0 on all of V. If c_D=0, ell_D=0. This proves
the needed proportionality even in old multiplicity spaces.

Choose an algebraic form in the filtered eigenspace with a(D_0) nonzero.
The half-integral-weight space has an algebraic model; multiplication
by theta and the algebraic cusp conditions give one description.
The character, sieve and Hecke conditions are algebraic linear
conditions. Nonvanishing of a linear functional after complex scalar
extension implies nonvanishing on an algebraic vector.
Only flat field extension from Qbar to C is used here. This does not
invoke integral base change in weight 3/2, which is not supplied by
Ramsey's high-weight base-change theorem. Integrality later in this
note is proved directly from Fourier coefficients.

Normalize a(D_0)=1. Let u be an automorphism of Qbar acting on the
character values by an odd power e, and put nu=eta^((e-1)/2).
The forms g^u and g twisted by nu have the same scalar character,
because psi^e=psi nu^2, and the same good eigenvalues, because
eta^e=eta nu^2. Put them at a common level supported on the same
primes. Proportionality in that common eigenspace gives
  u(xi)=nu(D/D_0) xi,        xi=a(D)/a(D_0).
Choose local square roots of D/D_0 at the conductor of eta and let
z be their eta-value. Then
  eta(D/D_0)=z^2,   u(z)=z^e,   nu(D/D_0)=z^(e-1).
Thus xi/z is fixed by every algebraic automorphism and lies in Q.
The quadratic ratio psi/eta is trivial on D/D_0, so (2) becomes
  (xi/z)^2=L_*(sigma D)/L_*(sigma D_0).                             (3)
The phase z is a 2-power root of unity and has valuation zero.

Consequently squarefree coefficient valuations are integers, and
  2v(a(D)/a(D_0))=v(L_*(sigma D)/L_*(sigma D_0)).
Vanishing is equivalent on both sides. No new-prime factor is missing:
the squarefree comparison includes local valuations zero and one.
The exact classical packet and comparison theorems are inputs; this
argument checks their use and the subsequent rational-phase deduction.

2. Minimum depth and the order of finite-precision choices
---------------------------------------------------------
Assume the source's even arithmetic lower bound. For the odd source,
assume the separately proved bound
  j(P_D)>=w(D)+s(D)/2-C.
The latter, together with odd detection, implies at an actual stage M
  v(a_M(D))>=min{M,w(D)+s(D)/2-C'}
at simple zeros, and a_M(D)=0 modulo 2^M at the other parameters.

After phase removal the visible coefficients are rational integers
or rational 2-adic numbers. Their finite valuations are integers.
The parity of the number of simple primes is fixed by the filter,
so w(D) has a fixed fractional part. Thus normalized visible depths
v(a(D))-w(D) belong to one discrete translate of Z and are bounded
below. A fixed witness gives an upper bound.

For the even source an attained minimum delta follows immediately.
For the odd source fix one ultrafilter on the increasing stages and
take the least depth attained on a large set by some bounded-weight
sequence of visible indices. Such depths form a nonempty, bounded
below discrete set, so a least one exists and is attained by a sequence.

Fix W. If the divisibility at that minimum failed for some index of
weight at most W on a large set of stages, choose one such violating
index at each stage. Its depths take only finitely many values below
delta, by the common lower bound. One value occurs on a large set and
contradicts minimality. Thus the divisibility holds for every index
of weight at most W at sufficiently advanced stages on the same
ultrafilter. No intersection over all W is required.

To prove an all-coefficient statement after a bounded normalization,
fix the desired residual precision R and maximum normalization n_max.
For weights above a sufficiently large threshold, the coarser bound
min{M,w(D)-C'} already pays n_max+R. Below the threshold use the
stabilized minimum, then choose M large enough. This gives the global
truncated estimate at one actual stage. It does not assert an
unbounded valuation estimate for an arbitrary finite-precision lift.

At a minimum unit, (3) gives v(L_*)<=2w(D)+O(1) in the even case.
Odd detection gives j(P_D)<=w(D)+O(1) in the odd case. The constants
come from fixed delta and fixed comparisons, not from the weight.

3. Difference operators and unary subtraction
---------------------------------------------
For a squarefree prime set U define
  n_U=ceil(delta+w(U)),       e_U=n_U-delta-w(U),
  B_U=2^(-n_U) product_{p in U}(U_p-eta(p)V_p)g.
Matching parity means e_U=0; opposite parity means e_U=1/2.
Each new level prime has exponent one. Its new character is
psi_U=psi product_{p in U}(p/ .).

Remove the eta phases in (1). The square-shell multipliers satisfy
  b_0=1, b_1=a_p-kappa, b_(s+1)=a_p b_s-p b_(s-1),
  kappa=(psi/eta)(p)(-D/p).
All good a_p are even in the rational-two-torsion range.

If p divides D, kappa=0 and b_s alternates 1,0 modulo 2.
The relevant neighboring-shell difference is therefore odd.
If p does not divide D, kappa is +1 or -1 and every b_s is odd;
the relevant difference is even.

For n=J r m^2, with J a subset of U and r squarefree coprime to U,
the source squarefree index is D=(U/J)r. It must be on the filter.
The preceding factors give
  v(B_U[n]) >= delta+w(D)+#J-n_U
            = w(r)+(1/2)#(simple primes in J)-e_U.                 (4)
The right side is an integer on the allowed filter. Its only possible
negative value without that parity condition would be -1/2. It is
therefore nonnegative. The preceding stage argument gives (4) truncated
at any prescribed R for the odd source, simultaneously for all U of
bounded weight and all Fourier indices.

For split p, a_p=p+1 modulo 4. Put Delta_s=b_(s+1)-b_s when p does
not divide D. Every Delta_s is even, and its recurrence gives
Delta_(s+1)=Delta_(s-1) modulo 4. Direct subtraction gives
  Delta_1-Delta_0
    =a_p^2-a_p kappa-2a_p+2kappa+1-p=0 modulo 4.
Consequently every Delta_s/2 has residue
  c_p(D)=(a_p-kappa-1)/2 modulo 2.                                (5)
For simple p the first-shell version alone is used. The four cases
p=1,3 modulo 4 and kappa=+1,-1 verify the displayed congruence.

In matching parity, the right side of (4) can be zero only for r=1
and split-only J. On such a squareclass, the same recurrences give
  B_U[J m^2]=B_U[J] eta(m) modulo 2.
Choose i in {0,1} with eta chi_(-4)^i odd and set
  Theta_J=sum_{m>0,(m,S)=1} eta(m)chi_(-4)(m)^i m q^(Jm^2).
For odd m, chi_(-4)(m)^i m is 1 modulo 2. Different squarefree J have
disjoint support. Hence
  H_U=(B_U-sum_{J subset U, split-only}B_U[J]Theta_J)/2              (6)
has integral coefficients. The divisibility is by the integer 2 in O,
not merely by the maximal ideal of a ramified coefficient ring.
In opposite parity set H_U=B_U.

The unary terms have character eta chi_(-4)^i(-J/ .) and new level
primes of exponent one. They need not all have the same character
as B_U. Their character ratios with psi_U are quadratic; this point
is used explicitly in the next step.

4. Integral traces despite the division in (6)
----------------------------------------------
Multiply by theta=1+2 sum_{m>0}q^(m^2) to obtain weight two with the
same reduced expansion. Take the finite-dimensional span of its
characteristic-zero good-Hecke eigensystems, including old multiplicity
spaces and diamond translates. Cuspidal constituents have the usual
modular Galois representations; Eisenstein constituents have the sum
of their two characters. Let t(u),d(u) be their commuting trace and
determinant operators.

Use the O-module generated by the weight-two vector, the undivided
unary terms, and all good-Hecke and diamond translates. Diamonds
preserve integrality because
  <a>H_U=psi_U(a)H_U
    +(1/2)sum_J B_U[J](psi_U(a)-eta(a)chi_(-4)(a)^i(-J/a))Theta_J.
Each character difference is zero or twice a unit. Good Hecke
operators preserve integral expansions. Finitely many Fourier
coefficients inject the ambient space into a finite coefficient
space, placing this entire module inside O^r. Noetherianity makes
it finite, hence closed. No saturation assertion is needed.

Simultaneous finite Chebotarev approximates t(u),d(u) by T_q and
q<q> on all constituent representations. The closed module is
therefore stable under them. On old multiplicity spaces they act by
the identity times the eigensystem scalar, so they commute with
level operators.

For q not dividing n the second term in
  (T_q f)[n]=f[qn]+q(<q>f)[n/q]
vanishes. Products of traces can thus be tested by distinct fresh
primes avoiding the tested index.

Determinants reduce to 1 on a single character constituent. On H_U
the only remaining diamond corrections are undivided unary terms.
A fresh trace kills each such correction modulo the maximal ideal:
its coefficient test at n becomes one at nq, which cannot have
squarefree part J when q divides neither n nor J. Thus on the
coefficientwise reduced trace orbit,
  d(u)t(v)=t(v).
The two-by-two matrix trace identity then gives
  t(u)t(v)=t(uv)+t(uv^-1),       t(1)=0.                            (7)
The last equality is reduction of t(1)=2, not a characteristic-zero
identity.

5. Inertia and omission of the whole prime
-----------------------------------------
At a new prime p in U every constituent is either old from level
omitting p, or has conductor exponent one and primitive ramified
quadratic determinant. Old constituents have trivial inertia.
In the other case the conductor formula excludes monodromy and
gives one unramified character and one ramified character. Local
compatibility identifies the U_p eigenvalue with the Frobenius
eigenvalue lambda on the unramified line.

Choose inertia tau_p with ramified character -1, and write
  rho(tau_p)=diag(1,-1), rho(phi_p)=diag(lambda,mu).
If z is the lower-right entry of rho(g), then
  t(g tau_p phi_p)-t(g phi_p)=-2 z mu,
  t(g tau_p)-t(g)=-2 z.
Multiplication by U_p on the first difference and by d(phi_p) on
the second gives -2 z lambda mu. Thus, in characteristic zero,
  U_p[t(g tau_p phi_p)-t(g phi_p)]
       =d(phi_p)[t(g tau_p)-t(g)].                                 (8)
Old constituents contribute zero to both sides. The same calculation
applies to Eisenstein pairs and the unary constituents.

The weight estimate (4), after unary subtraction, kills two fresh
traces whenever at least one is split in matching parity. In opposite
parity it kills any two. Apply (7) to t(gu)t(u): for the permitted u,
  t(gu^2)=t(g).
The split subgroup, or the whole rational group in opposite parity,
therefore acts through its elementary abelian 2-quotient. Ramification
is contained in the actual level. This proves dependence on the
fixed-support and vertex residue labels.

Now apply (8) at coefficient 1 and use fresh primes. Put V=U minus p,
and let m_U=1 in matching parity and 0 otherwise. The relative
first-shell factor compared with the omitted-source test is
  2^(1+n_V-n_U-m_U+m_V)c_p(D'),       D'=V q'.                      (9)
The exponent is:
  split p: 0;
  simple p, matching U: 0;
  simple p, opposite U: 1.
These are exactly the three nonzero contraction cases and the
zero simple--simple case.

The two product Frobenius choices g phi_p and g tau_p phi_p flip the
Legendre sign in (5), so their c_p values differ by 1 modulo 2.
Their restrictions to the omitted-source field agree: B_V and H_V
are constructed from the original form with the same delta and
characters, and their new support is V. They are unramified at the
entire rational prime p, including both places over p when it splits
in the quadratic label field. The two omitted symbols are therefore
equal. Subtracting the two first-shell tests in (8) leaves exactly
the common omitted symbol.

This proves the contraction rule with product Frobenius labels.
It also explains why omission of only one oriented prime ideal
would not suffice. Isolation of a primary split vertex follows by
the same fresh test at t(1)=0.

6. The simple-twin identity
--------------------------
For completeness, in matching parity test two copies of one simple
Frobenius on H_U. Write b=B_U[1], epsilon=chi_(-4)(q), and
kappa=(psi_U/eta)(q). The recurrence and diamond correction give
  (b eta(q)/2)[a_q+kappa(q-epsilon)
                       -q epsilon^i(1+epsilon)].
If q=3 modulo 4, its bracket divided by 2 is a_q/2 modulo 2.
If q=1 modulo 4, it is a_q/2+1 modulo 2. Both equal
  #E(F_q)/2 modulo 2.
As b reduces to A(U), good twins preserve the symbol exactly when
this last bit is 1.

Fresh-prime testing replaces the repeated Frobenius by two distinct
primes. Varying either prime keeps only the rational labels in the
omitted, opposite-parity source. The already proved zero
simple--simple contraction removes dependence on their mutual bit.
Their fixed-support and retained-split labels are the needed data.

7. What this establishes
-----------------------
Subject to the stated classical modular inputs and the separately
specified arithmetic lower bounds, the even coefficient comparison,
minimum-depth construction, integral coefficient operators, and
trace/contraction/isolation/twin identities are justified above.
Finite precision is chosen after each bounded graph problem, and
none of these arguments supplies its own arithmetic lower bound.

The graph, reserve, and nonvanishing steps are supplied in the later
arithmetic-graph-audit.txt, nonvanishing-audit.txt, and
restricted-pointwise-assembly.txt. The latter establishes the odd
lower bound before normalization in the restricted non-CM family.
The universal polynomial still needs the exact descent/pole hypothesis
match, the remaining height constants, and polynomial instantiation.

Paired Selmer functionals and the two uniformity requirements
3 November 2026

Scope

This supplement checks the passage from the finite Selmer diagrams to
the paired functional, and the order of quantifiers in the two uses of
ring-class determinants. It concerns the non-CM, full rational
two-torsion branch used for E_l: y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l).

It uses the finite arithmetic classes and local identities proved in
finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt, the finite-model and evaluation results
in limit-interface-audit.txt, the switch computation in
determinant-switch-audit.txt, and the algebra proved in
determinant-transfer-audit.txt. The coefficient constructions and graph
realizations remain the separate arguments in coefficient-trace-audit.txt,
weighted-theta-audit.txt, and arithmetic-graph-audit.txt.

1. The map that produces the functional

Let A be one of the coefficient rings at which the relevant full local
complexes are contractible. Write C_U for the Selmer cone with local
conditions U, and C_V for the cone with their orthogonal conditions.
Conjugate transport and the Weil pairing identify the coefficient
representation used for C_V with the dual representation required in
global duality. The scalar character is inverted by conjugation and
the universal quadratic character is its own inverse. Thus this
identification is over A, with no division by the order of the quadratic
character group.

Global duality, with the actual local orthogonality homotopies, gives

  C_V -> RHom_A(C_U,A)[-3].                              (1)

At every place where U differs from V, the entire local complex is
contractible over A. Replacing the local condition there therefore
induces an isomorphism between the two Selmer cones in the derived
category. This can be seen directly from the localization triangle:
the cone of the comparison is the local quotient, which is zero.
At every remaining place the conditions are already identified by
the retained self-duality. Consequently a class z in H^1(C_U) has a
well-defined image in H^1(C_V), then through (1) in

  H^{-2}(RHom_A(C_U,A)).                                (2)

On a bounded free model for C_U, degree -2 of its Hom complex is
Hom_A(C_U^2,A). A cocycle there is a functional lambda satisfying
lambda d^1=0. Boundaries change lambda by a functional that vanishes
on H^2. This gives precisely the closed degree-two functional required
by the determinant calculation.

At a fraction field with H^1 and H^2 each one-dimensional, the tensor
of z and this functional lies in

  H^1(C_U) tensor H^2(C_U)^* = D(C_U).

This checks the cohomological shifts. It does not identify an arbitrary
linear dual of H^1 with H^2, and it does not assume that z is primitive.
If the generic complex is acyclic the construction uses coordinate zero,
as stipulated in the determinant source.

The comparison is an isomorphism in the derived category. Its inverse
there is unique. Different contracting homotopies give representatives
of the same comparison, hence the same cohomology functional after
extension to the common fraction field. Therefore functionals constructed
at the (2)-localization and at characteristic-zero height-one
localizations agree generically. They need not be represented by the
same integral cochain at every localization.

2. Why the contractions cost no new power of two per prime

Put Lambda=Z_2[[t]], O=completion of Lambda_(2), and
Omega=F_2((t)). For an individual active prime the quadratic inertia
operator is -1. In characteristic zero, inertia minus one is -2,
which is invertible. Over Omega the quadratic character disappears,
but the unramified scalar is z=(1+t)^a with a odd. For full rational
two-torsion the residual Tate Frobenius is the identity. Hence the
finite and singular Frobenius matrices are both z-1 times the identity,
and z-1 has t-adic order one. They are invertible in Omega.

The same statement holds before evaluating a finite binary family.
For G=(Z/2Z)^b the ring O[G] is local with residue field Omega; its
maximal ideal is generated by 2 and the augmentation ideal. Modulo that
ideal all quadratic character values are one. The preceding calculation
therefore gives a contractible residual local complex at each new prime.

A bounded finite free complex over a local ring with zero residual
cohomology is contractible: cancel a unit pivot in its differential,
remove that free disk, and repeat. Every division in this cancellation
is by a unit of the local ring. Applied over O[G], this gives integral
contractions. There is no power of 2 contributed by the number of new
primes. At the moving prime r, inertia minus one is t times a unit,
also a unit over O and over O[G].

At t=0 in characteristic zero the r-inertia test no longer applies.
There the fixed trace bound makes the two Frobenius determinants nonzero.
This supplies the separate contraction over Lambda_(t). Its central
2-adic denominators are bounded by that trace bound. It is not an
argument that t is a unit at the central point.

At 2N the scalar deformation is trivial and the actual Kummer condition
is used. Its derived reduction includes E(F)[2^a] in degree zero and
E(F)/2^a in degree one. Its exact orthogonality supplies (1) without
imposing ordinary or semistable reduction. At derivative primes the
finite and singular plane conditions, including degree zero, are
already self-dual. They are not the full and zero conditions.

Thus only the fixed initial multiplier or a fixed old-support correction
can enter the exponent a_0 in lambda in 2^{-a_0}(C^2)^*. Passing to more
active primes or a larger binary group does not enlarge it.

3. A local sign check in the switch

The finite-to-singular identity is s(B)=J f(A), where J is complex
conjugation on the Tate module. The pairing used between the two local
coordinates is the conjugate-Weil pairing

  beta(v,w)=epsilon <v,Jw>,

with epsilon a unit and < , > the alternating Weil pairing. Since
J^2=1, beta(v,Jv)=epsilon <v,v>=0. This is the isotropy needed in the
switch. Using <v,Jv> as that value would be an error: for
J=diag(1,-1), v=(a,b), the ordinary alternating pairing gives -2ab,
which need not vanish.

The additional derivative-prime precision is also necessary. At a
quotient killed by 2^a, the condition ell^2=1 modulo 2^{a+1} kills the
pure-tame Fox cup-product term, whose coefficient is a signed
binomial(ell^2,2). The mixed coefficient remains a unit. These are
identities on the finite local models and survive their simultaneous
limits. This supplies isotropy over the integral ring, not merely after
inverting 2.

The determinant-switch proof then works for a divisible nonzero
localized class as well as a primitive one. A primitive complement
must not be required. The fractional complement computation in
determinant-switch-audit.txt gives equal determinant valuations in the
finite and singular conditions without such a requirement.

4. Uniformity while the companion field varies

Fix E and A. Here K=Q(sqrt(k)) may vary with omega(k)<=A; active support
h is unrestricted. For full rational two-torsion the residual bound
from limit-interface-audit.txt, after j derivative primes, is

  R(E,A,j)=56 omega(2N_E)+8A+56j+70.                     (3)

This follows by removing the active local complexes after residual
localization, bounding the pro-2 generator number over Q by S-units,
and using an index-two Schreier bound over K. No bound on the 2-adic
length of a varying class group is asserted. Formula (3) bounds vector
space dimensions, not lengths.

Together with the common a_0 from section 2, the outer-DVR argument is
applicable. If determinant valuations tended to minus infinity, pass
to the outer quotient retaining the actual evaluation maps. A primitive
inner kernel vector survives. Whenever the outer kernel has dimension
greater than one, the rank-two evaluation and its derivative switch
decrease that dimension by two and preserve inner determinant valuations.
The number of switches is bounded by the initial dimension (3).
Each later model therefore has one of finitely many bounds (3).

At the terminal rank-one complex a nonzero maximal boundary minor
survives over the outer DVR. Its inner valuations are bounded on a
large set. If that bound is b_0, the elementary determinant computation
gives v_O(u)>=-a_0-b_0, contradicting the chosen sequence. This proves
the qualitative constant C(E,A).

The central comparison is used only at already known simple zeros.
There the forward Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin theorem gives finite Sha.
Its exact active-prime term is 4w(h); all component factors at primes
of h cancel in the quadratic Weil-restriction comparison. The remaining
sum over k is at most 2A. Thus

  2j_h(k)>=4w(h)+s(h)+s(hk)-C(E,A).                     (4)

Neither a constant depending arbitrarily on K nor a bound on the
finite quotient of its base Selmer group could replace C(E,A) here.
Those would not justify the later lower bound for all odd parameters.

5. The lower bound precedes the odd normalization

The independent nonvanishing result supplies an absolute prime-factor
bound A_0. The curve and prescribed residue classes can change its size
threshold, but not A_0. Combining this with a fixed finite supply of
seed labels gives companions k'=k'(h) with omega(k')<=A independent
of h and

  v_2(L_*(hk'))<=2w(hk')+s(hk')+C_e.                   (5)

The even coefficient and cyclotomic arguments prove (5). They do not
use the odd normalization being constructed. The unnormalized
Heegner-height comparison gives

  2j_h(k')-2j(P_h)=v_2(L_*(hk'))+O_{E,A}(1).           (6)

Subtract (6) from (4), use (5), and use
w(hk')=w(h)+w(k'), with w(k')<=A. The result is

  2j(P_h)>=2w(h)+s(h)-C_o.                             (7)

The constants in (7) are independent of h. This is the input for the
odd coefficient minimum. The odd minimum is not used to obtain (7).
This is the exact location at which uniformity in varying K is needed.

6. Uniformity after fixing the final companion

For the missing-vertex argument choose one separate k with
L(E^(k),1)!=0 and then fix K=Q(sqrt(k)). This occurs before choosing
the binary dimension b. Unlike section 4, the constants may now
depend on this particular K.

At augmentation the universal Selmer complex is the fixed old complex
extended by the singular local blocks of the new primes. The old
complex is a subcomplex, so the augmented differential is triangular.
The finite-model construction gives a chain-homotopy equivalence to
this model, not just an equality of Euler characteristics. Over the
local augmentation ring, split free disks and identify minimal models.
Lifting these invertible graded changes of basis gives the required
model over O[G].

Each new block has residual determinant of t-order at most four.
Eliminate all such blocks at once. After a sign evaluation write the
large block as B=A+2E, with A=diag(A_i) the augmented block diagonal
matrix. Write

  det A_i=t^{d_i}v_i+2w_i,     d_i<=4, v_i in Lambda^*,
  C_i=adj(A_i)/(t^{d_i}v_i),   C=diag(C_i).

This C is an approximate inverse, not A^{-1}. Its entries have poles
of order at most four. Since

  BC=1+2F,   F=diag(w_i/(t^{d_i}v_i))+EC,

the entries of F also have poles of order at most four. Consequently,
modulo 2^M,

  B^{-1}=C sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-2F)^j.                    (8)

The term with index j has pole order at most 4(j+1), so (8) has
pole order at most 4M, independently of the number of blocks.
One cannot instead give A^{-1} a precision-independent pole bound:
even (t+2)^{-1} has terms t^{-1}-2t^{-2}+4t^{-3} modulo 8.
The Schur complement therefore has bounded old size and a pole bound
at every precision independent of b. This is a statement about its
sign evaluations. A bound on its coefficients in the group basis is
neither asserted nor needed.

The central torsion bound h_1 is legitimate with K fixed. The Pontryagin
dual of the fixed base 2-primary Selmer group is a finitely generated
Z_2-module. Its torsion submodule has finite length, so the discrete
Selmer group modulo its maximal divisible subgroup is finite. This
does not assume finite Sha or positive Mordell--Weil rank. The moving
r contribution is bounded by the trace estimate. Therefore h_1 is
independent of b.

In the notation of determinant-transfer-audit.txt, let 2m+2 bound the
old total graded rank. Its explicit clearing calculation permits

  c_*=a_0+4h_1,             L(M)=16mM.                 (9)

It gives f_x u_x=d_x with f,d in O[G], f_0 in Lambda and
v_2(f_0(0))<=c_*. Only f needs the pole bound L(M). All unused-prime
Euler corrections and eliminated-block determinant units go into d.
This distinction prevents an uncontrolled number of new factors from
entering the central denominator bound.

The unused-prime correction is integral before character evaluation.
For e_q=a_q-z-z^{-1} and the two-place singular determinant l_q,
l_q=e_q^2 modulo 2. Hence

  1+((l_q/e_q^2-1)/2)(1+g_q)

lies in O[G], evaluates to l_q/e_q^2 at an unused prime and to 1 at
an active one. The displayed division by 2 is exact in O[G]. No
character projector with denominator |G| is introduced.

clearing and Laurent computations are in determinant-transfer-audit.txt.

7. Finite precision is chosen in the correct order

The odd minimum and its ultrafilter are fixed using (7). Choose the
final k and K as in section 6. Fix the common label alphabet, its
projection maps, and seed reserves. The two coefficient unit bounds
and the fixed-K height comparison give a constant B such that every
nonzero address subsequently constructed satisfies

  v_2(u_x(0))<=B.                                       (10)

This B is independent of b. The rank pattern required in clearing is
checked at each character by central specialization. At nonzero
addresses it follows from the forward theorem. At zero it follows
from s_2(E/K)=1, since the fixed companion has corank zero. Global
invariants vanish, and duality pairs degrees one and two. Thus the
generic pattern is either acyclic or rank one in those two degrees.

Choose an integer M>max(c_*, B+2c_*), then choose

  b>(16mM+2)(2^M-1).                                    (11)

The source's bound (11) suffices; no improved Boolean-degree estimate
is needed here. Only now construct the finite address networks for
this b. Let W_b bound their weights. The stabilized odd symbol agrees
with an actual coefficient stage on all graphs of weight at most W_b,
and all required divisibilities hold there. These are finitely many
conditions on one ultrafilter. Their finite intersection is large.
Choose a stage in that intersection, avoiding the fixed k, and realize
the finite graph by primes.

No one coefficient stage is required to work for every b. The stage,
its detecting prime, and the size of the realized primes may depend on
b and M. The constants B,c_*,m do not. This is enough for the one
application of the binary coefficient comparison at (11).

That comparison supplies x!=0 with

  v_2(u_x(0)-u_0(0))>=M-2c_*>B.                         (12)

If the base derivative were zero, Gross--Zagier would make the central
Heegner point torsion. In a generically acyclic constituent u_0 is
zero by definition. In a rank-one constituent the equal generic and
central dimensions give a contraction over Lambda_(t), and the
specialized tensor in section 1 is zero because its point factor is
zero. Thus u_0(0)=0 in both cases. Equations (10) and (12) contradict
one another. This is the final contradiction, conditional on the
coefficient/graph inputs specified at the beginning of this note.

Conclusion and remaining scope

The paired-functional construction uses the actual finite duality maps,
has the correct shifts, agrees generically across the localizations,
and has no denominator loss per active prime. The two uniformities
needed in the restricted assembly are different, and the argument
uses each in its valid range. The final finite-stage choice does not
require a single stage or an intersection serving infinitely many
cube dimensions.

This supplement closes these specific compatibility and quantifier
checks. It does not by itself prove the upstream coefficient, analytic,
or graph inputs. A completion claim for the rational polynomial must
still audit their combined use with pole completeness and bind the
result to the actual generated expression and fixed constants.

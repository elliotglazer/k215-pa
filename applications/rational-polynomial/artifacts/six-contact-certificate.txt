Six-contact multiplication certificate and fixed constants
2 November 2026

Scope

This note adapts linear-prime-certificate.txt to the rational map in
six-contact-height-model.txt. It gives a deterministic finite prescription
for the constants. The multiplier catalogue and the resulting polynomial
have not been expanded or evaluated. This is not a Lean verification.

The arithmetic inputs remain the six-contact height estimate, the pole
predicate's completeness, and the linear prime-pattern theorem with its
upper sieve. Their source-level arguments are recorded separately.
An exact finite prescription is not a new proof of those inputs.

1. Contacts and the detector

Put C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60}, xi=1, and
 F6(t)=t(t+15)(t+20)(t+24)(t+30)(t+60).
Use
 R0=eta F6(t)/(t-1)^8,  eta in {1,-1}.
Exclude all seven finite contacts.

The divisor of R0 has order one at each point of C, order -8 at 1,
and order two at infinity. There are therefore six odd contacts.
Six witness groups, whose traces have twelve summands, suffice.
An earlier tentative count of seven groups was unnecessary.

Use the twelve tests, normalization a=1-R0 q^2, and definitions R,
D', W, Theta, B in linear-prime-certificate.txt without other changes.
In particular the precisions are ONE for the eight reciprocal-x tests,
and TWENTY-FIVE for all three logarithm differences AND the final
multiplication test. Assigning precision one to the logarithm
differences would not prove the required index congruences.

At a height-contact prime outside the exception set, t-1 is a unit
and v(R0) is the positive odd contact depth. Thus the soundness proof
there applies verbatim and gives p^25 dividing n1*n2-n3.

2. Twenty-two prime forms

For a prescribed integer A, put u3=Av-u1-u2. Apply the source's linear
prime-pattern argument to
 v, uj-bv  (j=1,2,3 and b in C union {1}).
There are 22 forms. Their coefficient vectors are primitive. Their
pairwise independence can fail only at primes dividing a nonzero
difference of contacts. All such differences have absolute value at
most 61. The three directions (1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1) stay distinct
over every field.

Here is one deterministic replacement for the source's finite
catalogue construction. Let
 P0=product of all primes <=324, W=8*product of odd primes <=324.
For each unit residue r modulo W, let h(r,1),...,h(r,22) be the first
22 primes greater than 324 congruent to r modulo W, in increasing order.
Dirichlet's theorem proves that this finite prescription terminates.
Let Hcat be their union, and set
 Kcat={d*h: d divides P0^4 positively, h in Hcat}.

This enlarged catalogue contains every tuple required in the source.
For each of 22 forms, its small-prime valuation is in {0,1,2,3,4}:
the union bound uses 22*2^(-5)<1. Its required unit residues, after
division by the desired prime residues c2=1 modulo 8, c7=3 modulo 7,
and cp=1 otherwise, specify a unit r modulo W. Among the 22 listed
primes for that r one can choose distinct h for all occurrences.
Different residue classes cannot share a prime. The needed d*h is in Kcat.

The fixed threshold 324 also suffices for the density estimate.
For p>324 and t=22 the proportion avoiding the t zero hyperplanes
is at least 1-t/p. Therefore its local factor beta satisfies
 log beta >= log(1-t/p)-t log(1-1/p) >= -t^2/p^2.
Here t/p<1/2, log(1-x)>=-x-x^2 for 0<=x<=1/2, and
-log(1-1/p)>=1/p. These lower bounds have a positive convergent product.
The simultaneous exclusions of zero and -k remove at most 44p^2
points of F_p^3. The catalogue-prime plane argument removes at most
42p points of its p^2 points. Both leave a point.

All later upper-sieve thresholds may be enlarged independently
without changing this fixed catalogue. The source's order of choices
is preserved: the catalogue precedes A, which precedes the cutoff L,
which precedes the dilation X.

3. Odd valuations for the constructed slopes

Set Sb={primes <=324} union Hcat. Choose the selected prime values
larger than every prime eventually put in S below.

Each displayed integral form value is a signed member of Kcat times
one selected prime. Outside Sb its valuation is therefore zero or one.
For a fixed slope t=uj/v and p outside Sb, the following cases exhaust
the possibilities.

If v_p(v)=0, a positive valuation at one finite form gives its contact.
Distinct finite contacts remain distinct modulo p, so at most one
of the seven forms has positive valuation. A contact in C gives
v_p(R0)=1; contact at xi gives -8.

If v_p(v)=1 and v_p(uj)=0, all finite forms are units, all t-b have
valuation -1, and v_p(R0)=2. If v_p(uj)=1, every finite form has
valuation one, since no displayed value can have valuation two;
all normalized t-b are units and v_p(R0)=0.

Consequently the odd valuations of R0 outside Sb are exactly +1,
at at most six selected primes. Give each of these a separate witness
group. No group is needed for xi or infinity. Multiplication by
ell^2 a^2, followed by division by Theta^2, preserves valuation parity.
Thus all other primes outside Sb automatically have even v_p(B),
whether positive or negative.

4. A deterministic local multiplier

Retain the explicit elliptic curve E/F, F=Q(sqrt(2)), and generator P
in explicit-elliptic-data.txt and elliptic-generator-proof.txt.
Normalize every finite valuation v above p by v(p)=1.

Define M as the least positive integer m for which
                    v(x([m]P)) <= -4
at every place of F above every p in Sb.

This is a terminating exact search, not an unspecified existence choice.
Each [m]P is obtained by rational arithmetic in Q(sqrt(2)). Valuations
of its algebraic coordinates at specified prime ideals are computable
by finite integer arithmetic. P is nontorsion, so x([m]P) is defined.

The points with v(x)<=-4 form an open subgroup near O for this integral
Weierstrass equation. Indeed they are its formal subgroup v(z)>=2.
The product of the local elliptic groups is compact. Its quotient
by this open subgroup is finite, so some positive multiple of P
belongs to it. This proves termination of the search.

Every multiple of [M]P remains in that subgroup. The integral formal
differential gives v(c_j)>=-v_p(j) for logarithm coefficients.
For v(z)>=2, all non-linear terms have strictly larger valuation
than z, including at p=2. Hence the logarithm and its degree-25
truncation preserve valuation there. Their ratios on integer
multiples are integral. This proves the small-prime integrality
needed in certificate completeness.

Set cstar=4M and
 S=Sb union {prime divisors of cstar}.
There is no repeated enlargement or fixed-point choice of M.
Selected primes exceed max(S), so they are coprime to M and cstar.

5. An explicit normalization ell

Put Bp=4 if p<=324 and Bp=1 if p is in Hcat. A selected prime is outside
Sb; thus every form value has small-prime valuation between 0 and Bp.
Consequently
 |v_p(t-b)|<=Bp,
 |v_p(R0)|<=14Bp,  |v_p(t-1)|<=14Bp.
Put Cp=14Bp, Dp=51Cp, and
 Ep=52Cp+v_p(12)+4,
 ell=product over p in Sb of p^Ep.

The test precisions are at most 25. Once the original D tests are
integral, the quantities
 Cgj=Dgj/[R0^kj (t-1)^(kj+1)]
have valuations at least -Dp. This holds at all places above p,
including ramified ones with the normalized valuation.

For a a unit at Sb,
 v(R Cgj^2)>=2Ep-Cp-2Dp=Cp+2v_p(12)+8>v_p(12).
Every Wg is therefore sufficiently close to one, and
 v_p(Theta-12)>v_p(12). Also
 v_p(B)>=2Ep-Cp-2v_p(12)=103Cp+8>=4.
Thus the pole predicate's threshold is met at every p in its set
{2,3,5,7,13}; at the remaining primes of Sb the valuation is nonnegative.

Weak approximation chooses q integral at the selected primes,
sufficiently small p-adically at Sb, and close to a real root
of 1-R0 q^2 after choosing eta so R0>0. Keep a nonzero.
Then B is positive and less than 1/2. At each selected odd prime,
the twelve inverse-W residues are zero or one with at least one one.
Because p>324>12, their sum is nonzero and v_p(B)=1.
At all remaining primes outside Sb, its valuation is even.
The pole-completeness input therefore supplies Phi0(B).

6. Fixed height and finite guard

The preceding prescriptions determine a finite S without dependence
on e, n, A, or any multiplication query. Using the bounds recorded
in six-contact-height-model.txt, define the following integers:
 D=324!*3^(256*(324!)^4),
 G0=116064000000,
 E0=10*(D*G0)^2,
 R=max(D,max(S),10^12),
 Q=R^(R*E0),
 Bcm=D^2*3^(21000*D^2)+1,
 Ccm=2*Bcm+4096*(1028+5*D+84000*D^2),
 Aheight=2*10^13*max(1,Ccm,Q^17),
 T=(48*Aheight)^13.

The source-level height argument gives h(t)<=Aheight*M_S(t)^24.
The finite-guard calculation uses only this inequality, not the
number of contacts. It therefore gives |ni|<T for every false
multiplication certificate.

For a triple of five-coordinate codes X=(X1,X2,X3), put
 Hguard(X)=product over (a,b,c) in Z^3 with
   |a|,|b|,|c|<=T and ab!=c
 of sum over i=1,2,3 and j=1,...,5
   (Xij-iota((a,b,c)_i)j)^2.

This is a finite, explicitly prescribed rational polynomial.
All its fixed rational denominators can be cleared. On actual codes,
Hguard vanishes exactly at those finitely many false triples.
Adding a rational witness s with s*Hguard-1=0 to the certificate
therefore defines exact multiplication, conditional on the recorded
arithmetic inputs. True triples never meet a factor's zero set.

What remains

The prescriptions above remove free choices of a height constant,
an exceptional set, a catalogue, M, ell, and a finite cutoff.
They do not constitute an evaluated coefficient list or a finished
universal polynomial. The remaining compilation must specify the
program enumeration and implement the integer-Diophantine translation
for the halting and approximation relations, then compose it with
the exact rational model and the ordinary-input bridge.

The nonformal arithmetic dependencies must be kept visible throughout.
In particular, the pointwise 2-converse is still an input proved only
by the recorded source-level analysis, not by a Lean artifact.

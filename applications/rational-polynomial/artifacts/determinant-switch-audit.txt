Determinant switches and rank reduction
2 November 2026

Scope
-----
This note verifies rd:det-switch, rd:rank-two, rd:away-two, rd:minor,
and the contradiction argument of rd:outer-bound in the frozen source.
It uses the finite arithmetic classes checked in
finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt and the limit/evaluation interface
checked in limit-interface-audit.txt.

The central specialization formula, its comparison of Sha and Heegner
indices, and the weighted-theta/binary-family construction are not
proved here. Thus this is not yet a proof of the pointwise converse.

1. The determinant line and the local comparison
-----------------------------------------------
For a perfect cohomological complex use the inverse determinant line
  D(C)=tensor_i (det C^i)^{(-1)^(i+1)}.
In degrees one and two this is det C^1 tensor (det C^2)^-1.
A two-term elementary divisor [O --pi^d--> O] in those degrees
contributes pi^-d to the coordinate of a cohomology tensor in this
integral determinant line.

At a derivative place the finite and singular planes F,S are free
of rank two. Their conjugate-Weil cross pairing is
  beta(x,y)=epsilon <x,Jy>,
where < , > is unimodular alternating, J is the integral involution
given by complex conjugation, and epsilon is a unit. In particular
J is unimodular.

Let C_str,C_F,C_S,C_rel be the four actual global Selmer complexes
with their compatible localization and duality triangles. Work over
a DVR O and its fraction field K. Suppose the generic cohomology is
in degrees one and two, H^1(C_F)=KA, and the constructed class B in
C_S has
  f(A)=v!=0,     s(B)=Jv,     f(B)=0.                  (1)

The localization of A implies H^1(C_str)=0. By duality the relaxed
localization image in F direct-sum S is self-annihilating and has
dimension two. It contains the independent vectors (v,0),(0,Jv).
Its intersections with F and S are therefore precisely their spans.
It follows that H^1(C_S)=KB and H^1(C_rel)=KA direct-sum KB.

Let A*,B* be the dual basis of H^2(C_str)=H^1(C_rel)*.
The finite-plane localization triangle gives the exact sequence
  0 -> KA -> F -> KA* direct-sum KB* -> KA* -> 0.
The boundary of x in F is epsilon_F <x,v> B*, with epsilon_F a unit,
because it is dual to relaxed localization. Choose x over K mapping
to B*. Its wedge v wedge x has unit volume in det_O F. This follows
from unimodularity and <x,v> being a unit; it does not require x or
v to be primitive integral vectors. Likewise choose y in S mapping
to A*, so Jv wedge y has unit volume in det_O S.

The two determinant exact sequences send the tensors of A and B
with their own dual functionals to
  (A* wedge B*)* tensor (v wedge x),
  (A* wedge B*)* tensor (Jv wedge y),
up to units and ordering signs. The first factors agree, and the
second factors have the same integral valuation. Thus
  v_O(u_F)=v_O(u_S).                                  (2)

All torsion in C_str remains in its determinant line and is common
to both expressions. For example v=pi^d e_1 may require
x=unit*pi^-d e_2; the wedge still has unit volume. Replacing the
fractional complement by an unjustified integral complement would
lose this equality.

2. A single evaluation detecting two classes
---------------------------------------------
Let H be a normal subgroup acting trivially on an absolutely
irreducible representation W over a field K, with End(W)=K.
Assume restriction of the relevant H^1 classes to H is injective.
Let b_1,b_2 be independent classes.

On H these are additive homomorphisms. Their joint image has a
K-span U stable under the ambient group, because conjugation of an
element of H transports its value by the coefficient action.
Both projections U -> W are surjective. A proper submodule of
W direct-sum W with both projections surjective is the graph of
a scalar isomorphism; equivalently it imposes a nonzero scalar
linear relation on b_1|H,b_2|H. Injectivity of restriction excludes
that relation. Hence U=W direct-sum W.

Project each value by the finite-localization map supplied by 1+tau,
which maps W onto the two-dimensional finite plane. The projected
joint image spans all pairs of finite-plane vectors. Let Q be their
2-by-2 determinant. If Q vanished on every actual joint evaluation,
it would also have zero polar form on every pair of evaluations,
because the image is closed under addition. The identity
  Q(sum a_i u_i)
    =sum a_i^2 Q(u_i)
      +sum_{i<j} a_i a_j (Q(u_i+u_j)-Q(u_i)-Q(u_j))
would make it vanish on the entire span. This is false, as witnessed
by a pair of basis vectors. Therefore one actual element of H gives
rank-two localization. One class may be any prescribed nonzero class.

The conclusion concerns a single evaluation, not merely a linear
combination of unrelated evaluations.

3. Rank reduction when the scalar character is not quadratic
-------------------------------------------------------------
At a characteristic-zero test where Psi^2!=1, moving-prime inertia
distinguishes the two summands of the induced representation. It is
absolutely irreducible by the Tate-image argument in the limit audit.

Suppose the specialized Selmer H^1 has dimension s>1. Include a
nonzero specialization of a primitive generic kernel vector in the
two classes of section 2. The finite Chebotarev construction realizes
rank-two localization at a derivative prime.

The relaxed localization image is self-annihilating. Since it
contains all of the two-dimensional finite plane, it equals that
plane. The strict kernel has dimension s-2. Changing to the singular
condition adds no localized vector, so the new H^1 dimension is s-2.

In the generic field the chosen primitive vector localizes nontrivially.
The original class spans the generic rank-one kernel, so it does also.
The constructed derivative class satisfies (1). Thus the generic
rank remains one and (2) preserves the determinant valuation.

4. The quadratic-character cases at characteristic-zero tests
---------------------------------------------------------------
This step cannot be replaced by assuming induced irreducibility.
If Psi^2=1, conjugation splits the specialized complex into two signs.
The induced Galois summands are nonisomorphic absolutely irreducible
two-dimensional representations. In the CM case this uses the
exclusion of the CM field.

Each sign has one finite coordinate and one singular coordinate.
Its local pairing is a nondegenerate symmetric hyperbolic plane.
In characteristic zero its isotropic lines are exactly the two axes:
in suitable coordinates the isotropy equation is 2xy=0.

If both signs occur in global H^1, choose a class from each sign,
including a nonzero sign component of the prescribed primitive vector.
Their joint restriction spans the sum of the two nonisomorphic simple
modules. The product of the two one-dimensional finite coordinates
is a nonzero quadratic polynomial on that span. The same polarization
argument gives one evaluation nonzero on both signs. It yields the
rank-two reduction of section 3.

If only one sign occurs and s>1, choose finite localization nonzero on
the prescribed vector in that sign. In its sign the relaxed image
is the finite axis. In the other sign the global finite intersection
is zero, so self-annihilation forces the relaxed image to be the
singular axis. The switch keeps a strict kernel of dimension s-1
and introduces one class in the other sign. The total dimension is
still s, but both signs now occur. One further rank-two switch lowers
the dimension by two.

Thus reduction terminates. The generic rank-one kernel supplies a
primitive vector surviving specialization after every switch, so the
specialized dimension cannot drop below one.

5. Integrality away from (2)
----------------------------
Localize Lambda=Z_2[[t]] at a height-one prime other than (2). The
residue field has characteristic zero. Local acyclicity and global
duality kill specialized cohomology in degrees zero and three.
Unit cancellations leave a square free complex in degrees one and two.

The generic kernel is saturated in a free module over a DVR, so a
primitive generic kernel vector has nonzero reduction. Apply sections
3 and 4 until the specialized dimension is one. In a minimal free
model there is then one term in each of degrees one and two. Its
differential is zero because the generic kernel also has rank one.
The class and its paired functional are integral: the sole fixed
denominator is a power of 2, a unit at this height-one prime.
Their determinant coordinate is therefore integral.

Equation (2) transports this conclusion back through all switches.
It holds at every height-one prime except (2). Normality, equivalently
the intersection description for the Krull domain Lambda[1/2],
then gives
  u in Lambda[1/2].                                   (3)

For the rank-one hypothesis at an unknown base, compact Selmer rank
equals the full 2-primary Selmer corank. This includes the Tate module
of divisible Sha. It must not be replaced by the Mordell--Weil rank.
Upper semicontinuity at the central rank-one fiber supplies the needed
generic rank bound even when the central point class is zero.

6. Terminal minors
-------------------
For a square two-term complex [O^n --D--> O^n] of generic kernel
rank one, let z be an integral kernel vector and let lambda be a
closed functional in 2^-a (O^n)*. Suppose a nonzero (n-1)-minor A
has valuation at most b. After permuting rows and columns, Schur
elimination by determinant-one triangular matrices gives
  u=plus or minus alpha beta/det A,
where alpha is the last source coordinate of z and beta is lambda
on the last target basis vector. The surviving coordinates are
unchanged by those triangular transformations. Thus
  v_O(u)>=-a-b.                                        (4)
The empty minor for n=1 is 1.

7. The uniform bound at (2)
---------------------------
Suppose, contrary to the desired uniform bound, a sequence of
simple-zero instances with bounded omega(disc K) has determinant
valuations tending to minus infinity. The residual-rank bound from
the limit audit gives uniformly bounded square two-term minimal
models. Retain their classes, cleared functionals, and evaluation and
duality diagrams, and pass to the outer DVR.

A primitive generic kernel vector from each inner model has an outer
limit with at least one unit coordinate. Hence the outer kernel
dimension is at least one. It may be larger than the inner generic
dimension. Its additional vectors need only be approximate inner
cycles; their crossed identities are valid by the integral degree-two
evaluation argument already checked in the limit audit.

If the outer dimension is greater than one, moving inertia has infinite
order there, so section 3 applies. Select a rank-two evaluation including
the surviving primitive vector, and realize it by derivative primes at
the original finite stages. Extend the same diagrams and use the same
ultrafilters. The outer dimension drops by two.

The inner generic localization is nonzero on an outer-large set.
Although the original inner class may be a highly divisible multiple
of its primitive vector, it still localizes nontrivially over the
inner fraction field. Equations (1) and (2) therefore preserve every
inner determinant valuation. New primitive vectors again prevent
the outer dimension from becoming zero.

After finitely many switches the outer kernel dimension is one.
Each fixed number of switches has its own uniform residual model
bound; the number of switches is bounded by the initial outer rank.
The terminal outer differential has a nonzero maximal boundary minor.
Its inner minors have one bounded finite valuation on a large set,
by the definition of the outer DVR. Equation (4) contradicts the
assumed valuations tending to minus infinity.

Consequently there exists C(E,A) with
  v_(2)(u)>=-C(E,A)                                    (5)
for the stated simple-zero instances. This proof is qualitative and
does not calculate C(E,A). A bound on model size alone would not imply
(5); the rank-reducing arithmetic switches are essential.

Assessment
----------
The determinant comparison, the two sign cases, the passage from a
span to one rank-two evaluation, and the outer contradiction are
consistent and proved above with the stated finite and limit inputs.
No primitivity of the localized Heegner point is required.

The remaining pointwise audit includes the central specialization and
isogeny comparison, followed by the coefficient/theta identities and
binary-family construction. The ordinary-input universal equation
still depends on completion of that audit and on the remaining explicit
height constants.

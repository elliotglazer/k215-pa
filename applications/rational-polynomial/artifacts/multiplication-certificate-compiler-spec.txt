Compilation of the rational multiplication certificate
3 November 2026

Scope and artifacts

multiplication-certificate-library.json is an explicit library of
integer-coefficient polynomial circuits over Q. Every call is literal
substitution of an earlier polynomial, with its own block of rational
witness variables. The library contains the unguarded height certificate.
rational-model-assembly.js supplies the finite guard, binds the fixed
scalar and normalization constants, and constructs the natural-number
predicate from four squares in the model.

The arithmetic proof still uses the source-level dependencies recorded
in six-contact-certificate.txt. This compiler does not prove those
dependencies. The enormous prescribed constants have not been evaluated.
The small-constant assembly used by the tests is a structural test only;
it is not advertised as a valid multiplication model.

1. Literal polynomial substitution

A template has its parameter variables first, followed by its existential
variables. A call records the earlier template number, expression nodes
for its parameters, and the start of a private block for all its witnesses.
Recursive substitution therefore ends at const, var, add and mul nodes.

The library audit checks backward references, parameter counts, complete
variable coverage, disjoint witness blocks, and ordinary polynomial leaves.
expandTemplate performs the actual substitution for manageable cases.
The fully expanded test-group polynomial agrees with the library call
both at exact satisfying witnesses and after changing a test output.

As before, conjunction uses sums of squares and disjunction uses products.
All existential variables range over Q. Thus the resulting root equation
is equivalent to the indicated finite existential formula.

2. The field-valued test group

Its parameters are four point codes C0,C1,C2,C3 and twelve output elements
D0,...,D11 of F=Q(sqrt(2)). The outer formula will impose
 C0=[cstar]P, Ci=[cstar]Pi for i=1,2,3.

The group introduces H,U1,U2,U3 in D and sets
 V0=H-C0, Vi=Ui-Ci.
The raw addition calls are legitimate because the outer scalar formulas
and these memberships put every input in D. The formula requires
H and V0 to be finite and nonzero.

For the eight points H,U1,U2,U3,V0,V1,V2,V3 it constructs z, 1/x and
the explicit polynomial L(z). The origin conventions are z(O)=0 and
(1/x)(O)=0. It requires nonzero L(z(H)) and L(z(V0)), then constructs
 L_i=L(z(Ui))/L(z(H)), R_i=L(z(Vi))/L(z(V0)).

Its outputs are, in this exact order:
 1/x(H), 1/x(U1), 1/x(U2), 1/x(U3);
 the conjugates of 1/x(V0), 1/x(V1), 1/x(V2), 1/x(V3);
 L_1-conjugate(R_1), L_2-conjugate(R_2), L_3-conjugate(R_3);
 L_1*L_2-L_3.

Conjugating R_i also conjugates the coefficients of L and the coordinates
of Vi, so it is the expression required by the original certificate.
The first eight tests have precision one; all four remaining tests have
precision 25. The template has 44 rational parameters and 162 witnesses.

3. The slope certificate

Parameters are t, the fixed normalization integer ell, and the four C codes.
It excludes t in {0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60,1}, imposes eta^2=1, and defines
 R0*(t-1)^8=eta*t(t+15)(t+20)(t+24)(t+30)(t+60),
 a=1-R0*q^2, R=ell^2*a^2*R0.
It explicitly requires nonzero ell and a. Therefore R0 and R are nonzero.

For six independent test groups define
 C_gj=D_gj/(R0^k_j*(t-1)^(k_j+1)),
 W_g=product_j(1+R*C_gj^2),
 Theta=2*sum_g (1/W_g)_0,
 B*Theta^2=R.
Here subscript 0 is the rational coefficient of 1 in F.

Each displayed quotient has its denominator excluded either directly or
by the preceding equations. In particular every W_g has an inverse-norm
guard, and Theta has a rational inverse guard. The template then calls
the explicit eleven-witness pole polynomial at B.

The normalized C_gj expression is exactly D'_gj/R^k_j after cancellation.
All factors cancelled are already nonzero. Hence this avoids no required
condition and changes none of the valuation arguments.
There are 22 parameters and 1,298 witnesses.

4. The height certificate and guard

The height certificate takes three root codes, ell, and the four C codes.
It imposes root membership in D. For each root it introduces a0,a1,b,
requires b nonzero, and sets b*x0=a0, b*x1=a1. The nine resulting rational
quantities are each a sum of three slopes with the preceding certificates.
It has 36 parameters and 35,106 witnesses.

For the finite guard, write every coordinate of iota(n) as A_nj/B_nj
in reduced form with B_nj positive, including the origin flag.
For each false triple a*b != c in [-T,T]^3, let D_abc be the product
of the fifteen denominators of its three point codes. Use the factor
 sum_(i,j) [D_abc*X_ij - (D_abc/B_nij)*A_nij]^2.

Every coefficient is an integer. The factor vanishes precisely at that
false triple of codes. The product H_T of these factors is therefore a
fully specified integer polynomial. The guard s*H_T-1=0 has exactly one
rational witness, independently of T. No rational constants need extra
witness variables in this guard.

The finite-height argument puts all false certificate triples inside
the prescribed box. Hence adjoining this guard makes multiplication
exact, provided the recorded arithmetic dependencies and fixed-constant
requirements hold. The true triples never vanish at any guard factor.

5. Fixed scalar maps with one witness

elliptic-division-polynomials.js gives an alternative to introducing
a witness point at every binary addition step. It uses normalized
division polynomials f_n in x, with
 psi_n=f_n for odd n and psi_n=2y*f_n for even n.

Put A=-11243+7950sqrt(2), F=x^3+A*x and D=16F^2.
The initial polynomials are
 f0=0, f1=f2=1,
 f3=3x^4+6A*x^2-A^2,
 f4=2(x^6+5A*x^4-5A^2*x^2-A^3).
For m>=3,
 f_(2m)=f_m*(f_(m+2)*f_(m-1)^2-f_(m-2)*f_(m+1)^2).
For 2m+1>=5, put L=f_(m+2)*f_m^3 and R=f_(m-1)*f_(m+1)^3.
Then f_(2m+1)=D*L-R if m is even, and L-D*R if m is odd.
These are finite polynomial recurrences with no division.

For a nonzero fixed n and a finite point p in D, set g=psi_n(p).
The graph of r=[n]p is
 g^2*x(r)=x(p)*g^2-psi_(n+1)(p)*psi_(n-1)(p),
 2*g^4*y(r)=psi_(2n)(p).
The second equation follows from the division-polynomial chain rule.
Since D is torsion free, g is nonzero. One rational inverse-norm witness
enforces this. A separate origin branch gives r=O when p=O.
Negative n uses negation; n=0 simply gives O.

The convention here is psi_2=+2y. Stange's corrected v3 uses -2y;
our psi_n equals (-1)^(n-1) times her polynomial. Both the chain rule
and the displayed x formula are preserved under this change.
The v2 HTML introduction has an incorrect sign in its x relation and
is not used for that identity. The corrected v3 has x([m])-x([n]).

Primary references:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15428v3
https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/arithmetic_curves/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/ell_generic.html#sage.schemes.elliptic_curves.ell_generic.EllipticCurve_generic.multiplication_by_m

The scalar graph passed 44 exact comparisons with independent affine
double-and-add, 44 changed-x checks, and 30 changed-y-sign checks.
The checks include zero, positive and negative scalars, and the origin.
They supplement the polynomial identities rather than prove them.

6. Simplified fixed constants

Let P0 be the product of the 66 primes at most 324, and W=4P0.
For each unit residue r modulo W, take the first 22 primes greater
than 324 in that residue class. Let H be their product and Hmax their
maximum. Dirichlet's theorem proves this finite prescription terminates.
Put Ns=P0*H.

The local condition defining M admits an elementary integer test.
Write 1/x([m]P)=(a+b*sqrt(2))/c with a,b,c integers, c positive and
gcd(a,b,c)=1. Then the required condition at every place above every
prime in Sb is equivalent to
 Ns^4 divides a, Ns^4 divides b, gcd(c,Ns)=1.

Indeed O_F=Z[sqrt(2)], so being in p^4 O_F tensor Z_p is equivalent
to both rational coefficients being in p^4 Z_p. In the reduced common
denominator representation a denominator divisible by p would leave
at least one coefficient with negative valuation. Thus the displayed
integer test is exact. The formal-group argument in the earlier note
proves that some positive m passes it. Choose the least and put cstar=4M.

The normalization simplifies to
 ell=12*P0^2916*H^732.
For small primes the old exponent is 52*56+v_p(12)+4=2916+v_p(12).
For catalogue primes it is 52*14+4=732.

Use the previous height formulas but replace R by
 max(D,Hmax,cstar,10^12).
This bounds every prime in Sb and every prime dividing cstar. It only
enlarges the previous permissible R and hence preserves the height
cutoff. fixed-certificate-constants.json records the entire prescription.
The implementation includes exact arithmetic algorithms for its finite
searches, but evaluating these enormous constants is not practical.

7. Witness counts and remaining assembly

The bound multiplication template uses:
 35,106 height-certificate witnesses;
 20 coordinates for C0,C1,C2,C3;
 4 scalar inverse-norm witnesses;
 3 witnesses fixing the nonintegral coordinates of P;
 1 finite-guard inverse.
Total: 35,134 rational witnesses, beyond its 15 root-code parameters.
This count does not depend on the magnitudes of cstar, ell or T.

The natural-number predicate writes a model integer as a sum of four
model squares. It uses four multiplication calls, 50 intermediate
coordinates, and 18 addition witnesses, for a total of 140,604 witnesses
beyond its five input coordinates. This is a predicate on model codes,
not an integrality test on ordinary rational coordinates.

These counts are verified by the small-constant structural assembly.
They do not validate those small constants as arithmetic choices.
The remaining task is to interpret the two saved integer polynomial
circuits through these exact model operations, compose the ordinary
integer input bridges, and verify the complete finite expression and
all its arithmetic dependencies.

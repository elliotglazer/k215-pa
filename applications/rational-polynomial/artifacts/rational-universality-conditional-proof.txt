1. Finite polynomial semantics and coefficients

All scalar variables in the candidate range over Q. In the underlying
Builder, equality f=g is represented by f-g; conjunction of polynomial
equations is represented by a sum of their squares; disjunction is
represented by their product. These replacements are equivalent over Q,
because Q is an ordered field and an integral domain. Empty conjunction
and disjunction use 0 and 1. RationalBuilder adds its fixed-coefficient
equations to the outer conjunction, so an unselected branch does not
leave a fixed rational constant unconstrained.

A rational constant p/q uses a fresh rational r and the equation
qr-p=0, with nonzero fixed integer q. Every scalar node is consequently
an integer-coefficient polynomial. A library call is literal polynomial
substitution, with fresh variables for the callee's witness block.
The library is acyclic; calls refer to earlier templates. Recipe calls
likewise have finite depth: universal calls halting and bridge; bridge
calls accuracy; halting and accuracy call power_value and the library.

The finite constructors also define ordinary polynomials. Each
psi_component has a fixed integer index. The recurrence for its
normalized division polynomial uses only sums and products and smaller
indices after the base cases 0 through 4. The finite guard is a product
over a fixed finite set of integer triples. At a triple (a,b,c), each
coordinate of iota(a),iota(b),iota(c) is a fixed rational A_j/B_j.
With D the product of the positive B_j, its factor is
 sum_j (D*X_j-(D/B_j)*A_j)^2,
which has integer coefficients. Computing these fixed rational points
uses a nontorsion P, so their prescribed division-polynomial denominators
do not vanish for nonzero indices. Index zero uses its separate O code.

The constants contain finite algorithms, all independent of e,n and
of every quantified variable. Dirichlet's theorem terminates each
prime-list search in a reduced residue class. The least_formal_multiple
search also terminates: at each of the finitely many primes dividing
its fixed argument N, sufficiently small formal neighborhoods of O
force both Q_p coefficients of 1/x to lie in N^4 Z_p. Their preimages
are open subgroups of finite index in the compact local elliptic groups.
Some positive multiple of P belongs to their intersection. Exact
coordinate arithmetic then detects the required coefficient divisibility
and denominator coprimality. The common integral basis is 1,sqrt(2),
including at 2; sufficiently small neighborhoods work also at ramified
or bad-reduction primes. Remaining operations are finite integer
arithmetic, maxima, powers with nonnegative integer exponents, and
factorials. All fixed scalar indices used by the library are positive.

Thus U denotes one finite integer-coefficient polynomial, with no
external predicate or oracle remaining in its syntax. This does not
assert that expanding its monomials or evaluating its huge coefficients
is practical. Neither operation is necessary to define the polynomial.

2. Exact elementary relations on the cyclic points

The map iota is injective because P has infinite order. Field elements
are pairs (a,b), meaning a+b*sqrt(2), with the exact product
(a,b)(c,d)=(ac+2bd,ad+bc). The norm a^2-2b^2 vanishes over Q exactly
when a=b=0. Therefore the polynomial norm*u-1 enforces precisely the
nonvanishing needed for a field inverse. Multiplication by a supplied
inverse is never inferred from a denominator-cleared equation alone.

On canonical elliptic inputs, raw_add has five branches: left O;
finite left and right O; finite inverses; distinct finite x-coordinates;
and equal finite points with nonzero y. In the last two branches the
norm inverse guard and the slope equation determine the unique slope.
The output formulas are x3=slope^2-x1-x2 and
y3=slope*(x1-x3)-y1. Equal finite x-coordinates on E imply y2=+/-y1;
the y=0 case belongs to the inverse branch. Thus these cases are
exhaustive and their outputs are the canonical group sum. In particular,
on iota(a),iota(b) the relation holds exactly for iota(a+b).

raw_add does not assert membership of arbitrary malformed inputs.
Every use in the proof below supplies canonical inputs by induction.
This precondition is essential and is not replaced by the template's
informal name.

For a positive fixed integer m, the scalar template uses
 g=psi_m(x,y),
 x([m]Q)=(x*g^2-psi_(m+1)*psi_(m-1))/g^2,
 y([m]Q)=psi_(2m)/(2*g^4).
These are the classical division-polynomial identities. The template
uses an explicit nonzero norm guard on g, the two denominator-cleared
coordinate equations, and a separate O-to-O branch. On the cyclic
points every finite input is nontorsion, so g never vanishes. Hence
this is exactly the fixed scalar graph on the inputs used here.
The normalized recurrences in elliptic-division-polynomials.js give
psi_m=f_m for odd m and psi_m=2y*f_m for even m; substituting these
identities into the standard recurrences gives precisely the saved
even/odd formulas, with D=(2y)^4=16(x^3+A*x)^2. Their use introduces
no extra variables depending on the magnitude of m.

For a primary reference see Stange, Division polynomials for arbitrary
isogenies, version 3, introduction (chain rule and relation to x):
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15428v3
That reference uses Psi_2=-2y. Our psi_n=(-1)^(n-1)*Psi_n, so its
chain rule with outer index 2 becomes psi_(2m)=2y([m]Q)*psi_m^4,
and its x-coordinate identity is unchanged. This accounts for the
sign convention used by the saved scalar graphs.

3. Defining natural indices, conditional on M

The natural template introduces four point codes U_i and four S_i,
requires multiply(U_i,U_i,S_i)=0, and adds the S_i by three raw_add
calls to give its supplied output A. Under M the U_i=iota(u_i) and
S_i=iota(u_i^2), with u_i in Z. The addition result is therefore
A=iota(sum_i u_i^2). Conversely, every a>=0 is a sum of four integer
squares, so A=iota(a) admits these witnesses. Thus natural is exact
on all rational five-tuples, not just on previously validated inputs.
The two intermediate sums are canonical by the addition argument.

This constrains an INTEGER INDEX, not the individual rational
coordinates. It does not define Z as a subset of Q.

4. Evaluation of an ordinary polynomial circuit

The three generator variables in each model recipe satisfy
 8g0-1641=0, 4g1+15465=0, 32g2-87483=0.
The recipe's special point references consequently denote exactly
O, P=(0,g0,-145,g1,g2), and -P. Source constants 0,1,-1 map to these.
Each other source constant is positive and uses its fixed scalar
template on P. Equal source constants may share their exact code.

Each explicit natural source variable has a five-coordinate code and
a natural-template call. The source inputs of halting and accuracy
have these calls as well. Every arithmetic addition or multiplication
node has a fresh output point and its raw_add or multiply equation.

Induct along the source's topological node order. A source variable
represents its natural integer assignment. A constant represents its
integer value. If preceding nodes represent a,b, section 2 or M forces
the next output to be iota(a+b) or iota(ab). Conversely these values
admit the required witnesses. Hence the output of every node is the
code of its EXACT integer polynomial value. Intermediate values are
allowed to be negative: no natural predicate is imposed on them.

For a zero-mode recipe, the five root-coordinate equations require
the final code to equal O. Injectivity of iota makes this equivalent
to the source polynomial being zero. For a value-mode recipe, those
equations identify it with the supplied output code instead.

5. Hierarchical power calls and quantifiers

The source circuits use one 474-node ordinary polynomial with three
parameters and 79 natural private variables. The recipe power_value
has 20 scalar parameters: three input point codes and an output code.
It restricts its 79 internal source variables to natural indices,
evaluates all 474 nodes as in section 4, and identifies the root code
with the supplied output. Its three inputs are permitted to be signed
integer codes; its arithmetic identity is valid for all such inputs.

This relation EVALUATES the power polynomial. It does not separately
require its value to vanish. In the parent formula, a nonzero value
can occur inside an unselected branch of a disjunction. Forcing every
power call to zero would change that parent polynomial and is not
what the saved recipe does.

The source's private blocks are disjoint, each is used by exactly one
call, and no parent variable node refers to their entries. Therefore,
in the soundness direction, the 79 natural indices extracted from
each rational subcall can be assembled with the parent's explicit
natural variables into one complete natural assignment to the source
polynomial. In the completeness direction, any complete natural
assignment splits into these private assignments and gives witnesses
to every value-mode subcall. There is no duplicated variable that
would need an unrecorded equality constraint.

verify-rational-translation-binding.js independently checks these
scope conditions for every one of the 15845 calls in each source
circuit. The source-level allocation proof is also included in
integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt.

6. The two translated predicates

Apply sections 3-5 to the pinned natural-witness theorems proved in
integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt and accuracy-correctness-proof.txt.
For ANY rational input codes A,B, the halting recipe V has witnesses
iff A=iota(a), B=iota(b) for natural a,b and program a halts on b.

For ANY rational input codes A,H,J, the accuracy recipe has witnesses
iff they are iota(k),iota(h),iota(j), respectively, with k,h,j natural,
h>=1, j=k*h and |rho(h,j)-k|<1/8. Here
rho(h,j)=Tr(z(jP)/z(hP))/2. The natural predicates supply membership
and the theorem for the accuracy polynomial supplies h>=1.

7. The bridge to ordinary integer parameters

On the domain just supplied, raw_trace_ratio introduces field values
z(H),z(J) and q, requires H finite, enforces z=-x/y in the finite
branches and z(O)=0, and imposes q*z(H)=z(J) with a nonzero norm
guard on z(H). It then sets r=q0, the coefficient of 1 in q.
All denominators are nonzero: H is a positive multiple of P; J is
either O or a nonzero multiple. Section 1 of the accuracy proof
shows that x and y are nonzero for every nonzero multiple of P.
Consequently r is exactly rho(H,J).

The strict_close template is
 (1-64(a-r)^2)*(q1^2+q2^2+q3^2+q4^2)-1=0.
It has rational witnesses iff |a-r|<1/8. For completeness, any positive
rational b/c is a sum of four rational squares: write the positive
integer bc as four integer squares and divide their bases by c.
Apply this to the reciprocal of 1-64(a-r)^2. Soundness follows because
the sum of squares in a solution is positive.

The bridge Gamma(a,A) conjoins accuracy(A,H,J), raw_trace_ratio(H,J,r),
and strict_close(a,r), with fresh rational witnesses. If a is an
ordinary integer, these imply A=iota(k), k>=0, |r-k|<1/8 and
|r-a|<1/8. Hence |a-k|<1/4, which forces a=k. The conclusion also
rules out malformed A. No exactness assertion is made for nonintegral a.

For the converse let a=k>=0. In the compact group
E(R) x E^sigma(R), positive multiples h_i*(P,P^sigma) approach O.
Indeed, covering by finitely many translates of a sufficiently small
identity neighborhood gives two orbit points with difference in any
prescribed neighborhood. Their positive index difference supplies h_i.
To obtain arbitrarily large h_i, shrink that neighborhood to avoid
the finitely many nonzero multiples with smaller positive indices.
The infinite order of P makes this possible.

At either real embedding z=-x/y is a local parameter at O and
z([k]Q)=k*z(Q)+O(z(Q)^2). Thus z([k]h_iP)/z(h_iP) tends to k
at both embeddings, and its half trace tends to k. For k=0 the ratio
is identically zero. Some positive h therefore gives
|rho(h,kh)-k|<1/8. Use H=iota(h), J=iota(kh), r=rho(h,kh).
Section 6 supplies accuracy witnesses, and the other two relations
have witnesses by their exactness. This proves

 for a in N and A in Q^5: Gamma(a,A) iff A=iota(a).

The limit argument proves existence only. No analytic approximation
or convergence-rate computation appears inside the polynomial.

8. Both universality implications and variable count

The universal recipe is the sum of squares of the three substituted
polynomials V(A,B), Gamma(e,A), Gamma(n,B), each with a disjoint
witness block. It existentially chooses the ten coordinates of A,B.

If U(e,n,w)=0 with rational w, each summand vanishes. The two bridges
give A=iota(e), B=iota(n). Section 6 then says program e halts on n.
Conversely, if the program halts, choose these exact codes. Section 6
supplies witnesses for V, and section 7 supplies both bridge witnesses.
Disjointness permits all choices at once, giving a rational zero of U.

The saved allocation counts, checked by the assembly auditor, are:

 halting:     292887770443 witnesses beyond 10 parameters;
 accuracy:    292888578910 witnesses beyond 15 parameters;

The bridge adds ten coordinates H,J, one r, nine trace witnesses and
four proximity witnesses: exactly 24 beyond accuracy. Therefore U has
 10+292887770443+2*292888578934 = 878664928321
rational witnesses, plus its two external parameters e,n.

The conclusion is the stated conditional universality theorem for U.
An unconditional claim still requires proving M for the prescribed
arithmetic certificate. That requirement is unchanged by this proof.

The soundness implication (a rational zero implies halting) uses only
the forward implication of M. It never needs existence of multiply
witnesses for arbitrary true triples. The separate certificate proof
in multiplication-certificate-correctness-proof.txt establishes that
forward implication from the cyclic-carrier and six-contact height
inputs without the new pointwise converse. The reverse implication
uses pole-predicate completeness and remains subject to its arithmetic
dependency audit.

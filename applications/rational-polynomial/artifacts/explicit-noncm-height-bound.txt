An explicit non-CM constant in the five-contact height argument
2 November 2026

Scope

Fix the cover from explicit-height-cover.txt and a finite exceptional
prime set S for which the geometric specialization statements hold.
The set S must include the bad primes of that cover and family. This
note does not assert that a proposed list of those primes is complete.

Put
 n0 = 120667349406998676479016960000,
 D = n0! * 3^(256*(n0!)^4),
 G = |GL_4(F_5)| = 116064000000,
 E = 10*(D*G)^2.
Choose an integer R >= max(D, max(S), 10^12), and put Q=R^(R*E).

For every non-CM fiber A in the height argument, and
 M = M_S(s) >= 1,
the estimates below give
                 max(1,h_F(A)) <= Q^17 M^24.                  (1)

The height is the original stable Faltings height. This is a bound
for the abelian surface, not yet for the base coordinate. The
base-height comparison constant remains a separate input.

We use the geometric representation construction in the pinned source,
with coefficient primes 5 and 3 as in five-contact-height-audit.txt:
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/Hilberts-tenth-problem-over-the-rational-numbers-October-6-2026/build/sections/04-height.tex

1. A bound for local ramification breaks

Let F/Q_p be finite Galois with ramification index e. Write d_F
for its different exponent in the integral valuation on F. Then
                 d_F <= e-1+e*v_p(e).                         (2)

To see this, pass to the maximal unramified subfield F0. A uniformizer
pi of F has an Eisenstein minimal polynomial
 X^e + a_(e-1)X^(e-1) + ... + a_0
over F0. The different is generated by its derivative at pi.
The nonzero derivative terms have valuations congruent to i-1 modulo e,
for i=1,...,e, and are therefore distinct. There is no cancellation
at the least valuation. In particular that valuation is at most the
valuation e*v_p(e)+e-1 of the leading derivative term. The unramified
extension F0/Q_p contributes no different.

If b is the largest positive upper ramification break, the integral
form of the different formula is
 d_F/e = 1-1/e + integral_0^infinity (1-1/|Gal(F/Q_p)^u|) du.
For 0<u<b the nontrivial group has order at least two. Consequently
 b <= 2*d_F/e <= 2*(1+log_2(e)).

If a possibly non-Galois local extension has degree at most n, its
normal closure has degree at most n!. Thus its normal closure has
no upper ramification above
 2*(1+log_2(n!)) <= 2*(1+n^2) <= 4*n^2.                      (3)
The estimate is deliberately loose. An unramified extension has no
positive break and satisfies the same conclusion.

These uses of the different and the upper numbering are the usual
local-field formulas, also used in the source's conductor lemma.

2. Uniform control of the Tate action

The global Hom-field L in the construction is Galois of degree at most D.
At a rational prime q set F=L_v, so [F:Q_q]<=D. Choose p0=3 if q is
not 3, and p0=5 if q=3. Put F1=F(A[p0]). Then
 [F1:F] <= |GL_4(F_p0)| <= G,
 [F1:Q_q] <= D*G.

The Raynaud criterion gives semistable reduction over F1 because full
p0-torsion is rational, p0>=3, and p0 is different from q. A convenient
reference is Silverberg--Zarhin, Theorem 3.5:
https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_1995__45_2_403_0.pdf

For every coefficient prime ell different from q, inertia acts
unipotently on V_ell(A) over F1. A compact unipotent ell-adic group
has no nontrivial pro-q subgroup. The wild inertia of F1 therefore
acts trivially.

By (3), for u>U=4*(D*G)^2 the absolute upper ramification group over
Q_q lies in the absolute Galois group of the normal closure of F1
and in its wild inertia. The subgroup rule for upper numbering then
places it in the wild inertia of F1. It kills V_ell(A).

3. Uniform control of the scalar character

The representation construction has a cochain beta with beta^2=chi,
where chi is a Dirichlet character tame at odd primes and of conductor
dividing 4 at 2. Its restriction to G_F is a character.

At odd q, local reciprocity identifies its square on F-units with
chi composed with the norm. The norm of a principal unit is a
principal unit, so the square is trivial on U_F^1. The resulting
sign-valued character of this pro-q group is trivial. Thus beta has
relative upper break at most zero there.

At q=2, chi on Z_2-units has order at most two, so beta has order at
most four on O_F-units. Put e_F=v_F(2)<=D. The logarithm identifies
U_F^(e_F+1) with the additive ideal of that exponent, and hence
                 (U_F^(e_F+1))^4 = U_F^(3e_F+1).
It follows that beta kills U_F^(3e_F+1). Its conductor exponent is
at most 3e_F+1 and its relative upper break at most 3e_F.

For u above the breaks of F/Q_q, the relative upper index is the
Herbrand inverse psi_(F/Q_q)(u), which is at least u. Therefore
u>U also kills beta. On this group the conjugate quasi-isogeny in
the representation formula is the identity.

The two-dimensional representation r_ell is consequently trivial
on every upper ramification group with u>U. Its Swan conductor is
at most 2U. The remainder of its full conductor, including the
monodromy contribution, is at most its dimension two. Thus
                 a_q(r_ell) <= 2+2U <= E.                    (4)

Use ell=5 at q!=5 and ell=3 at q=5. Compatibility identifies these
conductors with the exponents in the level of the same weight-two
newform. The prime-five modularity replacement is explained in
five-contact-height-audit.txt.

4. The level and the field of isogeny

If q>R, then q>D and q is outside S. Wild inertia cannot act
nontrivially on L, since its finite image is a q-group whose order
divides [L:Q]<=D. The surface has good reduction over L at q.
The square of beta is tame, so its wild restriction is again
sign-valued and trivial. Thus r_ell is tame at q and its conductor
exponent is at most two.

It is unramified outside S and the prime divisors of M. Combining
this with (4) gives the explicit level bound
 N <= (product over primes q<=R of q^E) M^2
   <= R^(R*E) M^2 = Q M^2.                                (5)

Let m_chi denote the order of chi, not the auxiliary level of the
Shimura curve. The sign-splitting construction gives
 m_chi <= 2 product_(q in S, q!=2)(q-1) M <= 2 R^R M.
Since L' is obtained by killing beta restricted to G_L,
 [L':Q] <= 2 m_chi D <= 4D R^R M <= Q M.                    (6)
The last inequality holds since R>=D, R>=10^12, and E>=10.

5. The modular Jacobian

The representation and Faltings' Hom theorem give an isogeny
 J=J_1(N) ~ A x D0 over L'.
In particular g0=dim(J)>=2. The index of Gamma_1(N) in SL_2(Z)
is at most N^2. Therefore both the degree of the Belyi map obtained
from j and g0 are at most N^2. Javanpeykar's Theorem 1.1.1 gives
 h_F(J) <= 13000000*g0*(Belyi degree)^5
        <= 13000000*N^12.
Reference:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.6404

Use Zarhin's trick on the complement, as in the source, to obtain
 C1=J^8 ~ C2=A^8 x DZ,
where DZ is principally polarized. Let g=dim(C1) and d=[L':Q].
Equations (5) and (6), and Q>=10^12, give
 g <= 8 Q^2 M^4 <= Q^3 M^4,
 d <= Q M,
 h_F(C1) <= 104000000 Q^12 M^24 <= Q^13 M^24.                (7)

6. Quantitative isogeny and final bound

Gaudron--Remond's Theorem 1.4 gives an isogeny C1 -> C2 with
 log(deg phi) <= 1024 g^3 [
  64g^2 log(14g) + log d
  + 2 log max(h_F(C1),log d,1)].
Reference:
https://lmbp.uca.fr/~gaudron/art13.pdf

For M>=1, use log M <= M^4/4 and log Q <= Q. From (7),
 log(14g) <= 5 Q M^4,
 log d <= 2 Q M^4,
 log max(h_F(C1),log d,1) <= 19 Q M^4.
The bracket is at most
 320 Q^7 M^12 + 40 Q M^4 <= 360 Q^7 M^12.
Consequently
 log(deg phi) <= 368640 Q^16 M^24 <= Q^17 M^24.              (8)

The lower bound in the source's Faltings-height convention is
 h_F(DZ) >= -(dim DZ)/2 * log(2*pi^2) >= -2g.
Height variation under isogeny, (7), and (8) now imply
 8h_F(A)
 <= Q^13 M^24 + (1/2)Q^17 M^24 + 2Q^3 M^4
 <= Q^17 M^24.
Since Q^17 M^24>=1, this proves (1).

7. Combination with the other height inputs

Let Cbase be a verified constant for
 h(s) <= Cbase*(1+max(0,h_F(A))),
in the explicit base coordinate used by the certificates.
Let C_CM(D) be the explicit CM surface bound from
effective-cm-height-bound.txt. Then one permissible choice is
 H_S = 2*Cbase*max(1,C_CM(D),Q^17),
and
                 h(s) <= H_S M_S(s)^24.

A numerical choice still requires Cbase and a certified exceptional
set S. Once they are fixed, finite-guard-bound.txt supplies
 T=(48*ceil(max(1,H_S)))^13.

This note removes the unspecified local conductor, isogeny-field,
modular Jacobian, and non-CM isogeny constants. It does not claim to
have fixed the remaining geometric comparison constant or assembled
the final universal polynomial. These are source-level arguments,
not a Lean formalization.

Ordinary integer inputs from an elliptic Diophantine model
2 November 2026

Status
------
The argument below proves an implication. If the cyclic elliptic model
has Diophantine multiplication over Q, then a fixed existential formula
connects ordinary nonnegative integer inputs to their elliptic codes.
It removes the extra input-bridge assumption from the earlier route.
It does not prove the arithmetic assumptions used to construct that
model, and is not an unconditional universal equation or a Lean proof.

Hypotheses and notation
----------------------
Let F=Q(sqrt(2)), let
  E: y^2=x^3-d^2 x,  d=75-53 sqrt(2),
and let P in E(F) have infinite order. Suppose D is a Diophantine subset
of Q^s with a computable bijection
  iota: Z -> D,  iota(k)=the fixed coordinate code of [k]P,
for which addition and multiplication of the indices are Diophantine.
Use a flag for O, with unused coordinates zero, and the rational
components of affine x and y elsewhere. The earlier conditional
finite-error construction is intended to supply this hypothesis.

All Diophantine definitions in this note use fixed rational coefficients
and finitely many rational witnesses. Fixed denominators can be cleared.
Let D_N be the codes of the nonnegative integers. It is Diophantine:
inside the model, a nonnegative integer is a sum of four integer squares.

1. Computable relations can be interpreted on the model
-----------------------------------------------------
If S is a recursively enumerable subset of N^r, then
  {(iota(n_1),...,iota(n_r)): (n_1,...,n_r) in S}
is Diophantine over Q.

Proof. DPRM gives an integer polynomial representing S with finitely
many nonnegative integer witnesses. Interpret every integer variable by
a variable in D_N, every operation by the assumed Diophantine graph,
and each of the finitely many integer constants by its fixed code.
All new witnesses are rational. Compose the finite existential formulas.
For an integer polynomial with negative coefficients, move its negative
terms to the other side before using nonnegative arithmetic. This proves
the assertion without a conversion of variable field elements to codes.

2. A rational-valued function approximating an index
--------------------------------------------------
Put z(Q)=-x(Q)/y(Q) for Q!=O and z(O)=0. For a nonzero multiple of P,
both x and y are nonzero: x=0 or y=0 would make the point two-torsion
on this curve. In particular z([h]P)!=0 when h>=1.

For H!=O and J in the cyclic group define
  rho(H,J) = (1/2) Tr_{F/Q}(z(J)/z(H)) in Q.

For every fixed k>=0 there exists h>=1 with
  |rho([h]P,[kh]P)-k| < 1/8.                         (A)

Proof. At both real embeddings of F the elliptic curve has a compact
real point group. Put g=(P,P^sigma) in the product of these two groups.
There are positive integers h_j tending to infinity with h_j g -> 0.
Here is a compactness proof: for any identity neighborhood U choose V
with V-V contained in U and cover the compact group by finitely many
translates of V. Among sufficiently many orbit points, two fall in the
same translate, so a positive multiple of g lies in U. To force that multiple
above any given bound, shrink U to exclude the finitely many positive
multiples up to that bound. None is zero because P has infinite order.
Applying this to a shrinking sequence of neighborhoods gives h_j.

The function z is a local parameter at O. The multiplication map [k]
has local expansion
  z([k]Q)=k z(Q)+O(z(Q)^2).
Thus z([k]Q)/z(Q) tends to k as Q tends to O, Q!=O, at either real
embedding. For k=0 the ratio is identically zero. The normalized trace
of the two ratios tends to k. Taking h=h_j for sufficiently large j
proves (A). No effective rate of recurrence is used or required.

3. A decidable accuracy relation on indices
-----------------------------------------
Define S subset N^3 by

 (k,h,j) in S iff h>=1, j=kh,
                  |rho([h]P,[j]P)-k|<1/8.           (B)

This relation is decidable. Given k,h,j, check the two integer conditions,
compute the two points by the exact algebraic group law, compute rho
in rational arithmetic, and compare rational numbers. For rho=u/v with
v>0, the last check is the integer inequality 8|u-kv|<v.
The procedure uses the fixed exact coordinates of P, not a table of
values or an oracle. It never invokes a Diophantine input-to-code map.

By section 1, there is a fixed Diophantine relation Acc(A,H,J) on D_N^3
which holds exactly when A=iota(k), H=iota(h), J=iota(j) and (B) holds.
By section 2, every A=iota(k), k>=0, has at least one such pair H,J.

The analytic limit proves this last existence assertion. The predicate
Acc itself uses an exact terminating integer algorithm, so no analytic
limit or infinitely many inequalities occur in its existential formula.

4. The ordinary-input bridge
---------------------------
Define Gamma(a,A) over Q by

 exists H,J,r:
   Acc(A,H,J) and r=rho(H,J) and |a-r|<1/8.           (C)

For every a in N and every A in D,
  Gamma(a,A) iff A=iota(a).                          (D)

Proof of completeness. Set k=a. Choose h from section 2, take
H=iota(h), J=iota(kh) and r=rho(H,J). Both conditions in (C) hold.

Proof of soundness. Acc supplies a nonnegative integer k with A=iota(k)
and |r-k|<1/8. The last condition in (C) then gives |a-k|<1/4.
Since a and k are integers, a=k.

For A outside D, Acc is false. Nothing is claimed about exact integrality
of a when a is an arbitrary rational number. In particular this does
not supply a Diophantine definition of Z inside Q.

5. All operations in (C) have finite polynomial encodings
--------------------------------------------------------
Write z(H)=s_0+s_1 sqrt(2), z(J)=t_0+t_1 sqrt(2). Then
  D_H=s_0^2-2s_1^2 != 0,
  rho(H,J)=(t_0 s_0-2t_1 s_1)/D_H.
Introduce u and impose
  u D_H-1=0,
  r D_H-(t_0 s_0-2t_1 s_1)=0.
The equations z=-x/y are encoded by their two rational components,
with the nonzero-y guard for affine points. At J=O set t_0=t_1=0.
There are finitely many charts. These give a polynomial T(H,J,r,eta)
whose rational solvability in eta is exactly r=rho(H,J) on the domain
specified by Acc. All denominators and chart conditions are retained.

The strict inequality in (C) has this polynomial encoding:
  (1-64(a-r)^2)(q_1^2+q_2^2+q_3^2+q_4^2)-1=0.      (E)
Indeed (E) forces its first factor to be strictly positive. Conversely,
if that factor is positive, its reciprocal is a sum of four rational
squares. For any positive rational b/c with positive integer b,c, write
bc as a sum of four integer squares and divide their four bases by c.

Let C_S(A,H,J,w)=0 be a polynomial encoding Acc, including its domain
conditions. One fixed polynomial encoding Gamma is therefore

 G(a,A,H,J,r,w,eta,q) = C_S(A,H,J,w)^2
                    + T(H,J,r,eta)^2
                    + ((1-64(a-r)^2) sum_{i=1}^4 q_i^2-1)^2.

Clear the fixed rational coefficients, if any. Every witness in G is
a rational variable, and all variable blocks have fixed finite length.

6. Conditional universal equation with ordinary integer parameters
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let V(A,B,v)=0 represent the ordinary halting relation on the model:
  exists v in Q^r: V(iota(e),iota(n),v)=0
       iff program e halts on n,
for e,n in N. Section 1 supplies V from DPRM and a fixed effective
enumeration of programs.

With disjoint witness blocks g_e and g_n for G, put

 U(e,n,y) = V(A,B,v)^2 + G(e,A,g_e)^2 + G(n,B,g_n)^2. (F)

Here A,B,v,g_e,g_n are all included in y. Formula (D) and the sum of
squares show, for ordinary integer e,n>=0, that

  exists y in Q^N: U(e,n,y)=0 iff program e halts on n.

The variable count N is fixed. Formula (F) is a construction from the
assumed model definitions, not an expanded coefficient list. A finite
arithmetic-circuit encoding introduces variables for intermediate sums
and products and then sums the squares of quadratic gate equations.
Thus the conditional construction can also have total degree at most
four, at the cost of more rational variables. No bound of 86 variables
or explicit numerical variable count is asserted.

What is and is not completed
---------------------------
The ordinary-input bridge is now a proved consequence of the precise
Diophantine-model hypothesis above, rather than an extra conjectural
assumption. The earlier nonuniform height bound remains a valid reason
that its particular finite-guard argument did not prove this bridge.
It does not obstruct the different approximation argument here.

To turn (F) into the requested unconditional equation, the arithmetic
construction of the model still needs complete verification, and its
fixed constants and polynomial definitions must be instantiated. In the
current route this includes the five-contact height input, pole-parity
completeness, and the finite-certificate theorems used for multiplication.
An alternative conditional certificate using linear prime patterns and
separate local witness groups is recorded in linear-prime-certificate.txt.
It retains the height and pole-parity dependencies.

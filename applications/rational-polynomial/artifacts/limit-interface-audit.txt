Limit-interface audit for the pointwise 2-converse
2 November 2026

Result and scope
----------------
This note verifies the model, character, residual-rank, evaluation, and
finite-realization arguments in ring-limits.tex, using the standard
arithmetic inputs stated below. It gives an explicit coarse rank bound.
The actual Heegner classes and their local boundary witnesses are
provided by finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt.

This is a written proof audit, not a Lean formalization. It does not
verify the later determinant-switch identity, weighted-theta identities,
or the construction of all binary families. Those remain necessary for
the full pointwise 2-converse.

Standard inputs used
--------------------
* Kummer theory, Dirichlet's unit theorem, and finiteness of class groups.
* Local Tate duality and Poitou--Tate/Nekovar duality for the stated
  Selmer cones over totally imaginary number fields.
* The open-image theorem for non-CM elliptic curves, the open Cartan
  image theorem for CM curves, and Bogomolov's scalar theorem.
* Finite Chebotarev and the ring-class exact sequence.
No rank-one converse or finiteness of Sha is an input.

1. Finite free models with actual cochain maps
----------------------------------------------
Let A be Artin local, with maximal ideal m and residue field k, and let
D be a complex of flat A-modules whose residual cohomology is finite and
bounded. Flat modules over A are free even in infinite rank: lift a
basis modulo m, use nilpotence to make the resulting map surjective,
then use flatness and nilpotence to show its kernel is zero.

Split the residual complex into its cohomology and contractible disks.
Lift the graded bases and disk contraction. Write d=d_0+epsilon, where
epsilon has coefficients in m. The inverses of 1+h_0 epsilon and
1+epsilon h_0 are finite geometric sums. The perturbation formulas give
  d_C=p_0 epsilon(1+h_0 epsilon)^-1 i_0,
  i=(1+h_0 epsilon)^-1 i_0,
  p=p_0(1+epsilon h_0)^-1,
  h=h_0(1+epsilon h_0)^-1.
They satisfy pi=1 and 1-ip=dh+hd. Nilpotence is uniform, so infinite
disk sums do not produce an infinite convergence problem.

Continuous cochains with a finite free coefficient module are flat:
locally constant functions form the filtered union of free modules of
functions on finite clopen partitions. Thus the construction applies
to the global and local cochains in question and to their cones.

Transport a map f by p'fi and retain the homotopies obtained from h.
Transport cycles and boundary witnesses after the finite arithmetic
transfer has been performed. These are actual cochain maps and
identities; a list of abstract cohomology groups would be insufficient.

2. Fixed-ultrafilter limits and extending a diagram
--------------------------------------------------
Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter on the original finite stages.
Bounded graded ranks are constant on a large set. At any fixed
coefficient precision, the rings and all bounded matrices are finite,
so each entry has a unique ultrafilter limit. The limits are compatible
as precision increases. Every specified matrix identity is polynomial
in finitely many entries and therefore survives.

An additional object is compared to the existing models through their
original cochain contractions. This extends the diagram without changing
its old entries. Reducing the whole diagram to a slower cofinal
precision also preserves its limits. Choosing an unrelated later
ultrafilter or unrelated cohomology models would not justify this
claim; neither operation is needed.

For a fixed family, the target ranks are bounded before taking the
limit. Its support consists of fixed primes and a bounded number of
moving primes. Over the fixed field K(E[2]), the S-unit rank and the
fixed class-group 2-rank bound residual H^1; global cohomological
dimension and Euler characteristic bound the other degrees. The
degree of the ring-class source field is irrelevant because the
source cochain complex is not retained.

3. The Kummer local condition includes degree zero
--------------------------------------------------
For a nonarchimedean local field F of characteristic zero put
  M=E(F)^wedge_2.
This is a finitely generated Z_2-module, with torsion E(F)[2^infinity].
The integral Kummer map defines
  U=M[-1] -> R Gamma(F,T_2 E).
The vanishing of H^0(F,T_2 E), which follows from the bounded local
torsion exponent, permits this map through the degree-one truncation.

A length-one free resolution of M shows that its derived reduction
modulo 2^n has
  H^0(U mod^L 2^n)=E(F)[2^n],
  H^1(U mod^L 2^n)=E(F)/2^n E(F).
The maps are respectively the full invariant inclusion and the usual
Kummer injection. Thus finite degree-zero invariants are retained.

The cup pairing on U with itself is zero in the derived category.
After canceling the common shift, a map M tensor^L M -> Z_2 is
determined by M tensor M -> Z_2; the Tor term in degree -1 supplies no
additional degree-zero map to this target. The remaining map vanishes
by finite-level local Tate orthogonality. A nullhomotopy in free
models gives the map from the local quotient to the shifted dual of U.
Modulo 2 this is a quasi-isomorphism by the perfect pairing of the
Kummer quotient with the Kummer image, including the dual of H^0.
Derived Nakayama proves the integral assertion.

At a derivative prime impose trivial coefficient action to the retained
precision and ell^2=1 mod 2^(a+1). The finite and tame scalar characters
have zero squares: the local field contains the required roots of
unity and -1 is a 2^a-th power, so the Hilbert-symbol identity
(x,x)=(x,-1) applies. Their cross pairing is a unit. In the split free
local model, the finite and singular planes are therefore exactly
isotropic, also at 2. Retaining degree zero gives the strict, relaxed,
finite, and singular conditions stated in the source.

These local orthogonality data are the hypotheses needed by global
Selmer duality. They explain why an ordinary filtration at 2 is not
required. Global duality is used on the actual finite cones; it is not
being invoked as a theorem about an unspecified varying-prime limit.

4. Moving ring-class characters and the trace bound
---------------------------------------------------
The character construction is compatible with arbitrarily many fixed
oriented split primes. Choose enough additional split prime ideals
to generate Cl(K). Give the prescribed odd primes weight 1, primes
over 2N weight 0, and the additional primes weight 0; conjugate weights
are negated. For a basis m^(a) of the relation lattice choose beta_a
with principal ideal product p_j^(m_j^(a)).

Over K(mu_{2^infinity}), prescribe the action on 2^n-th roots of
beta_a/bar(beta_a) by the exponent -sum_j m_j^(a)w_j.
If a product of these radicands is a 2^n-th power, its valuation at each
nonzero-weight prime is divisible by 2^n. These odd primes are unramified
in the cyclotomic extension, so the prescribed exponents respect every
Kummer relation. Weight-zero primes and units introduce no new weighted
condition. Kummer duality supplies the automorphism.

The split ring-class exact sequence then extends the primitive
power-residue character at a suitable split rational prime r to the
whole ring-class group with the chosen ideal values. This construction
gives zero local character at 2N and odd values at the prescribed primes.

The radical constraints do not force the Tate trace to be 2 or -2.
In the non-CM case the commutators of the determinant-one Tate image
contain a fixed open subgroup of SL_2(Z_2). Restriction to a quadratic
field changes the index by at most two, so this subgroup can be chosen
uniformly in K. The radical extension is abelian over the cyclotomic
field; hence those commutators fix every prescribed radical.

In the CM case work over the compositum with the CM field. Choose gamma
with cyclotomic value 625, a fourth power and therefore in the
cyclotomic image of this extension of degree at most four. For u fixing
the cyclotomic field, the element gamma u gamma^-1 u^-625 fixes the
radicals and has Tate action rho(u)^-624. Powers by the fixed nonzero
integer 624 map an open norm-one Cartan subgroup onto an open subgroup.
This gives the same uniform conclusion.

There are finitely many Tate cosets modulo the resulting fixed open
subgroup. In each choose a trace different from 2 and -2; trace cannot
be identically one of those two values on an open SL_2 or norm-one
Cartan coset. On the other Cartan-normalizer component trace is zero.
The maximum of the finitely many valuations of trace plus or minus 2
is a constant depending only on E.

Finite Chebotarev now imposes both the radical conditions and this Tate
condition, together with arbitrarily high r=1 mod 2^j. Since
det(1 plus or minus Fr_r)=1 plus or minus a_r+r, the same fixed bound
controls these determinants. Every stage uses a finite extension.

5. Removing active primes after residual localization
------------------------------------------------------
Modulo 2 the quadratic character disappears. At an active split prime,
the scalar Frobenius is z=(1+t)^a with a odd. Its linear coefficient is
1, so it cannot be algebraic over the finite constant field: an equation
z^(2^n)=z would contradict that coefficient. Thus the determinants of
zB-1 and q^-1 zB-1 are nonzero for B in GL_2(F_2).
Both the unramified and singular local complexes become acyclic over
F_2((t)). At the moving prime r, inertia minus one is t times a unit
and its local complex is likewise acyclic.

Consequently the active primes can be removed from the residual global
support after t is inverted. Retain the localization triangles before
taking the limit, so this is an assertion about the actual complexes.
The smaller support consists only of 2N, r, and the derivative primes.
Its generic F_2((t))-cohomology is bounded by its t=0 fiber, using a
minimal free model over F_2[[t]].

6. An explicit coarse bound for the residual ranks
-------------------------------------------------
Let F=Q(E[2]), f=[F:Q]<=6, and
  c_F=dim_F2 Cl(F)[2].
Let v=omega(2N), let k be the number of derivative primes, and assume
omega(disc K)<=A. Put
  s=v+A+1+k,     D=f(s+1)+c_F,     J=2(v+1+k).

Enlarge the allowed rational primes to include disc K. There are at
most s of them and at most fs primes of F above them. The Kummer
S-unit exact sequence bounds the generator number of the maximal
pro-2 Galois group over F by
  r_1(F)+r_2(F)+fs+c_F <= D.
Indeed, outside S a quadratic radicand must have even valuations;
its half-divisor gives a class in Cl(O_{F,S})[2], and the kernel
is O_{F,S}^*/O_{F,S}^{*2}. Dirichlet's theorem gives the stated rank.

The subgroup for FK has index at most two, so Schreier bounds its
generator number by 2D-1. It is the required maximal pro-2 group:
the Galois closure over F of a finite Galois 2-extension of FK is
again a 2-extension, and introduces no primes outside the enlarged set.

The residual module E[2] is trivial over FK and has dimension two.
Thus its H^1 there has dimension at most 4D-2. The group Gal(FK/K)
is a subgroup of S_3 and is generated by at most two elements.
A crossed cocycle to a two-dimensional module is determined by its
values on those generators, so its H^1 has dimension at most four.
Inflation-restriction gives
  dim H^1(K,E[2]) <= 4D+2
with the required ramification restriction. Passing back to the smaller
allowed support only decreases this upper bound.

Global H^0 has dimension at most two. For the totally imaginary
quadratic field K, the global Euler characteristic is -2, so H^2
has dimension at most H^1. The total global residual cohomology
dimension is therefore at most 8D+6.

There are at most J relevant finite places of K. At each, local Tate
duality bounds H^0 and H^2 by two; the local Euler characteristic
bounds H^1 by eight, even at 2 where [K_w:Q_2]<=2.
Thus each full local complex has total cohomology dimension at most 12.
The allowed local conditions have the same coarse bound: Kummer
conditions, finite or singular planes, and strict or relaxed conditions
all satisfy it. The long exact sequence of the Selmer cone now gives
the total bound
  R(E,A,k)=8D+6+24J.                                  (13)

This bounds the total rank of a minimal model over O after completion
at (2). Crucially, the number of active primes does not occur.

For the application with full rational two-torsion, F=Q and c_F=0.
Equation (13) simplifies to the valid coarse bound
  R(E,A,k)=56 omega(2N)+8A+56k+70.                     (14)

This is a rank bound, not a bound on the exponent or length of
arithmetic torsion. No such stronger conclusion is inferred.

7. Evaluation on every group sequence
--------------------------------------
Keep the original cochain contractions at each stage. Evaluating them
on any g=(g_i) and pair (g_i,h_i) gives integral matrices e^0,e^1_g,e^2_{g,h}.
Their limits exist for each choice under the same fixed ultrafilter.
The cochain identities give
  e^1_g d=(rho(g)-1)e^0,
  e^2_{g,h}d=rho(g)e^1_h-e^1_{gh}+e^1_g.
They remain identities after coefficient extension. Hence every
cycle, including one arising only after extension, gives a crossed
homomorphism on the group of all sequences.

For injectivity choose residual representatives of H^1 and consider
the linear evaluations
  (c,m) |-> b_c(g)-(rho(g)-1)m.
The intersection of all kernels consists exactly of c=0 and invariant
m. In a finite-dimensional vector space, at most dim H^1+dim M of
these tests already give that intersection.

The resulting map to the two-term evaluation complex is an isomorphism
on residual H^0 and an injection on residual H^1. Its cone has zero
residual cohomology in degrees at most zero. Cancel its unit disks;
the remaining free cone starts in degree one and still does so after
every coefficient extension. This proves injection on H^1 for any
field receiving the coefficient ring. A cocycle that is a coboundary
on all sequences is in particular one on the detecting list.

The finite list proves injectivity; it does not replace evaluation
on other sequences. All-sequence evaluation still comes from the
original cochain maps.

8. Restriction to the exact common kernel
-----------------------------------------
Let H consist of sequences acting trivially at each stage on the full
Tate module, every ring-class field, and the quadratic characters used.
It is an exact kernel, not just a set of actions tending to the identity.

The element z from section 1 of finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt is
central modulo H and acts by u^4 on the coefficients. If a cocycle
vanishes on H, it descends to the quotient and satisfies
  (u^4-1)b(g)=(g-1)b(z).
At characteristic-zero tests, division by the nonzero u^4-1 makes it
a coboundary. Its valuation depends only on E.

At the residual test use instead the sequence of inertia generators
at r. It is central modulo H, is exactly Tate-trivial, and acts by
1+t. The same argument divides by t, which is a unit in F_2((t)).
This proves the residual restriction injection too.

9. The outer DVR and approximate cycles
---------------------------------------
For an outer ultrafilter, take the ultraproduct of the completed
coefficient DVRs and quotient by elements whose valuations exceed
every fixed integer. Every nonzero element has a single finite
valuation on an ultrafilter-large set and is 2^n times a unit.
Thus the quotient is a DVR with uniformizer 2 and characteristic-zero
fraction field.

Bounded integral matrices and all their identities pass to this
quotient. An outer cycle need not lift to exact inner cycles:
representatives w_j need only have d_j w_j tending to zero in valuation.
The integral degree-two evaluation matrices imply that their evaluated
coboundaries also tend to zero. Hence the resulting outer evaluations
are still crossed cocycles.

Choose detecting lists over the inner residue fields, with the bounded
length provided by (13), and contract their cones using unit pivots.
These contractions are integral and survive the quotient. They prove
outer injectivity exactly as in section 7. The common scalar u^4-1
from section 8 has fixed finite valuation, so exact-kernel injection
survives as well.

The Tate representations of quadratic subgroups have only finitely
many possible image subgroups of index at most two in the fixed
compact 2-adic analytic image. After fixing one on a large set, a finite
list of Tate matrices spans M_2(Q_2); its nonzero spanning determinant
has fixed finite valuation and survives the quotient. Scalar character
weights are units and preserve this property. Moving inertia separates
the two induced summands, since 1+t has infinite order even modulo 2.
This proves the required characteristic-zero induced irreducibility,
with the stated exclusion of the CM field.

10. Finite Chebotarev realization preserves the old diagram
----------------------------------------------------------
At a finite Artin stage, all finitely many cochain coordinates in use
are locally constant. They factor through one finite Galois quotient,
enlarged to include the required Tate precision, ring-class fields,
and old finite data. For an exact-kernel element u, finite Chebotarev
realizes the conjugacy class of tau u. Choosing a place realizes the
desired representative. Its prime is inert in K, avoids the old
support, and has rational Frobenius tending to J.

On the K-prime the finite localization is evaluation at (tau u)^2.
The same procedure works for approximate outer cycles by keeping the
coefficient precision below the valuation of their coboundary.
Everything is done at the original stages, using the original
contractions and the same inner and outer ultrafilters. The old
diagram is therefore unchanged.

In induced-module notation u acts trivially on the coefficients and
tau^2=1. The crossed identity gives
  b((tau u)^2)=(1+tau)b(u).
Projection of im(1+tau) to either induced summand is an isomorphism
onto the two-dimensional coefficient module, including in characteristic
two. This is the evaluation used in rank reduction.

Assessment
----------
The finite-model, local-condition, moving-character, uniform-rank,
evaluation, exact-kernel, and finite-realization steps above provide
the limit interface used by the determinant argument. The rank bound
(14) is explicit for the rational-two-torsion application.

Finite-heegner-descent-audit.txt supplies the finite arithmetic classes
to which this interface is applied. Neither note proves the later
determinant-switch comparison or the weighted-theta and binary-family
steps. Subsequent supplements now check the determinant switch,
central specialization and weighted-theta odd detection; see
determinant-switch-audit.txt, central-specialization-audit.txt and
weighted-theta-audit.txt. The operator/trace and graph/assembly steps
remain. The restricted positive-rank theorem and the desired universal
rational polynomial have not been established by this audit.

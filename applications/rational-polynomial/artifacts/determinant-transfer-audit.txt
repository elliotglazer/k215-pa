An explicit algebraic audit of the determinant transfer
2 November 2026

Scope
-----
This note proves the algebraic clearing and transfer assertions used in
the pointwise 2-converse, subject to the stated inputs about actual
Selmer complexes and Heegner classes. It does not prove those arithmetic
inputs or the pointwise 2-converse itself. It is a written mathematical
proof, not a Lean formalization.

The principal new bound is
  c_* = a_0 + 4 h_1,
  L(M) = 16 m M,
where 2m+2 is the total rank of the bounded old complex. Here h_1 bounds
the torsion length of its central integral cohomology, and 2^a_0 clears
the fixed denominator of the dual functional. Neither bound depends on
the number of auxiliary primes.

1. Rings and Laurent expansions
-------------------------------
Let Lambda=Z_2[[t]], O=completion of Lambda_(2), and
G=(Z/2Z)^b. Put R=Lambda[G] and S=O[G].
A subscript x means evaluation at the sign character indexed by
x in F_2^b; subscript 0 is augmentation.

For M>=1,
  O/2^M O = (Z/2^M Z)((t)).
Indeed, a denominator outside (2) has the form t^d v+2w with v a
power-series unit. Its inverse modulo 2^M is the finite geometric
expansion around t^d v. Conversely inverting t supplies every Laurent
series with finite negative support. Passing to the inverse limit
identifies O with series sum a_n t^n, a_n in Z_2, for which
v_2(a_n) tends to infinity as n tends to minus infinity.
Its valuation is min_n v_2(a_n).

In particular, coefficient extraction gives a Z_2-linear map O -> Z_2
at every fixed Laurent degree. It commutes with all sign evaluations
of S, since G is finite.

2. Simultaneous elimination
--------------------------
Suppose A=diag(A_1,...,A_s) over Lambda, with each residual determinant
nonzero and ord_t(det A_j mod 2)<=h. Suppose B=A+2E over Lambda.
Write
  det A_j=t^{d_j}v_j+2w_j, d_j<=h,
and set C_j=adj(A_j)/(t^{d_j}v_j), C=diag(C_j).
Then BC=1+2F, and C and F have pole at most h. Hence
  B^-1 = C sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-2F)^j mod 2^M.
Every entry has pole at most Mh. Matrix multiplication adds pole orders;
summing an arbitrary number of terms cannot increase the maximum pole.
Thus the estimate is independent of s and of all block sizes.

The same estimate applies to P-Q B^-1 T when P,Q,T have entries in
Lambda. For a block over R whose augmentation is A, each sign evaluation
is A+2E_x. The block is invertible over S, since its determinant is a unit
after reduction modulo the maximal ideal (2, augmentation ideal) of S.
If T_0=0, the Schur complement has augmentation P_0.

This verifies rd:schur. In the stated application h=4, so the bounded
complex after simultaneous elimination has pole bound 4M at precision M.

3. Why the required graded model can be lifted
---------------------------------------------
This is a conditional algebra statement. Assume an actual bounded
finite free R-complex C has augmentation chain-homotopy equivalent
over Lambda to the asserted triangular old-plus-local complex T.

Over a local ring, a bounded finite free complex splits into a minimal
complex and elementary contractible disks. Cancel unit differential
entries in C over R first. Its augmentation is minimal over Lambda,
because the maximal ideal of R maps into the maximal ideal of Lambda.
Split T over Lambda into its minimal part and disks. A homotopy
equivalence between bounded minimal free complexes is a degreewise
isomorphism: reduce the homotopy identities modulo the maximal ideal,
where the differentials vanish, and use Nakayama's lemma.

Consequently, after adding to C the disks appearing in T, there is a
graded isomorphism between its augmentation and T which identifies
the differentials. Each degreewise invertible matrix lifts to an
invertible matrix over R, since the kernel of R -> Lambda lies in
the Jacobson radical. Changing bases gives an R-model with exactly
the asserted augmented differential. The old summand need not be a
subcomplex over R; only its augmentation is required to be one.

Thus the algebraic lifting used before the Schur complement is valid.
The hypothesis is a chain-level homotopy equivalence of the actual
arithmetic complexes. An equality of Euler characteristics would not
suffice and is not substituted for that hypothesis here.

4. An explicit bounded-complex clearing lemma
---------------------------------------------
Let D be a based bounded finite free S-complex. Suppose D_0 is defined
over Lambda, and let D_c=D_0 tensor_Lambda Z_2, where t maps to zero.
Assume the rational cohomology of D_c has dimension one in degrees
1 and 2 and zero elsewhere. Suppose the sum of the lengths of all
torsion cohomology groups of D_c is at most h_1.

Let r be the total graded rank of D; necessarily r=2m+2.
Let z be an integral degree-one cycle and let lambda be a closed
degree-two functional with 2^a lambda integral, a>=0 an integer.
Let eta be any element of S. On each sign constituent, define u_x
as eta_x times the coordinate of [z_x] tensor [lambda_x] in the
inverse determinant line when the generic cohomology is one in
degrees 1 and 2, and define u_x=0 when the complex is generically
acyclic. Assume these are the only generic patterns.

There exist f,d in S such that, for every x,
  f_x u_x=d_x,
  f_0 in Lambda,
  v_2(f_0(0)) <= a+4h_1.
If every entry of every D_x has pole at most J(M) modulo 2^M, then
every f_x has pole at most 4m J(M) modulo 2^M.

Proof.
A bounded free complex over the DVR Z_2 decomposes, in integral bases,
into elementary two-term complexes [Z_2 --2^a_j--> Z_2] and free
cohomology summands. This follows by splitting the saturated kernels
and putting each remaining injective map into Smith normal form.
The sum of the nonnegative a_j is the total torsion cohomology length.
There are precisely m elementary pairs and two free summands.

Lift these constant Z_2 basis changes to S. In degree i write the
resulting graded module as B_i direct-sum H_i direct-sum E_i, where
E_i maps to B_{i+1} at the central fiber. H_i has rank one for i=1,2
and zero otherwise. Let A_i be the square submatrix of the actual
differential D^i -> D^{i+1} from E_i to B_{i+1}. Put
  Delta_i=det A_i, Delta=product_i Delta_i.
An empty determinant or product is 1. Then Delta is a polynomial of
degree m in the differential entries, its augmentation is in Lambda,
and
  v_2(Delta_0(0))=sum_j a_j<=h_1.                 (1)

Work first after inverting Delta. An A_i-block can be canceled using
the matrices
  L = [ I       0 ]       R = [ I  -A_i^-1 B ]
      [-C A_i^-1 I ],         [ 0      I     ],
since
  L [ A_i B ] R = [ A_i          0        ].
    [ C   D ]     [  0   D-C A_i^-1 B    ]
Both basis changes have determinant one.

The differential preceding this one changes by R^-1 on its target,
and the following differential changes by L^-1 on its source.
The identity differential-squared=0 makes their components into the
canceled pair zero. Their other entries are unchanged. Therefore
canceling A_i does not change the surviving A_j-blocks for j!=i.
Every A_i can be canceled exactly once, using its original determinant.

The remaining complex is a map between two rank-one modules in
degrees 1 and 2. Each projected cycle or functional has denominator
dividing Delta. To see the denominator bound directly, each A_i^-1
is adj(A_i)/Delta_i, and each block is used at most once in composing
the projections. Thus no Delta_i is required more than once for
either projection. The factor 2^-a is kept separate.

The determinant comparison contributes
  product_i Delta_i^{(-1)^i},
up to an integral constant unit from ordering the bases. For example,
a disk in degrees 1 and 2 contributes Delta_1^-1 to the coordinate
of a cohomology element in the inverse determinant line. Multiplying
this comparison by Delta gives an integral polynomial. Thus there is
Q in S, obtained by multiplying the displayed numerators, such that
on every constituent with Delta_x!=0,
  u_x = 2^-a eta_x Q_x / Delta_x^3.             (2)
A fixed unit from the initial bases is absorbed into Q.

This expression also covers a generically acyclic constituent with
Delta_x!=0. There the remaining rank-one differential is nonzero,
so its degree-one cycle is zero over the fraction field. Consequently
the numerator Q_x is zero as required.

Take
  f=2^a Delta^4,     d=eta Delta Q.             (3)
Equation (2) proves f_x u_x=d_x when Delta_x!=0. When Delta_x=0,
both f_x and d_x are zero, so the same equality holds without any
division. This handles vanishing pivots separately, rather than
specializing a rational expression at an undefined denominator.

Equations (1) and (3) give the central bound a+4h_1. The polynomial f
has degree 4m in the differential entries, with coefficients integral
over Z_2, so its precision-M pole bound is 4m J(M). No bounds on z,
lambda, eta, or d are needed. This proves the lemma.

For the complex in section 2 with h=4, this gives
  c_*=a+4h_1,     L(M)=16mM.                    (4)
Since m=(r-2)/2, one may instead use L(M)=8(r_0-2)M if r<=r_0.

5. The unused-prime correction is integral
-----------------------------------------
The arithmetic input at an unused prime is the identity
  u_rel=e_q^2 u_prim/l_q,
with e_q,l_q units of S. The asserted Frobenius expressions satisfy
modulo 2
  (1+a_q z+z^2)(1+a_q z^-1+z^-2)
    =(a_q+z+z^-1)^2.
Thus l_q/e_q^2=1+2v_q for some v_q in S.
The single group-ring factor
  1+v_q(1+g_q)
evaluates to l_q/e_q^2 at an inactive character and to 1 at an active
character. Their product is integral regardless of the number of
primes. It is an allowed eta in section 4, so it does not enter f.

This verifies the algebra in rd:unused. It assumes, rather than
proves, the actual arithmetic formula for u_rel and the specified
Frobenius scalar and local factors.

6. Binary coefficient comparison
---------------------------------
A slight strengthening of the source bound is available. For q
specified Laurent coefficients of elements of S, precision M>=1,
and
  b > q(2^(M-1)-1),                            (5)
there is a nonzero sign address x at which all coefficients agree
with their augmented values modulo 2^M.

For one coefficient its sign-value function has the expansion
  A(x)=a_empty+sum_{I nonempty} 2^|I| a_I x_I,
where x_I=product_{i in I} x_i and a_I is in Z_2. The bit numbered j
is the coefficient of Y^(2^j) in (1+Y)^A(x) over F_2. A term involving
x_I in this generating function costs Y-degree at least 2^|I|.
Since |I|<=2^(|I|-1) for every nonempty I, the Boolean degree of bit j
is at most 2^(j-1) for j>=1. Bit zero is constant and imposes no test.
This follows first for nonnegative integer exponents and then by
continuity for Z_2 exponents.

All q coefficient congruences can therefore be imposed by Boolean
equations of total degree at most q(2^(M-1)-1). If this is less than b,
the product of one plus each equation, which indicates their common
zero set, has degree less than b. Every monomial omits a variable, so
its sum over F_2^b is zero in F_2. The common zero set has even size.
It contains 0, and hence also a nonzero address. This proves (5).
The source's larger threshold q(2^M-1) is consequently valid as well.

7. Transfer of the constant term
--------------------------------
Suppose f,d in S satisfy f_x u_x=d_x with u_x in Lambda[1/2],
f_0 in Lambda, and c=v_2(f_0(0)) finite. Suppose the precision-M
pole bound on all f_x is L(M), and M>c.
Test the coefficients of f from -L(M) through 0 and the constant
coefficient of d, a total of L(M)+2 tests. If
  b > (L(M)+2)(2^(M-1)-1),                    (6)
section 6 gives a nonzero x meeting all these congruences.

Every negative coefficient of f_x is divisible by 2^M. Its constant
coefficient has valuation c, so v_O(f_x)<=c. The equality f_x u_x=d_x
and integrality of d_x imply v_O(u_x)>=-c; also v_O(u_0)>=-c.
Since u_x,u_0 are in Lambda[1/2], they have no negative coefficients
and all their coefficients lie in 2^-c Z_2.

The constant-term convolution converges 2-adically, giving
  [t^0]d_x = [t^0]f_x u_x(0) mod 2^(M-c),
  [t^0]d_0 = f_0(0)u_0(0).
The two coefficient congruences then give
  v_2(u_x(0)-u_0(0)) >= M-2c.                 (7)
In particular the source's rd:binary-transfer is valid. Notice that
only one coefficient of d is tested; its negative pole support is
irrelevant.

Combining (4), (6), and (7), if every nonzero address has
v_2(u_x(0))<=B, choose an integer
  M>max(c_*, B+2c_*),
then any available family with
  b>(16mM+2)(2^(M-1)-1)
forces u_0(0)!=0. This is the algebraic contradiction needed for the
missing-vertex argument.

8. What this does and does not settle
-------------------------------------
The written proofs above verify the following algebraic steps with
explicit bounds:
* Laurent expansions and simultaneous Schur elimination.
* Lifting the augmented graded model, given its chain-level equivalence.
* Clearing bounded complexes, including constituents with zero pivots.
* Integrality of the unused-prime correction, given its local identity.
* Binary coefficient comparison and transfer of the constant term.

The remaining arithmetic obligations include constructing the stated
universal Selmer and Heegner diagrams with their actual specialization
maps, proving the local trace identities used in the correction,
producing the required binary families, and establishing the central
determinant comparison and noncentral upper bound. The weighted-theta
and limit arguments supplying these data are not proved by this note.

The torsion bound here concerns the finite quotient by the maximal
divisible subgroup of a fixed Selmer group. Finiteness of that quotient
does not assert finiteness of the entire Shafarevich--Tate group.

In particular this note does not exclude rank zero with a divisible
Q_2/Z_2 inside Sha for the restricted E_l family. Pole completeness,
the full five-contact height audit and its numerical constant, and
the final explicit universal polynomial remain unfinished.

This note checks nv:moment, nv:local-witness and nv:rough. The conclusion
is a source-level derivation relative to the classical analytic inputs
listed below, not a Lean formalization. It does not validate the remaining
cyclotomic and pointwise assembly steps.

The required conclusion has two distinct uniformity assertions:
* The number of prime factors in a positive-sign nonvanishing twist is
  bounded by one absolute integer A0.
* The minimum size of the twist may depend on the curve, the prescribed
  local classes and any finite set of forbidden primes.
The argument needs no uniform bound on that minimum size.

Classical inputs
----------------
Modularity and the functional equation for elliptic-curve twists;
Deligne's bound; the quadratic large sieve; symmetric-square continuation
and the Rankin--Selberg residue; analytic continuation and convexity for
finite-character twists of the fixed GL(2) cusp form; Waldspurger
nonnegativity; and the lower linear sieve.

Radziwill--Soundararajan, Proposition 2, supplies an independent model
for the central-value calculation with a prescribed squarefree divisor,
including an error O(X^(7/8+epsilon) u^(3/8) v^(1/4)):
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.7067
Their displayed progression modulus is lcm(8,N); the arbitrary enlarged
modulus used here must still be retained in the calculation. It is not
enough simply to quote their statement for a different modulus.
The derivative calculation below uses the same contour machinery, with
a double instead of a simple pole.

1. Fixing the local data
-----------------------
Fix the curve A, H divisible by 8N_A, a reduced residue class a modulo H,
and a sign sigma with sigma*a = 1 modulo 4. For positive squarefree
n = a modulo H, sigma*n is a fundamental discriminant coprime to 2N_A.
The root number is constant in this progression. Prescribed unit
squareclasses and the real sign are represented by a finite union of
such progressions; select one nonempty progression.

Adding a finite set of forbidden primes is accomplished by enlarging
H and taking a reduced CRT lift. This changes the constants, not the
absolute exponents in the estimates.

Let lambda(u) be the normalized coefficients, with center 1/2 and
|lambda(u)| <= tau(u). Put Q_A=sqrt(N_A)/(2*pi). For j=0 in sign +1,
and j=1 in sign -1, the symmetric approximate functional equation is

 L^(j)(1/2,f*chi_(sigma*n))
   = 2 sum_u lambda(u) chi_(sigma*n)(u) u^(-1/2)
                      V_j(u/(Q_A*n)),

 V_j(y) = (1/(2*pi*i)) integral_(2)
          exp(z^2) Gamma(1+z) y^(-z) dz/z^(j+1).

For j=1 the derivative of the completion adds no central-value term:
that value is zero by the negative sign.

2. Removing large square divisors
---------------------------------
The quadratic large sieve and the approximate functional equation give

 sum_(t<=Z, primitive discriminants) |L(1/2+delta+i*tau,f*chi_(sigma*t))|^2
    << Z^(1+rho) (1+|tau|)^C

for fixed delta>0 and any rho>0. The constants may depend on the fixed
form and small exponents. The polynomial exponent in tau is independent
of the level.

The treatment of square coefficient indices is essential. Write each
index as u*r^2 with u squarefree. Minkowski and the quadratic large sieve
give, up to arbitrarily small powers,

 sum_(r<=sqrt(U)) sqrt(Z+U/r^2)/r
     << (ZU)^epsilon (sqrt(Z)+sqrt(U)).

Restrictions (t,r)=1 may be discarded after absolute squares. Work
dyadically in t so the functional-equation length is
U <<_f T(1+|tau|)^C. On the line 1/2+delta the main coefficients improve
by n^(-delta); the dual coefficients cost at most U^delta but have the
prefactor T^(-2delta). Hence the dual term introduces no positive power
of T. Mellin separation handles smooth conductor-dependent weights.

Expand mu^2(n) by square divisors and truncate at d<=Y. For an omitted
term write n=t*v^2 with t squarefree. Necessarily v>Y, and the number of
omitted d is at most tau(v). The nonsquarefree approximate functional
equation equals a Mellin integral of the primitive twist L-function
with Euler factors at v deleted. On Re(z)=delta_M>0 those deleted
factors cost v^epsilon. Cauchy and the preceding second moment imply

 sum_(t<=2X/v^2) |L(1/2+delta_M+i*tau,f*chi_(sigma*t))|
     << (X/v^2)^(1+epsilon) (1+|tau|)^C.

Choose delta_M and every divisor/large-sieve exponent sufficiently small.
The resulting total tail is

             O(X^(1+rho) sum_(v>Y) v^(-2))
                  = O(X^(1+rho)/Y).

The extra 1/z in V_1 changes the constant only. A condition b|n can be
dropped in this absolute-value estimate, so the tail is uniform in b.

3. Poisson summation with the imposed divisor
--------------------------------------------
For d<=Y let J=lcm(d^2,b), with (J,H)=1, and c_J=a/J modulo H.
Separate a coefficient index as u=u1*q, where u1 is supported on H
and (q,H)=1. The value at u1 is fixed by the progression.

Poisson summation in n=J*m gives the prefactor 2X/(HJ) and q-summand

 lambda(q) q^(-3/2) (sigma*J*H/q)
 sum_(nu in Z) G_nu(q) e(nu*c_J*qbar/H)
 W_j(u1*q/X, nu*X/(HJ*q)),

where
 G_nu(q)=sum_(y mod q) (y/q)e(nu*y/q),
 W_j(z,R)=integral Phi(x)V_j(z/(Q_A*x))e(-R*x) dx.

The CRT identity producing the phase and the symbol is exact. If q has
a prime factor in common with J, the Jacobi symbol makes the term zero.
Thus no coprimality condition has silently been omitted.

4. Zero frequency and positivity of the main term
-------------------------------------------------
Only square q contribute when nu=0. With |alpha_p|=1 at p not dividing H,

 F_p^ev = sum_(r>=0) lambda(p^(2r))/p^r
        = (1+1/p)/[(1-alpha_p^2/p)(1-alpha_p^(-2)/p)] > 0,

 H_p = 1/p + (1-1/p)F_p^ev > 1/p.

The corresponding function of z factors as the symmetric-square
L-function at 1+2z times an absolutely convergent correction product
on a strip containing Re(z)>=-1/16. At a prime dividing J, omit the
correction factor and insert the inverse symmetric-square Euler
polynomial. Do not divide by H_p(z): its possible zeros cannot be
treated as harmless denominators.

These modified Euler factors have polynomial growth in J with an
absolute exponent. Shifting to Re(z)=-1/16 therefore gives an error

                    O(J^B1 X^(1-1/16))

for an absolute B1. The fixed factors supported on H are holomorphic
on this strip. Symmetric-square continuation also applies to CM forms;
the central value at 1 is positive by the Rankin--Selberg residue.

After summing the square-divisor residue, a prime outside Hb contributes
H_p-p^(-2), and a prime dividing b contributes p^(-1)-p^(-2). Their ratio is

 beta_p = (p-1)/(p^2 H_p-1) = 1/(1+p F_p^ev).

Consequently 0<beta_p<1 and beta_p=1/p+O(p^(-2)), with absolute implied
constant, since |lambda(p)|<=2. The fixed Euler factors are positive.
The residue of 1/z yields cX, c>0; the residue of 1/z^2 yields
cX log X+c1X, with the same positive leading coefficient.

Extending the square-divisor residue sum past Y costs
O(b^B2 X^(1+rho)/Y). The d^(-2+epsilon) tail remains summable after a
logarithmic factor, so this also justifies differentiation for j=1.

5. Nonzero frequencies and absolute exponents
---------------------------------------------
Normalize G_nu(q) by epsilon_q, equal to 1 or i according as q is
1 or 3 modulo 4. The resulting Gauss sum is multiplicative in q.
Expand the remaining periodic phase in Dirichlet characters modulo H.
The q-series is then

 D_(psi,J,nu)(s)
   = L(s,f*xi_prim) C_(psi,J,nu)(s),
 xi(q)=psi(q)(sigma*J*H*nu/q).

At p not dividing 2HJnu, the q-series local factor is
1+lambda(p)xi(p)p^(-s). Multiplying by the inverse GL(2) Euler polynomial
gives 1+O(p^(-2 Re(s))), with an absolute coefficient bound.

At a prime dividing nu, put a=v_p(nu). Direct finite Gauss sums give:
* for even r<=a, G_nu(p^r)=phi(p^r);
* for even r=a+1, G_nu(p^r)=-p^(r-1);
* for odd r=a+1, it is the primitive quadratic Gauss sum times p^(r-1);
* otherwise it is zero.
In every case |G_nu(p^r)/p^(r/2)|<=p^(r/2). At Re(s)>=3/4 its local
contribution is bounded by sum_(r>=0)(r+1)p^(-r/4), an absolute constant.
The inverse GL(2) Euler polynomial is also absolutely bounded there.
Thus the bad local factors cost at most (2J|nu|)^C with absolute C.

The conductor of xi_prim is at most 4HJ|nu|. The twisted cusp-form
L-function is entire, and convexity gives

 |D_(psi,J,nu)(3/4+i*tau)|
      <<_(f,H) (J|nu|)^C (1+|tau|)^C

after enlarging one absolute C. There is no pole crossed in this step.

Rapid decay and integration by parts in W_j restrict the effective sums to

 u1*q << X^(1+rho),  0<|nu| <<_H J X^(2rho),
 q >>_H X^(1-rho)/J.

These truncations can have negligible error with an absolute polynomial
loss in J. The large derivative orders needed when rho is small do not
force an unbounded J exponent: the cutoffs are scaled by J. For example,
the factor (Jq/X)^K in the nu-tail is paired with
(J X^(2rho))^(1-K), so the K-dependent J powers cancel. In the small-q
tail the analogous cancellation uses the cutoff X^(1-rho)/J.

For q around T, Mellin inversion expresses the sum as an integral of
T^s D(1+s) times the Mellin transform of a smooth weight. Shift to
Re(s)=-1/4. The bound is

              O((J|nu|)^C X^(C' rho) T^(-1/4)).

The u1-sum is absolutely convergent because u1 is supported on the fixed
finite set of primes dividing H. Sum the dyadic q ranges and nu:
including X/J, the bound becomes

                O(J^(2C+1/4) X^(3/4+O(rho))).

Choose rho small enough to replace the X exponent by 7/8. All exponents
are absolute; the curve and progression affect only constants.

6. Choosing the square-divisor cutoff
-------------------------------------
Because J<=b*d^2, the total error has the form

 O(b^B4 Y^(2B4+1) X^(1-kappa) + b^B4 X^(1+rho)/Y)

for absolute B4 and kappa>0. Take Y=X^delta with
(2B4+1)delta<kappa/2, and subsequently take rho<delta/4.
This leaves an absolute saving eta>0. It proves

 S_(0,b)(X)=cX product_(p|b) beta_p + O(b^B X^(1-eta))

and the stated derivative moment for b=1.

A numerical A0 is not extracted in this note. The needed assertion is
the existence of fixed absolute exponents B,eta, and the proof above
preserves that assertion. No uniform implied constant in f,H is used.

7. Sieve and consequences
-------------------------
In sign -1, cX log X+c1X with a power-saving error is nonzero in every
sufficiently large dyadic interval. At least one derivative is nonzero.
Together with the forced central zero this is a simple zero.

In sign +1, Waldspurger makes the summands nonnegative. Choose theta>0
with (B+1)theta<eta and D=X^theta. The sum of divisor remainders for
squarefree b<=D is O(D^(B+1)X^(1-eta))=o(X/log X).

The beta_p estimate gives dimension one:
 product_(w<=p<z, p not dividing H)(1-beta_p)^(-1)
 <= (log z/log w)(1+C_(f,H)/log w),
and the full product is comparable to 1/log z. The lower linear sieve
with z=D^(1/s), for any fixed s>2, therefore leaves positive weight.
A surviving n<2X has all prime factors at least z, hence

             omega(n) <= log(2X)/log(z).

Any fixed integer A0>2s/theta works for all sufficiently large X.
It is absolute. Since z tends to infinity, every prescribed finite
set of prime divisors is eventually avoided.

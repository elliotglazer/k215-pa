WEIGHTED THETA AND ODD DETECTION AUDIT
2 November 2026

Scope
-----
This note checks the construction and multiplicities in co:kummer,
co:theta-bridge, and co:odd of the pinned pointwise 2-converse. The
classical inputs are Faltings' Tate-module theorem, Bogomolov's scalar
theorem, finite Chebotarev, the Weil representation and theta
transformation, the CM reduction correspondence, and Gross--Zagier.
The local weights and their contribution to the coefficient are checked
below. This is not a Lean proof or a closure claim for the full converse.

Fix a negative odd fundamental discriminant h_* with L(E^(h_*),1)
nonzero. Its local classes agree with the positive odd-sign filter
at 2N. Keep its support fixed. For admissible positive squarefree D,
K_D=Q(sqrt(D h_*)) is imaginary and split at 2N. The parameter h_*
excludes the exceptional unit fields. Let P_D be the unaveraged genus
character sum of conductor-one Heegner points. The finite multiplier L
kills the specified cuspidal constants and bounded-degree torsion.

1. A finite-field functional that detects a fixed witness
-------------------------------------------------------
For any rational elliptic curve A, V_2(A) is irreducible over Q_2.
Faltings gives semisimplicity and
  End_{G_Q}(V_2(A))=End_Q(A) tensor Q_2=Q_2.
A reducible semisimple representation would have a nontrivial
idempotent in this endomorphism ring. The assertion includes CM,
because the nonreal CM endomorphisms are not defined over Q.

For nontorsion Q in A(Q), restrict its division cocycle to the kernel
H of the Tate action. Its image is a closed Z_2-submodule of T_2(A)
stable under G_Q. If its rational span were zero, the rational Kummer
class would factor through the Tate image. That image contains a
nonidentity central scalar z. The cocycle identity gives
  (z-1)c(g)=(g-1)c(z),
so such a class is a coboundary over Q_2. Kummer injectivity forbids
this for nontorsion Q. Irreducibility therefore makes the image a full
lattice. Intersecting H with the kernel of any fixed finite data still
has finite index and still has full-lattice image.

Choose an integral nonzero row b with bC=b, for complex conjugation C.
For the positive witness twist A=E^(D_w), complex conjugation has this
same action. If u in H has translation v, the square of a conjugation
lift times u has translation (1+C)v. Applying b gives 2b(v). The full
lattice permits a fixed finite nonzero valuation. Finite Chebotarev
then gives arbitrarily high precisions M and primes ell with
  Fr_ell = C modulo 2^M,
  (D_w/ell)=1, (h_*/ell)=-1,
and that fixed nonzero detection at the necessary extra precision.

Since Fr_ell^2=1 modulo 2^M, finite-field Kummer theory gives
  E(F_{ell^2})/2^M = T_2(E)/2^M
by evaluation at arithmetic Frobenius squared. Compose with b to obtain
lambda_M. It is integral and has Frobenius sign +1. Its value on
red_ell(LP_{D_w}) has bounded valuation independently of M.
Also ell=-1 modulo 2^M, by the determinant of C.

The compatibility of extra fixed field conditions with the conjugation
coset remains necessary. The argument does not prescribe arbitrary,
possibly inconsistent, Frobenius conditions.

2. The coefficient function on supersingular pairs
-------------------------------------------------
Take the definite quaternion algebra B ramified at ell and infinity,
an Eichler order R of level N, and
  X=B^times backslash B_hat^times / R_hat^times.
The elements of X are supersingular elliptic curves with cyclic
level-N subgroup. They are defined over F_{ell^2}. The reduction of the
fixed modular parametrization thus gives
  P_M(z)=lambda_M(L phi_ell(z)).
Choose integer lifts of these values.

The modular parametrization and its Hecke relations give Brandt
eigenvalue a_p(E) modulo 2^M for p away from N ell. Its Atkin--Lehner
eigenvalue is w_{p^e} at p^e||N after L kills the cusp constants.
The right ell-uniformizer acts as Frobenius on supersingular pairs,
so its eigenvalue on P_M is +1.

Only congruence eigenrelations are asserted. Arbitrary integer lifts
need not be characteristic-zero eigenvectors.

3. Local weights
----------------
Put V=B^0 and Q(x)=Nrd(x)=-x^2. Away from 2N h_* ell use the
characteristic function of R_p^0.

At p^e||N, the quadratic algebra is split on the allowed unit class.
An integral embedding acts on the cyclic level quotient through one
of its two roots, say r. Weight that root by a finite unit character
alpha_p with
  alpha_p(-1)=w_{p^e} chi_{h_*}(p)^e.
Either sign is attainable by a character of 2-power order: the
2-primary part of the unit group detects -1. Choose its depth once.
Order-unit conjugation preserves the selected quotient, and scaling
x by u multiplies the weight by alpha_p(u).

At 2 require (1+x)/2 integral and x^2 a unit square. When 2 divides N,
the eigenvalues of e_x=(1+x)/2 are (1+r)/2 and (1-r)/2. Their difference
is the odd unit r. Hence the two integral eigensummands remain distinct,
even modulo 2. For odd u,
  e_{ux}=u e_x+(1-u)/2.
This changes eigenvalues but preserves the eigensummands. The root
on the selected cyclic quotient is ur. Thus the scaling law remains
alpha_2(u), including u=-1 modulo 4. Selecting an eigenvalue merely
by its parity would have given the wrong rule.

At p|h_*, require v_p(Q(x))=1. Its reduction is a nonzero nilpotent.
For X_0=[[0,1],[0,0]], set
  epsilon_p(gX_0g^-1)=(det g/p).
The centralizer has matrices [[a,b],[0,a]], whose determinants are
squares. This makes epsilon_p well-defined on the whole nonzero
nilpotent orbit. Conjugation by u multiplies it by (det u/p).
Scaling by a multiplies it by (a/p), using diag(a,1).
Thus this weight has exactly the needed norm character.

At ell, the residue of an integral trace-zero element lies on the
trace-zero line in F_{ell^2}. Fix a basis beta of this line and give
t beta, t nonzero, weight (t/ell); give zero residue weight zero.
Order units act trivially on the residue field by conjugation.
A uniformizer acts by the nontrivial automorphism, sending beta to
-beta. Its weight is -1 because ell=-1 modulo 4.
Furthermore beta^2 and -1 are both nonsquares modulo ell, so
Q(t beta)=-t^2 beta^2 is a square. Since (|h_*|/ell)=1, after extraction
of indices |h_*|D this imposes (D/ell)=1.

All weights are locally constant and compactly supported. The full
weight f has right order-unit character
  f(u^-1 x u)=chi_{h_*}(Nrd u)^-1 f(x).
The product scaling character is even. At -1 its value is
  [product_{p^e||N} w_{p^e} chi_{h_*}(p)^e]
       chi_{h_*}(-1) (-1)=1.
The bracket is +1 because E^(h_*) has even functional sign; the last
two factors are both -1.

4. Transformation and uniform integrality
-----------------------------------------
The Weil representation sends an upper unipotent to multiplication
by the additive character of bQ(x), a diagonal unit to its Weil factor
times f(ux), and a Weyl element to Fourier transform.
Integral upper translations fix f. If f is constant on cosets of a
lattice L', its Fourier transform is supported on (L')^dual.
Sufficiently deep lower unipotents consequently fix f as well.

Relative to the ordinary theta multiplier cubed, the diagonal Weil
factor is the Hilbert symbol of det(Q). In a presentation
B=(-a,-b), Q has coefficients a,b,ab and determinant (ab)^2.
That factor is therefore trivial. The residual diagonal character is
precisely the computed scaling character. The local unipotent and
diagonal factorizations give a scalar transformation law at a level
supported on 2N h_* ell. The positive Gaussian gives weight 3/2 and
holomorphy. No level bound independent of ell is needed.

There is an explicit uniform choice for the stabilizer multiplier:
  c_0 = |GL_4(F_3)| = 24,261,120,       v_2(c_0)=9.
Indeed R_z^times is finite and acts faithfully on the rank-four
Z-lattice R_z by left multiplication. The principal congruence
subgroup of GL_4(Z) of level 3 is torsion-free. Its finite subgroup
therefore injects into GL_4(F_3), so |R_z^times| divides c_0.

Use the theta sum
  H_M(n)=c_0 sum_z P_M(z) chi_{h_*}(Nrd z)/|R_z^times|
                   sum_{x in B^0, Q(x)=n} f(z^-1 x z).
The right character cancels under a change of right representative.
Positive rational reduced norms give trivial global norm character
under a change of left representative. Thus the sum is well-defined.
Every c_0/|R_z^times| is an integer. Changing any lift of P_M(z) by
2^M changes every coefficient by an element of 2^M O. The stabilizer
denominators cause no loss of precision.

5. Good-prime recurrence
-----------------------
At a good prime p outside the weight support, Brandt neighbors are
the lines in F_p^2. A regular nonzero trace-zero matrix of norm n
has 1+(-n/p) invariant lines; a nonzero nilpotent has one; a zero
matrix has p+1. These are the three local counts in the ternary
theta Hecke identity.

For the division term, if a primitive trace-zero matrix y has p^2|Q(y),
choose its mod-p nilpotent invariant line. In a basis adapted to that
line, the diagonal entries are divisible by p, one off-diagonal entry
is a unit, and the determinant condition makes the other divisible
by p^2. Exactly this neighbor makes y/p integral. This verifies the
primitive exceptional term, rather than only the regular count.

Writing eta_B for the scaling character, the counts give
  H_M(p^2 n)+eta_B(p)(-n/p)H_M(n)
      +p eta_B(p)^2 H_M(n/p^2)
   =eta_B(p) chi_{h_*}(p) a_p(E) H_M(n) modulo 2^M.
The norm character contributes chi_{h_*}(p), and scaling contributes
eta_B(p). The same stabilizer weights appear in the neighbor count.

Extract a_M(n)=H_M(|h_*|n). Then
  eta=eta_B chi_{h_*},       psi=eta_B (|h_*|/ .),
so psi/eta=chi_{-1}, as required for the positive filter.
The recurrence is
  a_M(p^2 n)=[eta(p)a_p(E)-psi(p)(-n/p)]a_M(n)
                  -p eta(p)^2 a_M(n/p^2) modulo 2^M.
Extraction and the fixed squareclass sieves preserve integrality.
Their conditions are stable under multiplying an index by p^2.

6. Weighted CM comparison and the factor of two
----------------------------------------------
For n=|h_*|D, an element x counted by the theta series satisfies
x^2=D h_*. The condition (1+x)/2 integral is exactly the condition
that the maximal order of K_D embeds. The discriminant is fundamental,
so these integral embeddings are optimal.

At a level prime there are exactly two orientations. A stable lattice
splits into two integral idempotent summands; a cyclic quotient of
order p^e lies in exactly one summand. At ell there are two residue-field
orientations. At all other places there is one local embedding orbit.

For one full orientation tuple the embedding classes form a torsor
under Pic(O_{K_D}). If x_0 is a fixed rational embedding and z_0 one
representative, use representatives a z_0 with a in K_D_hat^times.
Since a centralizes x_0,
  (a z_0)^-1 x_0(a z_0)=z_0^-1 x_0z_0.
Thus the Schwartz weight is constant on the class orbit. The relative
norm weight is chi_{h_*}(Norm a), exactly the unramified genus character.
The CM reduction correspondence respects this class action and the
cyclic level subgroup.

The stabilizer of an embedding in R_z^times is O_{K_D}^times={+1,-1}.
Its orbit has |R_z^times|/2 elements. Division by |R_z^times| in the
theta sum therefore leaves a factor 1/2.

At p^e||N, orientation reversal multiplies the Schwartz weight by
alpha_p(-1)=w_{p^e}chi_{h_*}(p)^e. It multiplies the norm-weighted
parametrization value by the same sign. Their product is unchanged.
At ell, the two multipliers are both -1, again with product +1.
Hence all 2^{omega(N)+1} tuples have equal total contribution.
Together with 1/2 this gives exactly
  a_M(D)=c_0 2^{omega(N)} zeta_M(D)
                 lambda_M(red_ell(LP_D)) modulo 2^M,              (1)
where zeta_M(D) is a 2-power root of unity.

The conjugate-pair convention in Cornut--Jetchev's Corollary 4.1 matters.
It describes CM points using conjugate pairs of embeddings. Formula
(1) counts individual elements and explicitly includes both ell
orientations and the embedding stabilizer. One must not take a count
for conjugate pairs and then add or remove another factor of two.
The older Jetchev--Kane formulas are not a substitute for this weighted
orientation calculation.

There is no division by a class number or by the number of ramified
primes of D. The fixed scalar c_0 has the explicit value above.

7. Odd coefficient detection
----------------------------
Gross--Zagier identifies the height of P_D with the product derivative.
Since L(E^(h_*),1) is nonzero and the D-filter has odd sign, P_D is
nontorsion precisely when E^(D) has analytic rank one. At other
parameters P_D is torsion.

The points P_D lie in biquadratic fields. Their torsion has a uniform
bound: reduce prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion at two fixed good
primes of different residue characteristic. The residue degrees are
at most four. The two bounds together control every primary component.
Choose L to kill that torsion as well as the fixed cusp constants.

At a simple zero, restriction and the two quadratic projections have
composite [2], so comparison with the rational twist's free line costs
a fixed power of 2. Reduction and lambda_M are integral. From (1),
after paying these fixed factors and the fixed value v_2(c_0),
  v(a_M(D)) >= min{M, j(P_D)-C},
  v(a_M(D))<M  implies  j(P_D)<=v(a_M(D))+C.
A visible coefficient therefore detects analytic rank one. Section 1
gives a fixed witness whose coefficient valuation stays bounded as
M increases. All losses are fixed independently of D, ell and M.

Assessment and dependencies
---------------------------
The local weights, dyadic orientation, stabilizer multiplier, good
Hecke recurrence, and weighted coefficient count are consistent and
give (1) with the stated classical inputs. This audit also gives an
explicit uniform stabilizer multiplier.

This construction alone does not give the arithmetic lower bound
j(P_D)>=w(D)+s(D)/2-C used in minimum-depth normalization. That bound
belongs to the assembly argument and uses the even coefficient system
and the ring-class lower bound. The coefficient-operator/trace
interface, the arithmetic graph construction, and their assembly
must still be closed before claiming the restricted 2-converse.

An effective bound for the CM fibers in the five-contact argument
2 November 2026

Result
------
The CM finiteness step in the height proof can be made effective using
published results. It does not require the new pointwise 2-converse,
prime-two Fontaine--Mazur, or the exclusion of Landau--Siegel zeros.

For an integer d>=1 define
  B(d)=d^2 * 3^(21000 d^2) + 1,
  C_CM(d)=2 B(d)+4096(1028+5d+84000d^2).

If a CM elliptic curve E is defined over a number field L of degree
at most d, then its original stable Faltings height satisfies
  h_F(E) <= B(d).                                      (1)
If an abelian surface A/L is L-isogenous to E^2, then
  h_F(A) <= C_CM(d).                                   (2)

These deliberately large bounds replace an unspecified finite maximum
over CM invariants. They do not yet determine the full constant H_S
in the five-contact height inequality.

1. An effective discriminant input
----------------------------------
Let Delta<0 be the discriminant of an imaginary quadratic order and
h(Delta) its class number. Guy Fowler's published Proposition 2.7 gives
  h(Delta)<=d  =>  sqrt(|Delta|)<=2d^2 exp(21000d^2).     (3)

This proposition applies to every such order, including orders in any
possible exceptional quadratic field. Its proof uses the effective
Goldfeld--Gross--Zagier--Oesterle bound and the order class-number formula.
It does not use the exception-dependent bound in Proposition 2.5.

Reference: Guy Fowler, "Some uniform effective results on Andre--Oort
for sums of powers in C^n", Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 180
(2026), 607--641, Proposition 2.7.
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004125101825

For a CM elliptic curve the degree of its j-invariant over Q equals
h(Delta). Since j(E) belongs to L, this degree is at most d, so (3)
applies to its endomorphism order.

2. A direct upper bound for the elliptic Faltings height
-------------------------------------------------------
Use the original Faltings normalization, with archimedean metric
(i/2) integral omega wedge conjugate(omega).

A CM elliptic curve acquires everywhere good reduction over a finite
extension because its j-invariant is integral. Over such an extension,
the finite discriminant term in Silverman's height formula is zero.
For each complex embedding choose a reduced period
  tau=(-b+i sqrt(|Delta|))/(2a),
where (a,b,c) is a reduced positive primitive quadratic form of
discriminant Delta. Write y=Im(tau), q=exp(2 pi i tau). Then
  sqrt(3)/2 <= y <= sqrt(|Delta|)/2.

The contribution at this embedding in the original stable height is
  -log(2 pi) + (pi y)/6 - (1/2)log y
      -2 sum_{n>=1} log|1-q^n|.                        (4)
The stable height is the average of these contributions.
This is the general Silverman formula, not a use of a formula requiring
the initial Weierstrass coefficients to lie in Q(j(E)).

For completeness, the constants in (4) can be bounded directly.
Since pi>3 and sqrt(3)>5/3, we have pi sqrt(3)>5.
Also exp(5)>100, for example from exp(1)>8/3. Thus
  rho=|q| <= exp(-pi sqrt(3)) < 1/100.
For 0<=u<1, -log(1-u)<=u/(1-u), so
  -2 sum log|1-q^n|
    <= 2 sum rho^n/(1-rho^n)
    <= 2 rho/(1-rho)^2
    < 200/9801.
Further,
  -(1/2)log y <= (1/2)log(2/sqrt(3))
               < (1/2)log(6/5) < 1/10.
Dropping the negative term -log(2 pi), the total contribution is less
than pi sqrt(|Delta|)/12 + 1. Averaging gives
  h_F(E) <= pi sqrt(|Delta|)/12 + 1.

Apply (3), pi<6, and exp(1)<3 to obtain (1):
  h_F(E) <= (pi/6)d^2 exp(21000d^2)+1
          <= d^2 3^(21000d^2)+1.

Source for the normalization and the general height formula:
Barquero-Sanchez, Cadwallader, Cannon, Genao and Masri,
"Faltings heights of CM elliptic curves and special Gamma values",
Research in Number Theory 3, 13 (2017), equation (6.2).
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-017-0077-7#Sec6

3. Transfer to the abelian surface
---------------------------------
Let E^2 and A be L-isogenous, with [L:Q]<=d. Additivity gives
  h_F(E^2)<=2B(d).
Gaudron--Remond's isogeny bound, in dimension two and with source E^2,
provides an L-isogeny from E^2 to A of degree at most
  (28^256 d max(h_F(E^2),log d,1)^2)^8192.
The maximum is at most 2B(d). The height inequality for an isogeny
therefore gives
  h_F(A) <= 2B(d)
             +4096(256 log28 + log d + 2 log(2B(d))).    (5)

Put N=21000d^2. Since B(d)<=2d^2 3^N, we have
  log(2B(d)) <= log4 + 2log d + N log3
              <= 2+2d+42000d^2.
Also log28<4 and log d<=d. Substitution into (5) proves (2).

The isogeny bound uses the stable height and does not require the
source E^2 to have good reduction over the original field L. Passing
to a good-reduction extension in section 2 computes the same stable
height and does not enlarge d in the application of the isogeny theorem.

Reference: Gaudron--Remond, "Polarisations et isogenies",
Theorem 1.4 and its definition of kappa.
https://lmbp.uca.fr/~gaudron/art13.pdf

4. Making the Hom-field degree explicit in terms of the cover degree
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Let n be the degree of the fixed finite cover pi:Y -> P^1 in the
five-contact construction. For a nonbranch rational base point, its
fiber splitting field K_0 has degree d_0<=n!.

Every conjugate of the chosen surface is defined over K_0, so there
are at most d_0 conjugates. A geometric Hom group between two abelian
surfaces has Z-rank at most 16: its action on integral homology embeds
it in Hom_Z(Z^4,Z^4). Consequently the direct sum over all ordered
pairs has rank r<=16d_0^2.

The finite Galois image on this lattice injects into GL_r(F_3).
Here is the elementary reason. A nonidentity finite-order integral
matrix congruent to I modulo 3 would have a nonidentity prime-order
power H=I+3^s A, with s>=1 and A not zero modulo 3. If its prime order
q differs from 3, expanding H^q modulo 3^(s+1) is a contradiction.
If q=3, expanding modulo 3^(s+2) is again a contradiction. Thus the
principal level-three subgroup has no nontrivial torsion.

The Hom-field L used in the source is the fixed field of the kernel
of this action inside G_{K_0}. It follows that
  [L:Q] <= d_0 * 3^(r^2)
          <= n! * 3^(256(n!)^4).                        (6)
Normality and the unramified-outside-T property are as in the source's
Hom-field construction; this calculation supplies its numerical degree
bound. Let d_n denote the final integer in (6).

In the source's CM case, End^0(A)=M_2(k). All geometric endomorphisms
are defined over L. Taking the image of a multiple of a rank-one
idempotent produces E/L, and the matrix units give A~_L E^2.
Thus (2) applies with d=d_n.

5. Consequence for the five-contact estimate
--------------------------------------------
The subsequent explicit-height-cover.txt gives the numerical bound
  n0=16*9318960^4=120667349406998676479016960000
for the cover degree. It may replace n in (6), hence also in d_n and
the CM bounds below. The base-height comparison and non-CM constants
are still not calculated.

Suppose the base-to-moduli comparison has been made explicit as
  h(s) <= C_base max(1,h_F(A)).
Then the CM fibers satisfy
  h(s) <= C_base C_CM(d_n).
Since the contact radical M_S(s)>=1, this contribution is absorbed
in the desired h(s)<=H_S M_S(s)^24 by taking
  H_S >= C_base C_CM(d_n).

This removes the particular use of ineffective CM finiteness in
lines 669--680 of the frozen height source. The cover degree now has
the bound stated above. C_base, the full exceptional set and the
non-CM constants are still not calculated. Those computations and the remaining proof audit are
still needed before a numerical A>=H_S can be used in
T=(48A)^13.

1. Effective CM discriminant bound

Fowler, Some uniform effective results on Andre--Oort for sums of
powers in C^n, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 180 (2026),
607--641, Proposition 2.7, printed pages 615--616, states

 h(Delta)<=d  =>  sqrt(|Delta|)<=2*d^2*exp(21000*d^2).

This applies to every imaginary quadratic order. There is no
exceptional-field exclusion in Proposition 2.7. The exclusion
in Proposition 2.5 belongs to a different estimate.

The proof uses the effective fundamental-discriminant estimate
 h(D)^2 >= log(|D|)/42000
from Proposition 2.6 and the order class-number formula. For
Delta=f^2 D with D different from -3,-4, these imply h(D)<=d
and phi(f)<=d. The elementary inequality phi(f)^2>=f/2 then
gives f<=2*d^2. For D=-3,-4, the unit index is at most three,
so f<=18*d^2 and sqrt(|Delta|)<=36*d^2, which is smaller
than the displayed bound. Thus the large constant also handles
the two unit exceptions.

The bound in effective-cm-height-bound.txt agrees with the
published statement and does not rely on an ineffective
Siegel constant or the absence of an exceptional quadratic field.

2. Explicit isogeny bound and normalization

Gaudron--Remond, Polarisations et isogenies, Duke Math. J. 163
(2014), 2057--2108, defines on printed page 2058

 kappa(A)=((14*g)^(64*g^2)*[k:Q]
             *max(h_F(A),log([k:Q]),1)^2)^(1024*g^3).

The same paragraph explicitly uses the original stable Faltings
height. Theorem 1.4 on printed page 2060 supplies isogenies in
both directions of degree at most kappa(A), for varieties defined
over k and isogenous over the specified extension K of k.

Taking K=k=L and A=E^2 gives g=2, hence 64*g^2=256 and
1024*g^3=8192. This is exactly the bound used in
effective-cm-height-bound.txt. Taking A=C1 gives exactly the
logarithmic bound used in explicit-noncm-height-bound.txt.
No change of height normalization or extra good-reduction
extension is required for either application of this theorem.

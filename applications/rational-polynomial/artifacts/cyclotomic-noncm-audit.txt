Scope
-----

in cyclotomic.tex, openai/math commit

It is a source-level mathematical audit with classical cohomological and
Euler-system theorems as inputs. It is not a Lean formalization.
The non-CM restriction suffices for every E_l in the pole predicate:
j(E_l)=35152/9 is not an algebraic integer.

The main external inputs are compact global and local duality, the
cyclotomic mu=0 theorem for abelian fields, Kato's fixed-form zeta
classes and rational divisibility, and the forward analytic-rank-zero
theorem. Kato's relevant results are 8.9-8.12, 9.7, 12.5(3), and
12.8.2. The p!=2 hypothesis of 12.5(4) is not imported into 12.5(3),
nor is the integral assertion of (4) used:
https://www.numdam.org/article/AST_2004__295__117_0.pdf

This audit does not cover the separate CM replacement for rational
divisibility in the general pointwise paper.

1. Two coefficient rings and their intersection
-----------------------------------------------
Let Lambda=Z_2[[t]], O=completion of Lambda_(2), and kappa=F_2((t)).
Let G be a finite elementary abelian 2-group.

There is no evaluation at t=0 on all of O. Evaluation is used only after
characterwise membership in Lambda[1/2] has been proved.

Suppose u is in O[1/2][G], every character value u_x is in Lambda[1/2],
and 2^C u is in O[G]. Fourier inversion initially puts each group-basis
coefficient in Lambda[1/2], possibly with a denominator involving |G|.
But O intersect Lambda[1/2]=Lambda: a power series with a bounded
2-denominator has O-valuation equal to the minimum coefficient valuation.
Applying the intersection to 2^C u gives

                         2^C u in Lambda[G]

with the original C, without retaining the Fourier denominator.

For z in Z_2[G], its values on the Boolean character cube have the form
a_empty+sum_(I nonempty)2^|I| a_I x_I. The jth binary digit is the
coefficient of Y^(2^j) in (1+Y)^z modulo 2. A contribution using x_I
costs at least 2^|I| in Y-degree, hence the resulting Boolean polynomial
has degree at most 2^j. Consequently r congruences modulo 2^M require
Boolean equations of total degree at most r(2^M-1). If b is larger,
their common-zero indicator has even sum over F_2^b. Since zero is a
solution, a nonzero solution exists. This proves cy:binary.

2. The positive complex
-----------------------
Use the fiber of global cochains mapping to ordinary real cochains,
with no finite-place Selmer condition. All family characters have
the same real sign.

At finite Artin coefficient quotients, continuous cochains are flat
coefficient modules, obtained from finite partitions. The global-to-real
restriction is an isomorphism in sufficiently high degrees. Residual
cohomology is therefore bounded and finite. A composition series
extends the amplitude bound to finite coefficients. Cancel invertible
disks in a free resolution; the minimal model has precisely the residual
cohomology ranks. Nilpotence lifts the cancellations, and compatible
basis changes give a finite free model over Lambda[G]. The same
cochain construction gives derived coefficient change. No rank bound
uniform in the growing support is asserted at this step.

The invariant real line in the elliptic representation gives a connecting
class a. There are no rational global invariants, so this is injective
on its rational line. Its lattice index is fixed by E and the real sign.
Real H^1 is killed by 2; hence twice any ordinary global class has a
positive lift. Different lifts change its wedge with a by zero.

3. Exact rank two after localization at (2)
------------------------------------------
First take a trivial residual constituent. In the real cyclotomic layer
K_n of degree d_n=2^n:
* the number s_n of places over a fixed finite support is bounded;
* the 2-rank c_n of its S-class group is bounded, by cyclotomic mu=0
  and the Iwasawa structure/control theorem;
* unit signatures surject onto all d_n real signs.

For the last assertion, if zeta has order 2^(n+2), the unit
1+zeta+zeta^(-1) has norm -1. Indeed the recursively defined minimal
polynomial f_(n+1)(X)=f_n(X^2-2), f_1=X^2-2, has f_n(-1)=-1.
The signature vector thus has odd augmentation. In the group algebra
of the cyclic 2-group over F_2 it is a unit, so its translates span
all signatures. For n=0 use -1.

Kummer theory gives dim H^1=d_n+s_n+c_n. Its real restriction is
surjective. The kernel in H^2 has dimension c_n+s_n-1, using the
single Brauer sum relation and the finite place over 2. Thus the
positive fiber has H^1 dimension d_n+O(1) and bounded cohomology in
other degrees. Derived specialization of a perfect F_2[[t]] complex
modulo t^(2^n) makes rank r contribute r*2^n+O(1). After inverting t,
one trivial constituent therefore contributes exactly one dimension
in degree one.

Extensions preserve this concentration. For E[2] there are two trivial
constituents. A minimal model over the local ring O[G] consequently
has just O[G]^2 in degree one. This is exact freeness, with no new
lattice index and no dependence on the support size.

4. Fixed-form classes before taking characters
----------------------------------------------
Kato's auxiliary traces land in the lattice of the one fixed modular
form of E. Projection to T_2E costs a fixed power of 2. It does not
require projecting separately to integral lattices of varying twists.

At cyclotomic level m*2^n, push the induced coefficient module by

 [sigma] |-> chi_h0(sigma)[sigma|Gamma_n][psi(sigma)].

This is an integral module map. There is neither division by |G| nor
an assertion that arbitrary cohomology classes lift under specialization.
Kato's norm relations give an actual inverse-system class; the prime 2
is already omitted, so passing up its tower introduces no new Euler
factor.

The smoothing factors in weight two are
(c^2-c*sigma_c)(d^2-d*sigma_d). Choose c,d trivial on all tame characters
and c,d=1+2^e modulo 2^(e+1), for a fixed sufficiently large e.
Their cyclotomic exponents have valuation e-2. Modulo the maximal ideal
of O[G], 1-(1+t)^kappa(c) is nonzero in F_2((t)). Each smoothing factor
is therefore a unit in O[G]. Its central value has valuation e.
The integers vary with the tame modulus; these two valuation properties
do not. This gives a uniform integral bound after unsmoothing at (2).

The central reciprocity formula for this actual fixed-form class z is

 v(exp_2^* z_h(0))
   = v(L_*(h)) + sum_(q unused) v(#E^h(F_q)) + O_E(1),       (1)

with zero dual exponential when the central L-value is zero.
The character sum is unnormalized. Its odd quadratic Gauss sum gives
sqrt(|h|), a 2-adic unit, and the period line is that of the fixed form.
At an unused good prime the Euler factor is #E^h(F_q)/q. At a ramified
good prime the L-factor is 1. These account for all varying factors.
Fixed local twists at 2N have only finitely many possibilities.

5. Rational divisibility and local corrections
----------------------------------------------
Primary-source scope check (3 November 2026): see
restricted-converse-primary-input-checks.txt. Kato 12.5(3) permits
coefficient prime two; 12.5(4) does not. This confirms the cited
rational statement's range, not the separate uniform integral bound.

Work now characterwise over A=Lambda[1/2]. Kato 12.5(3) gives the
rational height-one divisibility, with its local H^2 correction at 2.
For non-CM E, the open-image input is an open subgroup of SL_2,
as in 12.8.2. Full residual image and the stronger integral theorem
12.5(4) are not required.

For an odd place, the localization cone from j_* cohomology to full
global cohomology is the degree-(1,2) complex

 W_q tensor A --(1-gamma_q*Fr)--> W_q tensor A,
                       W_q=H^1(I_q,V_h).

The cyclotomic exponent at an odd q is nonzero. Its determinant D_q
is nonzero, so the map is injective even after localization at a
height-one prime dividing D_q. Localization is not reduction.
The cone has H^1=0 and H^2 length v(D_q). Thus the increase in global
H^2 length is at most that local length; the localization sequence
does not need a vanishing H^3 hypothesis.

At an unused good prime,
 D_q=P_q(chi_h(q)gamma_q), P_q(Z)=1-a_q Z/q+Z^2/q.
At an active good prime inertia is -1 rationally, W_q=0, and D_q=1.

At bad primes W_q must not be replaced indiscriminately by V_h^I(-1).
For split multiplicative reduction it is Q_2(-1), giving 1-gamma_q/q,
whose central value is nonzero. Generally local duality and the
finiteness of local elliptic torsion give H^2(Q_q,V_h)=0; hence D_q(0)
is nonzero. The finite local twisting list therefore admits fixed
correction factors with nonzero central values.

Compare the fixed-form zeta class with a generator of the rational
Kato line only after taking a character. Rank-one cohomology and the
reciprocity laws show that their quotient is a nonzero scalar, a
group-like unit, and exactly the omitted Euler factors. Infinitely
many nonvanishing finite-order cyclotomic tests identify the quotient:
after denominators are cleared, Weierstrass preparation forbids
infinitely many zeros of a nonzero bounded-denominator power series.
The scalar need not have bounded 2-valuation; it is a unit in A.
Uniform integrality is supplied by section 4, not this comparison.

Multiplying by all unused D_q pays for their H^2 length increments.
A fixed multiplier F_2 pays for the local Iwasawa H^2 at 2. It may be
chosen with F_2(0)!=0: derived local control and H^2(Q_2,V_h)=0 exclude
a t-factor. Further fixed bad-prime discrepancies are covered by one
fixed F_E with F_E(0)!=0. The corrected class satisfies, at each
height-one prime of A,

              length H^2 <= length(H^1 / A*z).       (2)

6. Reconciling Euler conventions integrally
-------------------------------------------
If the trace convention reverses Frobenius, both P_q(Z) and
Z^2 P_q(Z^(-1)) are units in O[G]. Their difference is
((q-1)/q)(1-Z^2), divisible by 2. Let r_q be the required ratio;
then r_q-1 is divisible by 2.

For the universal inertia element g_q, use

               1 + ((r_q-1)/2)(1+g_q).

This is a unit in O[G]. At an active character it equals 1; at an
unused character it equals r_q. Its unused central value is 1 because
Z=+1 or -1 there. Hence multiplying these corrections introduces no
loss per prime, and does not change (1). One must perform this check
before character evaluation; a separate factor 1/2 for each prime
would destroy the desired uniformity.

7. The determinant coordinate
-----------------------------
In the inverse determinant of the positive complex, define u by the
wedge of a and a positive lift of 2z. The real line introduces no
height-one torsion over A. Inequality (2) therefore makes u_x regular
at every height-one prime other than (2). Normality gives

                          u_x in Lambda[1/2].

The exact free rank-two model over O[G] and the fixed integral
construction give 2^Cint u in O[G], with Cint independent of all new
primes, the base discriminant and the cube dimension. Section 1 then
gives 2^Cint u in Lambda[G]. The choice of determinant basis changes
only an integral unit.

8. Central specialization at a corank-zero constituent
------------------------------------------------------
Corank zero already implies Mordell--Weil rank zero and finite
2-primary Sha. It does not yet assume central L-value nonvanishing.

For q!=2, integral Kummer cohomology equals full H^1(Q_q,T_h). The
quotient in the inverse-limit Kummer sequence is the Tate module of
the local Weil--Chatelet group, and is zero by local duality with
the finite 2-completion of E^h(Q_q).
At 2 the quotient by Kummer cohomology is a free rank-one lattice L2.
Its dual exponential has bounded lattice index for the finite list
of local twists, by the formal logarithm and local duality.

Rational Poitou--Tate, using corank zero, identifies ordinary global
H^1 with L2 tensor Q_2 and gives rational H^2=0. Adding the real line
gives H^1 of dimension two in the positive complex. Its central and
generic ranks agree, so the same derived determinant specializes.

Let i be the length of L2 modulo the image of the global free line.
Integral Poitou--Tate gives, up to fixed real and global torsion terms,

 length H^2_global
    = s(h)-i+sum_(q in S) length E^h(Q_q)[2^infinity].        (3)

Global torsion is bounded by its injection into the local torsion at 2.
The real terms are killed by 2 with fixed dimension. Terms at 2N are
bounded by the fixed local twisting classes.

The coordinate of z in the global free line has valuation
v(exp^* z)-i+O(1). Degree-two torsion subtracts its length in the inverse
determinant coordinate. Inserting (3) cancels i exactly and leaves

 v(u_h(0)) = v(exp^* z)-s(h)
             -sum_(q in S)length E^h(Q_q)[2^infinity]+O_E(1).

At an active good q, inertia invariants of the divisible torsion are
exactly E[2]; Frobenius invariants have length t_q. At an unused good
q, reduction identifies local torsion with E^h(F_q)[2^infinity].
Thus (1) cancels every unused contribution and yields

            |v(u_h(0))-[v(L_*(h))-2w(h)-s(h)]|<=Cloc.       (4)

The primes of the base h0 are active, so they are included in the
subtraction 2w(h), not absorbed into a possibly growing error.

If L_*(h)=0, its dual exponential vanishes. The rational global class
then satisfies the finite condition at 2, and at every other place
automatically. The rational Selmer group is zero. Its positive lift
is a multiple of the real line, and its wedge is zero. Consequently
u_h(0)=0. Conversely, (4) makes u_h(0) nonzero when L_*(h) is nonzero.

9. Both conclusions
-------------------
For a single nonzero central value, the forward theorem gives corank
zero. Uniform integrality and (4) imply

                   v(L_*(h)) >= 2w(h)+s(h)-Cint-Cloc.

For the missing vertex assume corank zero at h0 and nonzero central
values with D(h_x)=v(L_*(h_x))-2w(h_x)-s(h_x)<=B at every x!=0.
Their determinant values have valuations at most B+Cloc.

Choose M with M-Cint>B+Cloc. Apply the binary congruence lemma to the
constant coefficient of 2^Cint u in Z_2[G]. For a sufficiently large
cube it supplies x!=0 with

                    v(u_x(0)-u_0(0))>=M-Cint.

The ultrametric inequality forces u_0(0)!=0 and
v(u_0(0))<=B+Cloc. The base corank-zero specialization then gives
L_*(h0)!=0 and D(h0)<=B+2Cloc.

No bound in this argument grows with the cube dimension or the number
of prime factors of h0 or of the new parameters. Prime sizes and the
dimension needed for a selected precision may grow. The conclusion is
the non-CM cyclotomic input needed for the restricted pointwise route;
the ring-class final transfer and the remaining explicit universal-
polynomial construction are separate steps.
